Aristotle with a Bust of Homer

Rembrandt van Rijn · 1653 · Oil on canvas · The Met

Free to use

Don Antonio Ruffo, a Sicilian nobleman, ordered a painting from Rembrandt without naming a subject. Ten years later Rembrandt painted two more pictures for him, a Homer and an Alexander the Great that is now lost. In 1815 it went to Sir Abraham Hume, who lent it to an exhibition in London, and his heirs later sold it to Rodolphe Kann in Paris. The Metropolitan Museum paid 2.3 million dollars for it in 1961, at that time the highest price ever paid for a painting at public or private sale.
