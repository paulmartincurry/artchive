What though the field be lost?
All is not lost; the unconquerable Will,
And study of revenge, immortal hate,
And courage never to submit or yield:

Paradise Lost — What Though the Field Be Lost (Book I)

John Milton · 1667

During its composition Milton was often ill with gout, and he was grieving for his second wife, Katherine Woodcock, who died in 1658, and for their infant daughter. He wrote it in blank verse, unrhymed iambic pentameter, which was little used outside drama in the seventeenth century until this poem. From 1668 some printings added Arguments, brief summaries, at the start of each book.
