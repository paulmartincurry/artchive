The Picture of Dorian Gray (Ch. XIII)

Oscar Wilde · 1890

The story was commissioned over dinner at the Langham Hotel in London on 30 August 1889, when an editor from the American Lippincott's Monthly Magazine asked both Wilde and Arthur Conan Doyle for novellas. Wilde delivered his manuscript seven months later. The editor cut about five hundred words without telling him, and the story filled the first hundred pages of the July 1890 issue. Wilde said the idea came to him in December 1887, after he sat for his portrait to a Canadian painter. The typescript is now at UCLA.
