There is nothing in the world more soft and weak than water, and yet for attacking things that are firm and strong there is nothing that can take precedence of it;--for there is nothing (so effectual) for which it can be changed.

Tao Te Ching — There is nothing in the world more soft and weak than water (Ch. 78)

Laozi (Lao Tzu) · 4th–3rd century BC

Translated by James Legge

Wang Bi, a minor official of the state of Cao Wei, wrote a commentary on this book before he died in an epidemic in 249, at the age of 23. The text that circulated with his commentary was widely considered the best copy of the book until the Han-era silk manuscripts were found at Mawangdui in 1973.
