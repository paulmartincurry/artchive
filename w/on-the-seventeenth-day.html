On the Seventeenth Day

Wang Xizhi · 13th-century rubbing of a series of 4th-century texts · Album of thirty leaves, ink on paper · 24.4 × 12.7 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

This album of thirty leaves is a thirteenth-century rubbing of a series of texts Wang Xizhi wrote in the fourth century. Apart from the Orchid Pavilion Preface, his work was mostly letters and copies of existing texts. He studied calligraphy as a boy under Wei Shuo, known as Lady Wei. He held government posts from 324 to 354, and in 347 was made General of the Right Army. Emperor Taizong of Tang later made his style the standard for Chinese calligraphy.
