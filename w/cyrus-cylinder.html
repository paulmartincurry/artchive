Cyrus Cylinder

Maker unknown · 539–538 BCE · Baked clay · British Museum

Free to use with credit. Photo: Prioryman, CC BY-SA 3.0

The cylinder was built up in layers of clay around a cone-shaped core, then given a fine slip to take the writing, and buried in the foundations of the main temple of Babylon. Hormuzd Rassam, digging for the British Museum, found it there in March 1879, already broken. A smaller piece was bought from an antiquities dealer by J. B. Nies of Yale University and was not recognised as part of it until 1970. The two fragments were reunited in 1972 and together carry 45 lines of text.
