The Artchive
Unto the Dwelling whose tenants are (ever) bereft of the daylight,
Where for their food is the dust, and the mud is their sustenance: bird-like
Wear they a garment of feathers: and, sitting (there) in the darkness,
Never the light will they see.

The Epic of Gilgamesh — The Dwelling of Darkness (Tablet VII)

Anonymous (Standard Babylonian) · c. 1200 BCE

Translated by R. Campbell Thompson, 1928

The Standard Babylonian version of the epic consists of twelve tablets and was edited by Sîn-lēqi-unninni, who is thought to have lived sometime between 1300 BCE and 1000 BCE. Hormuzd Rassam discovered it in the library of Ashurbanipal in Nineveh in 1853. In the seventh tablet Enkidu dreams that he is taken captive to the Netherworld. For 12 days his condition worsens, and he dies.
