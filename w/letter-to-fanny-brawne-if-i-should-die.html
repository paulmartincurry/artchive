“If I should die,” said I to myself, “I have left no immortal work behind me—nothing to make my friends proud of my memory—but I have lov’d the principle of beauty in all things, and if I had had time I would have made myself remember’d.”

Letter to Fanny Brawne (February 1820): If I should die

John Keats · February 1820

Keats wrote this letter to Fanny Brawne in February 1820, aware that he was dying. He had suffered two lung haemorrhages in the first few days of that month, the first on 3 February. He was twenty-four. In September 1820 he left for Rome, knowing he would probably never see her again.
