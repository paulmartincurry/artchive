Parthenon Frieze (West Frieze, Block II)

Maker unknown · c. 443–437 BC · Pentelic marble · H. 1 m · British Museum, London

Free to use

The frieze ran for 160 metres around the inner building of the Parthenon and was one metre high; about 128 metres survive. It was carved between about 443 and 437 BC, most likely under the direction of Phidias, from marble quarried on Mount Pentelicus and carried 19 kilometres to the Acropolis. A guide strip left in the stone is the best evidence that the blocks were carved after they were set on the wall. It was painted and fitted with metal details, and no colour survives. This is Block II of the west side.
