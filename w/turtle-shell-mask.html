Turtle-Shell Mask (Krar)

Maker unknown · Mid to late 19th century · Turtle shell, wood, cassowary feathers, fiber, resin, shell, paint · 54.6 × 63.5 × 57.8 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

Torres Strait Islanders are the only people in the world known to make masks of turtle shell, called krar, the word for turtle shell, in the Western Islands. This one was made on Mabuiag Island in the mid to late nineteenth century, with wood, cassowary feathers, fibre, resin, shell and paint, and is 54.6 centimetres high. In 1898 and 1899 the anthropologist Alfred Cort Haddon collected about 2,000 Torres Strait objects, fearing missionaries would destroy them.
