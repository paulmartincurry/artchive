Horses, night, and the desert know me;
so do swords, spears, the paper and pen.

Qasida (The Steed, the Night, the Desert)

Al-Mutanabbi · ca. 955 CE

Translated by Raymond Farrin · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Al-Mutanabbi began writing poetry at nine. His name means the Would-be Prophet: as a young man he claimed to be one, led a revolt in Syria and spent two years in prison. From 948 he spent nine years at the court of Sayf al-Dawla in Aleppo, fought beside the prince against the Byzantines, and composed 300 folios of poetry for him. He was killed resisting thieves on the road to Baghdad.
