The Horse in Motion

Eadweard Muybridge · 1878 · Photograph

Free to use

Leland Stanford, a former Governor of California who bred racehorses, paid for these photographs because he believed painters drew running horses wrongly. Eadweard Muybridge set a battery of 12 cameras along Stanford's track at Palo Alto, each shutter tripped by a wire as the horse passed, and made the first run in front of the press on 15 June 1878. The cards measured 22 by 14 centimetres and sold for $1.50 apiece. When Stanford published a book on the project in 1882, Muybridge was not credited, and his lawsuit over it was dismissed.
