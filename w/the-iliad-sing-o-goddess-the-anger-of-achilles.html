The Iliad — Sing, O goddess, the anger of Achilles (opening)

Homer · c. 8th century BCE

Translated by Samuel Butler

The poem opens by calling on the Muse. Most scholars place its making in the late eighth century BC, and traces of it in art and other writing show it existed by 630 BC. Its setting is some four hundred years older than that. It covers a few weeks near the end of a ten-year siege, and neither the fall of Troy nor the wooden horse appears in it. In the 1920s Milman Parry showed that its repeated epithets and phrases were tools of oral composition, sized to fill part of a hexameter line.
