Come live with me and be my Love,
And we will all the pleasures prove
That hills and valleys, dales and fields
Or woods or steepy mountain yields.

The Passionate Shepherd to His Love

Christopher Marlowe · 1599

The poem is dated 1599, six years after Marlowe's death, and Walter Raleigh answered it in 1600 with a reply in the nymph's voice. In 1587 Cambridge had hesitated to give Marlowe his master's degree over a rumour that he meant to go to the English seminary at Rheims; the Privy Council intervened and it was awarded on schedule. About 1846 William Sterndale Bennett set the words as a four-part madrigal.
