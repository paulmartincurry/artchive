Paulownias and Chrysanthemums

Sakai Hōitsu · early 1800s · Two-panel folding screen; ink and color on gilded paper · Image: 152.7 × 154.9 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

Sakai Hōitsu was the son of the lord of Himeji Castle. He became a Buddhist priest in 1797 and spent his last 21 years in seclusion, studying and copying the work of Ogata Kōrin. Here he used tarashikomi, dripping ink and colour onto the gilded paper and letting it pool, for the bark and the leaves. He often painted two-panel screens like this one for city clients with small rooms.
