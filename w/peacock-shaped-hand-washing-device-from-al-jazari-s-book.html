Peacock-shaped Hand Washing Device, from al-Jazari's Book of Ingenious Devices

Maker unknown · 1315 · opaque watercolor and gold on paper · Cleveland Museum of Art

Al-Jazari, an engineer from upper Mesopotamia, wrote his book of mechanical devices in 1206 while in the service of a king. This leaf comes from a copy made in Damascus in 1315, one of 15 manuscript copies that survive. In the design the peacock's mouth is a spigot that pours water, and a figure comes out of a small door to offer soap. It is not known for certain that the device was ever built.
