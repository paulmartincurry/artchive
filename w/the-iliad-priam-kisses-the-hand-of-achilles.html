Fear, O Achilles, the wrath of heaven; think on your own father and have compassion upon me, who am the more pitiable, for I have steeled myself as no man yet has ever steeled himself before me, and have raised to my lips the hand of him who slew my son.”

The Iliad — Priam Kisses the Hand of Achilles (Book XXIV)

Homer · c. 8th century BCE

Translated by Samuel Butler

In the last book of the Iliad, Zeus sends Hermes to escort Priam, king of Troy, into the Greek camp. Achilles gives back Hector's body and promises a truce for the funeral. Priam is said to have fathered fifty sons; he was killed at around eighty, in the sack of the city, by Achilles' son Neoptolemus.
