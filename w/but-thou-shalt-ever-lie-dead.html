But thou shalt ever lie dead, nor shall there be any remembrance of thee then or thereafter, for thou hast not of the roses of Pieria; but thou shalt wander obscure even in the house of Hades, flitting among the shadowy dead.

But Thou Shalt Ever Lie Dead (fragment 68)

Sappho of Lesbos · ca. 600 BCE

Translated by Henry Thornton Wharton

Stobaeus, writing about 500 AD, quoted these lines as addressed to an uneducated woman. Plutarch quoted them too, in one place as written to a certain rich lady. Sappho herself came from a wealthy family on Lesbos.
