The Pantheon, Rome

Architect unknown; built under Hadrian · ca. 126 · Concrete

Free to use with credit. Photo: NikonZ7II, CC BY-SA 4.0

The inscription on the front names Marcus Agrippa, whose earlier temple on the site burnt down. Hadrian finished the present building, probably dedicated around AD 126, and had Agrippa's old inscription put on it, so the date of construction is uncertain. The height to the oculus and the diameter of the interior are the same, 142 feet, and no larger dome of unreinforced concrete has been built since. It became a church in 609. In 663 the emperor Constans II stripped the bronze tiles from the roof, which stayed uncovered for nearly a century.
