Diary of a Madman (final entry)

Nikolai Gogol · 1835

Translated by Richard Pevear and Larissa Volokhonsky, 1998 · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

This is the only work Gogol wrote in the first person, and it takes the form of dated diary entries. It came out in 1835, when he was twenty-five or twenty-six, the same year as his novel Taras Bulba. Its hero is a minor civil servant whose job is mending pens. In the second half of the nineteenth century medical specialists wrote articles citing the story as an accurate outline of how paranoid delusion progresses.
