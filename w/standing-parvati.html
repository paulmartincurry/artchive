Standing Parvati

Maker unknown · c. first quarter of the 10th century · Copper alloy · 69.5 × 26 × 17 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

Chola bronzes were cast by the lost-wax method. The figure was modelled in beeswax kneaded with camphor and a little oil, coated in clay from termite hills and fired, so that the wax ran out and molten metal could be poured in. The mould was then broken off, which is why no two are the same. This Parvati, 69.5 centimetres high, came to the Met in 1956 as a bequest of Cora Timken Burnett.
