“Then I tried to find some way of embracing my poor mother’s ghost. Thrice I sprang towards her and tried to clasp her in my arms, but each time she flitted from my embrace as it were a dream or phantom, and being touched to the quick I said to her, ‘Mother, why do you not stay still when I would embrace you?

The Odyssey — His Mother's Ghost (Book XI)

Homer · c. 8th century BCE

Translated by Samuel Butler

Anticlea, the mother of Odysseus, was a daughter of Autolycus and a granddaughter of the god Hermes. In the poem she tells her son that she died of grief, longing for him while he was at war. In some other accounts she killed herself on hearing a false report about him.
