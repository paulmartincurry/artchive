We hate poetry that has a palpable design upon us, and, if we do not agree, seems to put its hand into its breeches pocket. Poetry should be great and unobtrusive, a thing which enters into one's soul, and does not startle it or amaze it with itself—but with its subject.

Letter to John Hamilton Reynolds (3 February 1818)

John Keats · February 3, 1818

John Hamilton Reynolds was a close friend and correspondent of Keats. He had taken a junior clerkship in an insurance office, and from 1818 to 1820 he worked in Essex Street for a solicitor, Francis Fladgate. The edition dates this letter Hampstead, Tuesday, February 3, 1818.
