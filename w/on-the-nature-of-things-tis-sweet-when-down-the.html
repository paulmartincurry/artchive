'Tis sweet, when, down the mighty main, the winds
Roll up its waste of waters, from the land
To watch another's labouring anguish far,

On the Nature of Things — 'Tis Sweet, When Down the Mighty Main (Book II, proem)

Lucretius · c. 55 BCE

Translated by William Ellery Leonard

These lines open the second of the poem's six books. The poem was almost lost during the Middle Ages, and was rediscovered in 1417 in a monastery in Germany by Poggio Bracciolini. It is possible that the very first copies were made by the slaves of Atticus, an Epicurean publisher who was a close friend of Cicero.
