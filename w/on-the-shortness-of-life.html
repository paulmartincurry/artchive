we do not receive a short life, but we make it a short one, and we are not poor in days, but wasteful of them.

On the Shortness of Life (opening)

Seneca the Younger · c. 49 CE

Translated by Aubrey Stewart

The essay is addressed to a man called Paulinus, the official in charge of Rome's grain supply and quite plausibly the father of Seneca's wife. It closes by urging him to give up public work. The usual date is around 49 AD, the year Seneca was allowed back to Rome after eight years of exile on Corsica, though one scholar places it in 55 and reads it as an excuse for Paulinus to retire early.
