Savoyard Helmet (Todenkopf)

Maker unknown · c. 1600–20 · Steel, blackened · H. 30.5 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

Helmets of this kind were built to stop shot from the wheel-lock guns of the early seventeenth century. Their second job was to frighten: the face is cut to suggest a human face or a skull, and in Germany they were called Todenkopf, death's head. The name Savoyard recalls the cavalry of Charles Emanuel I, Duke of Savoy. This one is blackened steel, 30.5 centimetres high, and was worn with a three-quarter cuirassier armour.
