The example of the syllogism that he had learned in Kiseveter’s logic — Caius is a man, men are mortal, therefore Caius is mortal — had seemed to him all his life correct only as regards Caius, but not at all as regards himself. … And now here it is!

The Death of Ivan Ilyitch — Caius Is Mortal (Ch. VI)

Leo Tolstoy · 1886

Translated by Constance Garnett

Tolstoy first published this novella in 1886, eight years after Anna Karenina and after the moral crisis he went through in the 1870s, which he set out in his Confession. He was in his late fifties. Ilyich is a patronymic; the judge's surname is Golovin.
