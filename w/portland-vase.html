Portland Vase

Maker unknown · c. AD 1–25 · Cameo glass · H. 24 cm; diam. 17.7 cm · British Museum, London

Free to use with credit. Photo: Marie-Lan Nguyen, CC BY 2.5

The vase is violet-blue glass under a continuous layer of white glass, cut back as a cameo. It is first recorded in a letter of 1601, belonged to the Barberini for some two hundred years, and was bought by Sir William Hamilton between 1778 and 1780. The British Museum has kept it since 1810 and bought it from the Duke of Portland in 1945. On 7 February 1845 William Lloyd, who had been drinking all week, threw a sculpture onto its case and shattered it. Josiah Wedgwood spent four years copying it in jasperware.
