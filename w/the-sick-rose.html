O rose, thou art sick!
The invisible worm,
That flies in the night,
In the howling storm,

Has found out thy bed
Of crimson joy,
And his dark secret love
Does thy life destroy.

The Sick Rose

William Blake · 1794

The Sick Rose is the thirty-ninth plate of Songs of Innocence and of Experience, with a border and a picture drawn around the words. Blake wrote it some time after 1789. In a draft the worm is called her, not his. Catherine Boucher, his wife, worked on his books as a printmaker and colourist.
