And he unrolled his feathers
And rowed him softer home

Than oars divide the ocean,
Too silver for a seam,
Or butterflies, off banks of noon,
Leap, splashless, as they swim.

A Bird came down the Walk

Emily Dickinson · published 1891

Three copies of this poem in Dickinson's hand differ in the punctuation of its middle lines. One went to her literary mentor, Thomas Wentworth Higginson; one she kept; and a fair copy was written into one of her manuscript booklets. The fair copy is the version usually printed. The poem first appeared in 1891, in the second collection of her work.
