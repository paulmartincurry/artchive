In the future days, which we seek to make secure, we look forward to a world founded upon four essential human freedoms.

The first is freedom of speech and expression—everywhere in the world.

Annual Message to Congress — The Four Freedoms

Franklin D. Roosevelt · 1941

During 1940, after a press conference in which he discussed long-range peace objectives, Roosevelt started collecting ideas for a speech about rights and freedoms. He gave it as his State of the Union Address in 1941, when Western Europe lay under Nazi domination. Norman Rockwell later painted the four freedoms as scenes of everyday American life, and after the war they were embedded in the Charter of the United Nations.
