Haʻalelea's Feather Cape

Maker unknown · Late 18th – Early 19th Century · ʻŌʻō and ʻiʻiwi feathers · Bishop Museum, Honolulu

Free to use

A Hawaiian feather cape starts as a netting of olonā, a plant of the nettle family. Small bundles of feathers were tied into it close together until the surface was covered. The yellow came from the ʻōʻō, a mostly black bird that was caught alive, relieved of its few yellow feathers and released. The red ʻiʻiwi were more plentiful and were killed and skinned. The size of a cape showed the rank of its wearer.
