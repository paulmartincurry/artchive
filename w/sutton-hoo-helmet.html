Sutton Hoo Helmet

Maker unknown · ca. 620–625 · Iron, bronze, tin, gold, silver, garnet group · British Museum

Free to use with credit. Photo: Geni, CC BY-SA 4.0

The helmet was wrapped in cloths and laid to the left of the dead man's head in a ship that had been hauled uphill from the river and buried under a mound. Long afterwards the chamber roof gave way, and the excavators of 1939 found the helmet as hundreds of rusted fragments. It was pieced together in 1945–46 and then taken apart and rebuilt in its present form in 1970–71. The finds belonged by law to Edith Pretty, who owned the land, and she gave them to the British Museum. Only six Anglo-Saxon helmets are known.
