Out flew the web and floated wide;
The mirror crack'd from side to side;

The Lady of Shalott (Part III)

Alfred, Lord Tennyson · 1832 (Revised 1842)

Written in May 1832 and published that year in twenty stanzas, the poem was cut to nineteen for the version of 1842. Tennyson took the story from a thirteenth-century Italian tale. In an 1857 illustrated edition William Holman Hunt drew the Lady tangled in the threads of her tapestry, and Tennyson took him to task because nothing of the kind happens in the poem. Hunt kept returning to the picture and painted a large version shortly before his death, with assistants, as he was too frail to finish it alone.
