Study of a Tulip (Perregoen Machieu)

Pieter Holsteyn II · c. 1645 · Point of brush and transparent and opaque watercolors with traces of graphite and gum glazing on antique laid paper · Sheet: 36 × 27.3 cm (14 3/16 × 10 3/4 in.) · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

The sheet comes from a tulip book, a set of pictures a grower used to sell bulbs during the Dutch tulip mania. The streaks on the petals were caused by a virus in the bulb, and they made the flower more valuable. Pieter Holsteyn was the son of a glass painter and worked in Haarlem, Amsterdam, Munster and Enkhuizen, signing with the monogram PH. The drawing was sold at Sotheby's in Amsterdam on 10 May 1994 and reached the Cleveland Museum of Art in 2020.
