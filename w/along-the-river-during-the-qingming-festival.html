Along the River During the Qingming Festival

Zhang Zeduan · early 12th century · Silk · Palace Museum, Beijing

Free to use

The scroll is 25.5 cm high and 5.25 metres long and is read from right to left as it is unrolled. Along its length are 814 people, 28 boats, 60 animals, 30 buildings and 170 trees; only about twenty of the people are women. The earliest record naming Zhang Zeduan as the painter is an inscription dated 1186. Puyi, the last emperor of China, took the scroll with him when he was forced out of the Forbidden City in 1924, and it was recovered in 1945. In January 2012 it went on show in Tokyo, the first attested time it had left China.
