Burney Relief (Queen of the Night)

Maker unknown · 19th–18th century BCE · Clay · 49.5 × 37 × 4.8 cm · British Museum, London

Free to use with credit. Photo: Gennadii Saus i Segura, CC BY-SA 4.0

The plaque was never excavated and its find-site is unknown. A Syrian dealer had it first and may have acquired it in southern Iraq in 1924. The British Museum declined to buy it in 1935, and it passed to the London dealer Sidney Burney, whose name it took. The museum bought it in 2003 for £1,500,000 and renamed it the Queen of the Night. It is fired clay, 49.5 centimetres high, and its authenticity has been questioned since it first appeared in the 1930s.
