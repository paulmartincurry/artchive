He sang of love when earth was young,
And Love, itself, was in his lays.
But ah, the world, it turned to praise
A jingle in a broken tongue.

The Poet

Paul Laurence Dunbar · 1903

Dunbar told an interviewer he was tired, so tired of dialect, though he is also quoted as saying that his natural speech was dialect. He credited William Dean Howells with his early success but accused him of doing irrevocable harm by praising the dialect verse above the rest, and he was angry that editors refused his more traditional poems.
