Hagia Sophia

Isidore of Miletus and Anthemius of Tralles · 532–537; minarets added after 1453 · Building

Free to use with credit. Photo: Arild Vågen, CC BY-SA 3.0

The emperor Justinian I had this church built between 532 and 537, after the previous one on the site was destroyed in the Nika riots. It was designed by two Greek geometers, Isidore of Miletus and Anthemius of Tralles. It remained the largest church in the world for more than five hundred years. After the Ottoman conquest in 1453 it became a mosque and the minarets were added; it was a museum from 1935 and became a mosque again in 2020.
