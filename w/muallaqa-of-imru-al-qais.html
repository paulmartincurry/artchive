Halt, friends both! Let us weep, recalling a love and a lodging

Muʿallaqa of Imruʾ al-Qais

Imru' al-Qais · Mid-6th Century CE

Translated by A. J. Arberry · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

The poem comes from an Arabia that had no formal writing system, and it was passed on by word of mouth. It is one of the seven Mu'allaqat. Its author was the last king of Kinda, and the stories say his father banished him for his poetry, his drinking and his pursuit of women. After his father was killed he spent the rest of his life trying to win back the kingdom. He fell ill near Ankara on the way home from Constantinople and died there.
