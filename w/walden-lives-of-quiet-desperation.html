The mass of men lead lives of quiet desperation. What is called resignation is confirmed desperation.

Walden — Lives of quiet desperation (Ch. 1)

Henry David Thoreau · 1854

This comes from Economy, the first and longest chapter of Walden. Thoreau lived at the pond for two years, two months and two days, leaving on 6 September 1847, and the book folds that time into a single year. The cabin was about two miles from his family's home in Concord. His mother and sisters often visited with baskets of pies, doughnuts and prepared meals, and his mother took his laundry home to wash.
