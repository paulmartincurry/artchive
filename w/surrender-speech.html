Hear me, my chiefs, my heart is sick and sad. From where the sun now stands, I will fight no more forever.

Surrender Speech

Chief Joseph (Hin-mah-too-yah-lat-kekt) · October 5, 1877

The surrender came on the afternoon of 5 October 1877 in northern Montana Territory, after a five-day siege in freezing weather with no food or blankets. Chief Joseph was thirty-seven, and his band had travelled more than 1,170 miles in over three months, stopping about 40 miles short of the Canadian border. The words are attributed to him as reported by an army lieutenant, Charles Erskine Scott Wood. He surrendered on the understanding that his people could return to Idaho; he died in 1904 on a reservation in Washington.
