Stela of Amenemhat and Hemet

Maker unknown · about 1956–1877 BCE · Limestone and pigment · 31.1 × 41.7 × 6.7 cm · Art Institute of Chicago

Free to use

The stela was first carved in raised and sunken relief, and only later were some of its surfaces painted in black, brown, green, yellow and white. Its hieroglyphs name Amenemhat and his wife Hemet and call on the god Osiris to grant them sustenance in the afterlife. Ralph Huntington Blanchard of Cairo sold it to the Art Institute of Chicago in 1920, with James Henry Breasted acting as agent.
