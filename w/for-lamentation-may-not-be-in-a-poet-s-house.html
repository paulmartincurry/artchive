For lamentation may not be in a poet's house: such things befit not us.'

Death is evil; the Gods have so judged: had it been good, they would die.

For Lamentation May Not Be in a Poet's House (fragments 136 and 137)

Sappho of Lesbos · ca. 600 BCE

Translated by Henry Thornton Wharton

Aristotle quoted the second of these fragments in his Rhetoric. Maximus Tyrius wrote that Sappho blamed her daughter for lamenting, as Socrates blamed Xanthippe for lamenting his death. Stobaeus tells how Solon of Athens heard his nephew sing an ode of Sappho's over wine and asked to be taught it, so that he would not die before he had learned it; it may have been this one.
