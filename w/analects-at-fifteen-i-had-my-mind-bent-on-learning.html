At fifteen, I had my mind bent on learning. At thirty, I stood firm. At forty, I had no doubts. At fifty, I knew the decrees of Heaven. At sixty, my ear was an obedient organ for the reception of truth. At seventy, I could follow what my heart desired, without transgressing what was right.

Analects — At fifteen, I had my mind bent on learning (Book II, 4)

Confucius · c. 5th century BCE

Translated by James Legge

Confucius's father, an elderly commandant of the local garrison, died when the boy was three, and his mother raised him in poverty. He was educated at schools for commoners. In his early twenties he is said to have held various government jobs and to have worked as a bookkeeper and a caretaker of sheep and horses. His sayings were compiled only after his death; the oldest handwritten copies yet found in tombs date from about 50 BC, and one of them was damaged by fire and later by an earthquake.
