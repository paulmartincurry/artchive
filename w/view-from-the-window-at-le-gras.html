View from the Window at Le Gras

Nicéphore Niépce · 1827 · Heliography, pewter, bitumen · Harry Ransom Center, University of Texas at Austin

Free to use

The plate is pewter, 16.2 by 20.2 centimetres, thinly coated with bitumen of Judea, a natural asphalt that hardens in light; the parts that stayed soft were washed off with lavender oil and white petroleum. Niépce exposed it in a camera obscura at a high window of his house in the summer of 1827. Eight hours is the traditional estimate, but a researcher who recreated the process found the exposure must have run for several days. The plate was last shown in 1905 and then dropped out of sight for 47 years, until two historians tracked it down in 1952.
