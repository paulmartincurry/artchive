Leaf from a Latin Bible: Initial P with St. Paul

Circle of William de Brailes · c. 1230–40 · ink, tempera, and gold on vellum · Leaf: 18.5 × 13.2 cm (7 5/16 × 5 3/16 in.) · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

The leaf, 18.5 by 13.2 centimetres, comes from a one-volume Bible small enough to carry and study, the kind made by lay workshops in university towns such as Oxford. The P opens the Epistle to Philemon, and a contorted little figure in a scholar's cap props it up from below. Initials in the margin follow a system used only by Carthusian monks, so the Bible was probably used in a monastery. William de Brailes is one of the few English illuminators known by name; his signature survives on a psalter leaf.
