He piled upon the whale’s white hump the sum of all the general rage and hate felt by his whole race from Adam down; and then, as if his chest had been a mortar, he burst his hot heart’s shell upon it.

Moby-Dick — The sum of all the general rage and hate (Ch. 41, Moby Dick)

Herman Melville · 1851

Harper's refused Melville an advance, and since he already owed the firm almost $700 he borrowed money and paid for the typesetting himself. The London edition was printed in only 500 copies, and fewer than 300 sold in the first four months. Almost 300 unsold American copies were lost when a fire broke out at Harper's in December 1853. In all, the book earned Melville $1,260.
