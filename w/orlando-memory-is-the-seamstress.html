Memory is the seamstress, and a capricious one at that. Memory runs her needle in and out, up and down, hither and thither. We know not what comes next, or what follows after.

Orlando — Memory Is the Seamstress (Ch. 2)

Virginia Woolf · 1928

Woolf noted the idea for Orlando in her diary on 5 October 1927, and the book was out almost exactly a year later. It drew on the family history of Vita Sackville-West, her lover, whose childhood home, Knole, had gone to a male cousin under the inheritance laws. The Hogarth Press, which Woolf and her husband Leonard had founded in 1917, published it on 11 October 1928, when she was forty-six. The story ends at midnight on that same day.
