Two birds, fast bound companions,
Clasp close the self-same tree.
Of these two, the one eats sweet fruit;
The other looks on without eating.

Mundaka Upanishad — Two Birds (3.1.1–3)

Vedic Upanishadic Seers · 1st millennium BCE; English 1921

Translated by Robert Ernest Hume

The image of the two birds is older than this Upanishad: it is found in the hymns of the Rig Veda, I.164. Mundaka means shaved or shorn, and can also refer to the lopped trunk of a tree. The Upanishad has 64 verses in three parts, and Shankara cites it 129 times in his commentary on the Brahmasutra.
