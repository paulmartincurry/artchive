Learn from me, if not by my precepts, at least by my example, how dangerous is the acquirement of knowledge and how much happier that man is who believes his native town to be the world, than he who aspires to become greater than his nature will allow.

Frankenstein — How dangerous is the acquirement of knowledge (Ch. 4)

Mary Shelley · 1818

Victor was a pen name of Percy Shelley's, used in a book of poems he wrote with his sister Elizabeth in 1810, and there is speculation that he was one of her models for the character. At Eton he had experimented with electricity, magnetism and gunpowder, and his rooms at Oxford were full of scientific equipment. When Mary Shelley revised the novel for the 1831 edition she was more critical of Victor's decisions than in the first text.
