The Maxims of Ptahhotep

Vizier Ptahhotep · ca. 2000–1800 BCE

Translated by Miriam Lichtheim · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

The text is attributed to Ptahhotep, a vizier of the Fifth Dynasty king Djedkare Isesi, and presents him as a man of ninety-six writing for the son who would succeed him. Most historians think it was composed later, in the Middle Kingdom. Four copies are known. The only complete one, the Prisse Papyrus, was found at Thebes in 1847 by Émile Prisse d'Avennes and is now in the Bibliothèque Nationale in Paris. Egyptologists count it among the hardest Egyptian literary texts to translate.
