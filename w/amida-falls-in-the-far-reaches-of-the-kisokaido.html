Amida Falls in the Far Reaches of the Kisokaido (A Tour of Waterfalls in Various Provinces)

Katsushika Hokusai · c. 1833 · Color woodblock print; ōban · 38.2 × 25.4 cm · Art Institute of Chicago

Free to use

The round gorge before Amida Falls was considered to resemble the head of Buddha. Hokusai drew the gorge from a bird's-eye view and the rest of the scene from the front. His was the first series of ukiyo-e prints to take falling water as its subject.
