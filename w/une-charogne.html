Ah then, o my beauty, explain to the worms
Who cherish your body so fine,
That I am the keeper for corpses of love
Of the form, and the essence divine!

Une Charogne (A Carcass)

Charles Baudelaire · 1857

Translated by James McGowan, 1993 · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

In 1841 Baudelaire's stepfather put him on a ship for Calcutta, and he turned back after a few weeks stopped on Mauritius and Réunion. He began composing some of the poems of Les Fleurs du mal on his return to Paris. Some appeared in the Revue des deux mondes in 1855, two years before the book. He told his mother that he cut a third from the proofs.
