Senjafuda

Maker unknown · photographed 2006 · Paper

Free to use with credit. Photo: Jnn, Wikimedia Commons

Senjafuda are slips bearing a worshipper's name, posted on the gates and buildings of shrines and temples in Japan. The first were wooden slats hung from temple gates with bamboo nails; they have been made of paper since the Edo period. A single slip measures 58 millimetres wide and 173 tall. They were pasted on with rice paste, and pilgrims used their walking staffs as applicators to reach high places.
