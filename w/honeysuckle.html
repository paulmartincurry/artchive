Honeysuckle

William Morris · early 20th century · Plain weave linen, printed · 87 x 90.5 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

The pattern repeat of Honeysuckle is about 76 by 88 centimetres, which spans almost the full width of the loom; Morris held that large patterns were more restful to the eye than small ones, even in modest rooms. His firm printed textiles by hand with wood blocks rather than by roller, and from 1881 it dyed and printed at Merton Abbey on the River Wandle. This piece was printed in the early twentieth century, after Morris's death in 1896.
