The heart of a woman falls back with the night,
and enters some alien cage in its plight.

The Heart of a Woman

Georgia Douglas Johnson · 1918

This is the title poem of Johnson's first collection. She had trained as a teacher in Atlanta, left teaching in 1902 to study music at the Oberlin Conservatory, and worked as an assistant principal before her family moved to Washington, D.C., in 1910. Her first published poem appeared in 1905 in the journal The Voice of the Negro. Maya Angelou took the title of her 1981 autobiography from this poem.
