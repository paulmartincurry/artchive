Make me a grave where'er you will, in a lowly plain, or a lofty hill;
Make it among earth's humblest graves, but not in a land where men are slaves.

Bury Me in a Free Land

Frances Ellen Watkins Harper · 1858

Harper wrote the poem for The Anti-Slavery Bugle newspaper in 1858, the year she turned thirty-three and the year she refused to leave her seat for the segregated section of a Philadelphia trolley car. She had been born free in Baltimore, was orphaned at three, and at thirteen went to work as a seamstress and nursemaid for a family that owned a bookshop. After John Brown was executed she sent a copy of the poem to his widow.
