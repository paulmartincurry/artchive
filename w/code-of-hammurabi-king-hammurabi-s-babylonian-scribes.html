Code of Hammurabi (prologue)

King Hammurabi's Babylonian Scribes · ca. 1750 BCE

Translated by Martha T. Roth · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

The main copy of this text is cut into a basalt stele 2.25 metres tall, in about 4,130 lines of cuneiform. A fifth of them are the prologue and epilogue, which are in a poetic style; the rest are the laws. It was compiled near the end of Hammurabi's reign, in the Old Babylonian dialect of Akkadian. Six hundred years later the stele was carried off to Susa as plunder, and it was found there in 1901. It is now in the Louvre.
