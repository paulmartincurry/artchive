Go down, Moses,
’Way down in Egypt land,
Tell ole Pharaoh,
Let my people go.

Go Down, Moses

Traditional African American Spiritual · 1862

The earliest written record of the song is as an anthem of the contrabands, people who had escaped slavery, at Fort Monroe before July 1862. Their chaplain, Lewis Lockwood, wrote down what he heard and published it in the National Anti-Slavery Standard, and it became the first spiritual known to be printed as sheet music. A biography of Harriet Tubman published in 1869 quotes her as saying she used it as a code song.
