Now small fowls flew screaming over the yet yawning gulf; a sullen white surf beat against its steep sides; then all collapsed, and the great shroud of the sea rolled on as it rolled five thousand years ago.

Moby-Dick — The great shroud of the sea (Ch. 135, close)

Herman Melville · 1851

The whale that sank the Essex on 20 November 1820 was reportedly about 85 feet long; the ship herself measured 87 feet, and the whale struck her twice. Her twenty men took to three whaleboats. Seventeen set off from an uninhabited island for South America, and by the time they were rescued only five were alive, after resorting to cannibalism. The first mate, Owen Chase, wrote an account that Melville used as one of the inspirations for the novel, whose ending is based on the sinking.
