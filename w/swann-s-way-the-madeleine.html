No sooner had the warm liquid, and the crumbs with it, touched my palate than a shudder ran through my whole body, and I stopped, intent upon the extraordinary changes that were taking place.

Swann's Way — The Madeleine

Marcel Proust · 1913

Translated by C. K. Scott Moncrieff, 1922

Proust began the novel in 1908, at thirty-eight. Fasquelle, Ollendorff and the Nouvelle Revue Française all turned down the first volume, and he arranged with the publisher Grasset to pay the cost of printing it himself. It came out in 1913. The madeleine episode closes the opening part, Combray I. The town of Combray was modelled on Illiers, the village where Proust spent long holidays as a child, and on his great-uncle's house in Auteuil.
