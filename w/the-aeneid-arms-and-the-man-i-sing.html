Arms, and the man I sing, who, forc’d by fate,
And haughty Juno’s unrelenting hate,
Expell’d and exil’d, left the Trojan shore.

The Aeneid — Arms, and the man I sing (opening)

Virgil · 29–19 BCE

Translated by John Dryden

The Aeneid took Virgil the last ten years of his life, from 29 to 19 BC, and runs to 9,896 lines. Legend has it that he wrote only three lines a day. The poem was unfinished when he died, and he left a wish that the manuscript be burned. Augustus ordered his literary executors to disregard it and to publish the poem with as few changes as possible.
