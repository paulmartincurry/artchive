Tomb of Pope Alexander VII

Gian Lorenzo Bernini · 1671–78 · Marble, gilded bronze and Sicilian jasper · Over life-size · St. Peter's Basilica, Vatican City

Free to use with credit. Photo: Jean-Pol GRANDMONT, CC BY 3.0

Pope Alexander VII ordered his own tomb, and his diary first mentions it in 1656, but work did not start until 1671, after his death. The niche chosen in St. Peter's had a door in it that could not be moved, so Bernini let the shroud of Sicilian jasper hang over the doorway and placed a gilded bronze Death beneath it, raising an hourglass. Pope Innocent XI objected to the nudity of Truth, and Bernini was made to dress the figure. It was finished in 1678, when Bernini was in his eighties.
