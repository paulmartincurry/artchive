Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge

El Lissitzky · 1919–20 · Lithograph · 48.8 × 69.2 cm · Russian State Library, Moscow

Free to use

El Lissitzky made this lithograph in 1919, during the Russian Civil War between the Reds and the Whites. The title was allegedly suggested by the writer Ilya Ehrenburg and may have been chosen to answer the pogrom slogan Beat the Jews. A year earlier the architect Nikolai Kolli had put up a Red Wedge monument in Moscow, a red triangle driven into a white block, and the historian Maria Elena Versari argues that Lissitzky borrowed its language, though he never mentioned it. The image became known in the West after he moved to Germany in 1921.
