We have been burning the bodies of Shelley and Williams on the sea-shore, to render them fit for removal and regular interment. … All of Shelley was consumed, except his heart, which would not take the flame, and is now preserved in spirits of wine.

Letter to Thomas Moore (Pisa, 27 August 1822)

Lord Byron · August 27, 1822

Shelley's body washed ashore at Viareggio ten days after his boat went down and was identified by Edward Trelawny from the clothing and a copy of Keats's Lamia in a jacket pocket. It was cremated on a beach near Viareggio on 16 August. The presumed heart resisted burning and Trelawny retrieved it; it was possibly calcified from an earlier tubercular infection, or was perhaps his liver.
