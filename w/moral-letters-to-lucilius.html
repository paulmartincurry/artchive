In fact, we are wrong to think that death lies ahead: much of it has passed us by already, for all our past life is in the grip of death.

Moral Letters to Lucilius (Letter 1)

Seneca the Younger · ca. 63–65 CE

Translated by Margaret Graver and A. A. Long · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

This is the first of 124 surviving letters that Seneca wrote in the last two or three years of his life, after he had retired from more than ten years in the service of the Emperor Nero. Lucilius, who received them, was procurator of Sicily, and a letter from central Italy took four to eight days to reach him. Seneca was executed by forced suicide in the year 65. The letters were first printed at Naples in 1475.
