Teotihuacan Mask

Maker unknown · 300–600 CE · Travertine (tecali) · 19.1 × 16.5 × 8.6 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

Many stone masks have been found at Teotihuacan, and they have generally been believed to have had a funerary use, though some scholars note that they do not seem to have come from burials. This one, carved in travertine, dates from 300 to 600 CE, when the city held an estimated 100,000 people. It is 19.1 centimetres tall.
