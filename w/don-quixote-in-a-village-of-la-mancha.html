In a village of La Mancha, the name of which I have no desire to call to mind, there lived not long since one of those gentlemen that keep a lance in the lance-rack, an old buckler, a lean hack, and a greyhound for coursing.

Don Quixote — In a village of La Mancha (opening)

Miguel de Cervantes · 1605

Translated by John Ormsby

A local tradition in Argamasilla de Alba holds that Cervantes began the book there while a prisoner in the cave of Medrano. He left the village of the opening sentence unnamed on purpose, and explains in the last chapter that it was so that all the towns of La Mancha could contend for it. In 2004 a team from Complutense University deduced that it was Villanueva de los Infantes. The first edition was printed in 1605 by Juan de la Cuesta.
