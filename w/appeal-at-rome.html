I offer neither pay, nor quarters, nor provisions; I offer hunger, thirst, forced marches, battles and death. Let him who loves his country in his heart and not with his lips only, follow me.

Appeal at Rome

Giuseppe Garibaldi · July 2, 1849

Translated by G. M. Trevelyan, 1907

Garibaldi was two days short of his forty-second birthday on 2 July 1849, and a general of the Roman Republic. He had come back to Italy only the year before, after fourteen years of exile in South America that began with a death sentence for his part in an uprising in Piedmont. In Montevideo he had worked as a trader and schoolmaster and raised an Italian Legion for the Uruguayan Civil War. His wife Anita, who had fought beside him in Brazil, died on 4 August 1849, a month after this appeal.
