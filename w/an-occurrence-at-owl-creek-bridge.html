Peyton Farquhar was dead; his body, with a broken neck, swung gently from side to side beneath the timbers of the Owl Creek bridge.

An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge (ending)

Ambrose Bierce · 1890

Bierce fought for the Union in the Civil War, at Shiloh among other battles, and later drew maps of likely battlefields as a topographical engineer. The story ran in the San Francisco Examiner on 13 July 1890, when he was forty-eight, and was collected the next year in Tales of Soldiers and Civilians. The real Owl Creek Bridge is in Tennessee; Bierce probably moved the story to northern Alabama because that bridge had no railroad near it at the time.
