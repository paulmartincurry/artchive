The end of autumn, and some rooks
Are perched upon a withered branch

On a Withered Branch (Kare-eda ni)

Matsuo Bashō · 1680

Translated by Basil Hall Chamberlain, 1902

By 1680 the poet had a full-time job in Edo teaching twenty disciples, under the pen name Tōsei. That winter he moved across the river to Fukagawa, out of the public eye. His disciples built him a hut there and planted a Japanese banana tree, a bashō, in the yard, and he took his name from it. He was about thirty-six.
