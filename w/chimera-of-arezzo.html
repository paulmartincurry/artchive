Chimera of Arezzo

Maker unknown · c. 400 BC · Bronze · 78.5 × 129 cm · National Archaeological Museum, Florence

Free to use with credit. Photo: Sailko, CC BY-SA 3.0

Construction workers found the bronze on 15 November 1553 near the San Lorentino gate in Arezzo, in a hoard that had been buried for safety. It lacked its snake tail and two paws and was first taken for a lion; Giorgio Vasari identified it from ancient coins. Cosimo I de' Medici claimed it and showed it in the Palazzo Vecchio. The tail was not replaced until 1785, when the restorer set the snake biting the goat's horn, incorrectly. An Etruscan inscription on the right foreleg is thought to read as an offering to the god Tinia.
