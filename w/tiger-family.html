Tiger Family

Maker unknown · late 1800s · Hanging scroll, ink and color on paper · 170 × 90.4 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

The painted paper is 1.7 metres tall, and with its mounting the scroll is over two and a half. Korean tiger paintings usually include magpies; here the painter put a crimson sun behind a pine instead. On New Year's Eve, pictures of fierce tigers were traditionally pasted on entrance doors to keep evil spirits out. Cleveland bought the scroll in 1997 from a dealer in Amagasaki, Japan.
