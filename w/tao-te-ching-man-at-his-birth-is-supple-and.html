Man at his birth is supple and weak; at his death, firm and strong. (So it is with) all things. Trees and plants, in their early growth, are soft and brittle; at their death, dry and withered.

Tao Te Ching — Man at his birth is supple and weak (Ch. 76)

Laozi (Lao Tzu) · 4th–3rd century BC

Translated by James Legge

Legends say Laozi was born old and lived for 996 years, after twelve earlier incarnations. Traditional accounts make him royal archivist to the Zhou court at Wangcheng, modern Luoyang, where he met Confucius. The Tang dynasty later claimed him as its ancestor.
