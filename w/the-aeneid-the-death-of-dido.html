I have lived and fulfilled Fortune's allotted course; and now shall I go a queenly phantom under the earth. I have built a renowned city; I have seen my ramparts rise;

The Aeneid — The Death of Dido (Book IV)

Virgil · 29–19 BCE

Translated by J. W. Mackail

Virgil is said to have recited this book, with the second and sixth, to Augustus. The only full account of Dido that survives from before his is by his contemporary Trogus, in which she climbs the pyre and kills herself with a sword when a king named Iarbas demands to marry her. Virgil died in 19 BC with the poem unfinished and left a wish that the manuscript be burned; Augustus ordered it published.
