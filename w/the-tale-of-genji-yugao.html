The Tale of Genji — Yūgao (the phantom)

Murasaki Shikibu · early 11th century

Translated by Arthur Waley, 1925

The Tale of Genji was written in the early 11th century by Murasaki Shikibu, a lady-in-waiting at the Heian court. Its first thirty-three chapters follow Genji's youth and love affairs; he is the emperor's son by a low-ranking concubine, taken out of the succession for political reasons and made a commoner with the surname Minamoto. Murasaki is believed to have drawn Genji partly from Fujiwara no Michinaga, regent in all but name.
