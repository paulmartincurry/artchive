Twelve-sided Ewer with Sphinxes and Human-Headed Inscriptions

Maker unknown · 1300–1350 · Hammered sheet of brass inlaid with silver · Height 44.8 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

The ewer was hammered from a sheet of brass, given twelve sides and inlaid with silver in Khurasan between 1300 and 1350. A crouching lion sits on the spout, sphinxes walk round the middle, and the lowest band is an inscription whose letters end in human heads, a script found only on metalwork in Islamic art. It stands 44.8 centimetres high. Cleveland bought it from Hagop Kevorkian in 1945.
