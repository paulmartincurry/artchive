Ko-omote Noh Mask

Maker unknown · 18th century · Cypress wood with white, black, and red pigments · 21.6 × 14 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

Noh masks are carved from blocks of Japanese cypress and painted with natural pigments on a base of glue and crushed seashell. When the actor tilts his head slightly upward the mask catches more light and appears to laugh or smile; tilted downward, it appears sad or mad. In most plays only the main actor wears a mask.
