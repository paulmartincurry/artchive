I bite into a persimmon
and a bell resounds—
Hōryūji

Haiku (Persimmon)

Masaoka Shiki · 1895

Translated by Janine Beichman · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to the one haiku on purpose.

Shiki had tuberculosis, and his pen name is a word for the cuckoo, a bird said in Japan to cough blood as it sings. In 1895 he went to China as a war correspondent and arrived after the peace treaty had been signed. He coughed blood on the voyage back, was put in hospital in Kobe, and then convalesced in his home town of Matsuyama, in the house of the novelist Natsume Sōseki. He turned twenty-eight that October.
