Chokwe Male Face Mask (Chihongo)

Maker unknown · Mid–late 19th century · Wood, raffia, burlap, turaco feathers, guinea fowl feathers, and pigment · 66.1 × 50.8 × 25.4 cm (26 × 20 × 10 in.) · Art Institute of Chicago

Free to use

Chihongo is the male counterpart of the female mask called mwana pwo, and both are danced during male initiation rites; chihongo also appears where a chief's affirmation is wanted. The feathers on this one come from turaco and guinea fowl. Its paper trail begins in a private collection in Berlin around 1912. It later belonged to a collector in Munich and to dealers in New York and Dallas, and the museum bought it in 2003.
