Stirrup-Spout Bottle with Mountain Sacrifice Scene

Maker unknown · 500–800 CE · Ceramic, slip · 21 × 12.7 × 19.1 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

Stirrup-spout bottles are not shaped for pouring or filling, and their only confirmed purpose is as grave goods. Moche potters relied mainly on red and white and used moulds, which allowed some forms to be made in quantity, and their vessels show hunting, fishing, fighting, sacrifice and ceremony. This bottle, made between 500 and 800 CE, is 21 centimetres tall and was given to the Met in 1964 by Mr. and Mrs. Nathan Cummings.
