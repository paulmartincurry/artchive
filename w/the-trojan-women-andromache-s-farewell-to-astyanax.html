The Artchive
Kiss me. This one time;
Not ever again. Put up thine arms, and climb
About my neck: now, kiss me, lips to lips.

The Trojan Women — Andromache's Farewell to Astyanax

Euripides · 415 BC

Translated by Gilbert Murray

Euripides staged The Trojan Women at the City Dionysia in 415 BC as the third play in a group drawn from the Iliad, with Alexandros, Palamedes and the satyr play Sisyphus, which survive only in fragments. He came second, losing to the little-known tragedian Xenocles. Some scholars link the play to the Athenian massacre on Melos in 416 BC; the historian Mark Ringer thinks the timing makes that unlikely.
