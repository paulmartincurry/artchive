The Artchive
O Soul go not to the West
Where level wastes of sand stretch on and on;
And demons rage, swine-headed, hairy-skinned,
With bulging eyes;
Who in wild laughter gnash projecting fangs.
O Soul go not to the West
Where many perils wait!

The Great Summons (the four quarters)

Qu Yuan · 3rd century BC (attributed)

Translated by Arthur Waley

The Great Summons is one of the poems of the Chu Ci anthology, and its authorship has been given both to Qu Yuan and to Jing Cuo, a poet otherwise little known. It comes from a shamanic practice of calling back the soul of someone who seemed to have died, most likely at first so that it would return to its body. One tradition, which historians think unlikely, holds that Qu Yuan wrote it to talk himself out of suicide.
