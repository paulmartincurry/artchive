I die of thirst beside the fountain,
Trembling with cold and love’s fire;
Blind I am, yet I see things plain;
Witless; and yet of wisdom’s choir,
Full careless; yet discipline admire.

Ballade: I die of thirst beside the fountain

Charles d'Orléans · 1458

Translated by A. S. Kline · Free to reproduce for non-commercial use (poetryintranslation.com terms); not public domain.

In 1458 Charles d'Orléans opened a poetry contest at his château of Blois with this line, and about ten ballades were written on it, François Villon's among them. He had fought at Agincourt in 1415 and spent 25 years as a prisoner of war in England.
