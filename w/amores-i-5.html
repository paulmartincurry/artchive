One half of the window was open, the other closed:
the light was just as it often is in the woods,
it glimmered like Phoebus dying at twilight,
or when night goes, but day has still not risen.

Amores I.5 (Corinna in an Afternoon)

Ovid · c. 16 BCE

Translated by A. S. Kline · Free to reproduce for non-commercial use (poetryintranslation.com terms); not public domain.

The first collection of the Amores ran to five books and is thought to have been published in 16–15 BC, when Ovid was in his late twenties. The version that survives is a later one, cut down to three books. Ovid had been trained in rhetoric so that he could practise law, and he held minor public posts before resigning to write poetry, a decision of which his father disapproved.
