All visible objects, man, are but as pasteboard masks. But in each event—in the living act, the undoubted deed—there, some unknown but still reasoning thing puts forth the mouldings of its features from behind the unreasoning mask. If man will strike, strike through the mask!

Moby-Dick — Pasteboard masks (Ch. 36, The Quarter-Deck)

Herman Melville · 1851

Ahab was apparently not drawn from any captain Melville sailed under. Melville built him with Samuel Taylor Coleridge's lecture on Hamlet in mind, which described Shakespeare making a character out of one faculty in morbid excess. His speeches mix Quaker archaism with Shakespeare's idiom. In the novel he is fifty-eight, and the deck has been bored with shallow holes to steady his ivory leg.
