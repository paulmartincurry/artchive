Ghost and Oil Lamp

Tani Bun'ichi · 1810 · hanging scroll, ink, color, and gold on silk · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

An inscription in gold on the stem of the lamp stand dates the painting to the twelfth month of 1810. In it Tani Bun'ichi wrote that he did not like painting ghosts and tried it only after listening to a man named Ono. He was the adopted son of the painter Tani Bunchō, was about twenty-three at the time, and died eight years later. A near copy of the scene, once owned by a teller of ghost stories, is in a temple in Tokyo.
