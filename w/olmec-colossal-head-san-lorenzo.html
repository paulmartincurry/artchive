Olmec Colossal Head, San Lorenzo

Maker unknown · ca. 1200–900 BCE · Basalt · Museo de Antropología de Xalapa

Free to use with credit. Photo: Marshall Astor (Life on the Edge), CC BY-SA 2.0

The basalt for the San Lorenzo heads came from the Sierra de los Tuxtlas, more than 150 kilometres away, moved by people who had no beasts of burden and no working wheels. The sculptors chose roughly spherical boulders, chipped them into shape and finished the surface with hammerstones and abrasives. Two of the San Lorenzo heads were recarved from stone thrones, and one keeps traces of plaster and red paint. The heads at this site were buried by 900 BC.
