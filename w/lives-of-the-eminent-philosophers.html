Lives of the Eminent Philosophers (Book VI)

Diogenes of Sinope (via Diogenes Laërtius) · 4th century BCE

Translated by R. D. Hicks

Diogenes was the son of a money-changer in Sinope, on the Black Sea, and left the city after he and his father were accused of debasing the currency. He spent his winters in Athens, begging and sheltering in a large ceramic jar made for storing food; the Athenians reportedly replaced it when a youth broke it. His summers he spent outdoors in Corinth. Diogenes Laertius holds that he lived to be about ninety, and the Corinthians buried him outside the city walls near the western gate.
