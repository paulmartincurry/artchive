Sword of Goujian

Maker unknown · ca. 496–465 BCE · Bronze · Hubei Provincial Museum

Free to use with credit. Photo: Windmemories, CC BY-SA 4.0

The sword came out of a tomb in Hubei in December 1965, in a black-lacquered wooden scabbard that fitted almost air-tight. The tomb had been soaked in underground water for more than 2,000 years, and the blade was almost untarnished and still sharp. Eight characters inlaid in gold near the hilt say that the King of Yue made it for his own use. In 1994 a worker in Singapore knocked it against its case and left a 7 millimetre crack, and it has not left China since.
