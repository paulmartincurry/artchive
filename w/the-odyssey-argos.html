As soon as he saw Ulysses standing there, he dropped his ears and wagged his tail, but he could not get close up to his master. … As he spoke he went inside the buildings to the cloister where the suitors were, but Argos died as soon as he had recognised his master.

The Odyssey — Argos (Book XVII)

Homer · c. 8th century BCE

Translated by Samuel Butler

Odysseus raised Argos as a hunting dog but left for Troy before he could hunt with him; other young men took the dog after deer, wild goats and hares. The name comes from a Greek word for shining white that was also used of quick dogs. Argos is the only member of the household to recognise Odysseus without help from a god or proof from Odysseus himself.
