We shall not always plant while others reap
The golden increment of bursting fruit,
Not always countenance, abject and mute,
That lesser men should hold their brothers cheap;

From the Dark Tower

Countee Cullen · 1927

From the Dark Tower was collected in Copper Sun, which Cullen published in New York in 1927. In a poetry contest run by the magazine Opportunity he came second to Langston Hughes's The Weary Blues. Hughes, a close friend, later criticised Harlem Renaissance writers, Cullen among them though not by name, for wanting to run away spiritually from their race.
