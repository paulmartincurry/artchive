We declare that the world’s splendour has been enriched by a new beauty; the beauty of speed. A racing motor-car, its frame adorned with great pipes, like snakes with explosive breath . . . a roaring motor-car, which looks as though running on shrapnel, is more beautiful than the Victory of Samothrace.

Initial Manifesto of Futurism (points 1–8)

F. T. Marinetti · 1909

The manifesto was printed in French on the front page of the Paris daily Le Figaro on 20 February 1909. Marinetti was thirty-two, born in Alexandria to Italian parents, with a law degree from Pavia that he never used. The year before, he had driven his car into a ditch outside Milan to avoid two cyclists, and he refers to the crash in the text.
