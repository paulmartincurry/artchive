What a chimera then is man! What a novelty! What a monster, what a chaos, what a contradiction, what a prodigy! Judge of all things, imbecile worm of the earth; depositary of truth, a sink of uncertainty and error; the pride and refuse of the universe!

Pensées — What a chimera then is man (434)

Blaise Pascal · 1670

Translated by W. F. Trotter

Pascal fell seriously ill in 1659 and in his last years often tried to refuse his doctors' care. His last major undertaking was a public transport service in Paris, horse-drawn carriages that ran five fixed routes at a fixed price of five sols and left even with no passengers. He died on 19 August 1662, the morning after going into convulsions. An autopsy found grave damage to his stomach and brain, and the cause of his illness was never precisely determined.
