An erring Yaksha made his hapless home,
Doomed by his master humbly to abide,
And spend a long, long year of absence from his bride.

Meghadūta (The Cloud Messenger)

Kālidāsa · ca. 4th–5th Century CE

Translated by Arthur W. Ryder, 1912

The Meghadūta runs to 120 stanzas in two parts. Little is known of Kālidāsa's life beyond what can be inferred from his poems and plays, and none of his work can be dated with precision; the most widely held theory places him in the reign of Chandragupta II, who ruled from 380 to 415. Ramtek, near Nagpur, is believed to have prompted the poem. Later Sanskrit poets wrote so many messenger poems on its model, often in its metre, that they became a genre.
