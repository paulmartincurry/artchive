Like that of a cuckoo in the dense jungle,
flitting through deep forest thickets:
sweet was the tone of my voice.
With age, it cracks here & there.

Therīgāthā (Ambapālī)

Ambapālī (Courtesan-Turned-Elder Nun of Vesālī) · ca. 5th–3rd Century BCE

Translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Ambapālī was the royal courtesan of Vaishali, a republic in what is now Bihar, around 500 BC. Her name comes from the Pāli word for mango. Buddhist tradition records that she built a monastery in her own mango grove and gave the land to the Buddha's order, and that the Buddha stayed there shortly before his death. She later gave up her position. The tradition says she came to her insight by contemplating the ageing of her own body. Her son became an elder in the order.
