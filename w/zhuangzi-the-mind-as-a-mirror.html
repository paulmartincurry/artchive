All of which may be summed up in the one word passivity. For the perfect man employs his mind as a mirror. It grasps nothing: it refuses nothing. It receives, but does not keep. And thus he can triumph over matter, without injury to himself.

Zhuangzi — The Mind as a Mirror (ch. VII)

Zhuangzi (Chuang Tzu) · 4th century BC

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, 1889

The book takes its name from Zhuang Zhou, whom the historian Sima Qian describes as a minor official from the town of Meng in the state of Song, in the late fourth century BC. Giles, its translator, held the chair of Chinese at Cambridge for 35 years and gave his name to the Wade–Giles romanization.
