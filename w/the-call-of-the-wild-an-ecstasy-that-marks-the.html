There is an ecstasy that marks the summit of life, and beyond which life cannot rise. And such is the paradox of living, this ecstasy comes when one is most alive, and it comes as a complete forgetfulness that one is alive.

The Call of the Wild — An ecstasy that marks the summit of life (Ch. 3)

Jack London · 1903

Jack London went to the Klondike in 1897, aged twenty-one, and stayed almost a year before scurvy sent him home to California. Buck was based on a dog he knew in Dawson City, a St. Bernard and Scotch Collie cross owned by two brothers named Bond. He set out to write a short story of 4,000 words and stopped at 32,000. The Saturday Evening Post bought it for $750 and ran it in five instalments in 1903.
