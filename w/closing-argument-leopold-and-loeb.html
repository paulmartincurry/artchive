I am pleading for the future; I am pleading for a time when hatred and cruelty will not control the hearts of men.

Closing Argument, Leopold and Loeb

Clarence Darrow · August 1924

Darrow was sixty-seven and spoke for twelve hours. He had his two clients, aged nineteen and eighteen, plead guilty so that the case would go before a judge and not a jury, which turned the trial into a long sentencing hearing. Judge John Caverly sentenced both to life in prison plus 99 years. The argument was published in several editions in the late 1920s and early 1930s, and reissued when Darrow died.
