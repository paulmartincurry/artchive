Had I so interfered in behalf of the rich, the powerful, the intelligent, the so-called great … every man in this court would have deemed it an act worthy of reward rather than punishment.

Address to the Court

John Brown · November 2, 1859

John Brown was fifty-nine and had worked most of his life as a tanner and surveyor when, in October 1859, he led a raid on the federal armory at Harpers Ferry, Virginia. He was tried for treason against Virginia, the murder of five men and inciting a slave insurrection, and found guilty on every charge. He was hanged on 2 December, a month after this address, the first person executed for treason in the history of the United States. His brother Frederick visited him in jail before the execution.
