A Madman's Diary

Lu Xun (Zhou Shuren) · 1918

Translated by Yang Xianyi and Gladys Yang · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Lu Xun took the diary form and the idea of the madman from Nikolai Gogol's story of the same name. He opens with a short note in classical Chinese and then gives 13 fragments of the diary in the vernacular, at a time when classical Chinese was still the standard written language. He was thirty-six. The story later opened his first collection and is often called China's first modern short story, though Chen Hengzhe had used the same elements six months earlier in a journal for Chinese students in the United States.
