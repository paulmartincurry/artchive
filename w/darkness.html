The bright sun was extinguished, … and the icy Earth
Swung blind and blackening in the moonless air;

Darkness

Lord Byron · 1816

Byron wrote this in July 1816 at Geneva, at twenty-eight. Mount Tambora had erupted the year before and the summer was cold and dark across Europe. He said he wrote it on a day when the fowls went to roost at noon and candles were lit as at midnight. An Italian scientist had predicted that the sun would go out on 18 July, and the prediction set off riots. The poem was published the same year in The Prisoner of Chillon collection.
