Let right deeds be
Thy motive, not the fruit which comes from them.
And live in action! Labour! Make thine acts
Thy piety, casting all self aside,
Contemning gain and merit; equable
In good or evil: equability
Is Yog, is piety!

Bhagavad Gita — Let Right Deeds Be Thy Motive (Chapter II)

Vyasa (Sanskrit Epic) · 2nd–1st century BCE; English 1885

Translated by Edwin Arnold

The Gita is set as a dialogue between the prince Arjuna and his charioteer Krishna at the start of the Kurukshetra War, when Arjuna hesitates to fight. Edwin Arnold, who made this verse version, had been Principal of the Deccan College in India; his Song Celestial came out in 1885.
