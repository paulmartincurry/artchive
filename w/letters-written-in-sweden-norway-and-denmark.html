Letters Written in Sweden, Norway, and Denmark (Letter VI)

Mary Wollstonecraft · 1796

Wollstonecraft spent three months of 1795 in Scandinavia, travelling with her one-year-old daughter and a maid. She was there to negotiate over a ship and cargo stolen from her lover, Gilbert Imlay, a purpose that stayed unknown until a historian found the documents in Swedish and Norwegian archives in the 1980s. The letters draw on her journal and on what she wrote to Imlay. She published them in January 1796 because she was heavily in debt, and it was the last of her works issued in her lifetime.
