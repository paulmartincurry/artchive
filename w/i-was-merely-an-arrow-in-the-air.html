I was merely an arrow in the air,
Which falls back, finding no refuge there.

I Was Merely an Arrow in the Air (fragment)

Al-Mutanabbi · 10th century CE

Translated by Reynold A. Nicholson, 1907

Al-Mutanabbi was born in Kufa in 915. After his years with Sayf al-Dawla he went to the court of Kafur in Egypt, who mistrusted him, and when he left in 960 he wrote satires against his former patron. In 1932 a bookselling street in Baghdad, about a kilometre long, was named after him; a statue of the poet stands at its end above the Tigris.
