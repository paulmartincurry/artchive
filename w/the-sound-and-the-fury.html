I give it to you not that you may remember time, but that you might forget it now and then for a moment and not spend all your breath trying to conquer it.

The Sound and the Fury (June Second, 1910)

William Faulkner · 1929

Faulkner began this as a short story and called it Twilight while he worked. Harcourt Brace, the publisher of his previous novel, turned the finished manuscript down in March 1928. One of that firm's editors, Harrison Smith, set up a new company that took the book on. Before it came out on October 7, 1929, Faulkner heavily rewrote this section, trying the quarrel between Quentin and Caddy in three different places before leaving it at the end. He was thirty-two.
