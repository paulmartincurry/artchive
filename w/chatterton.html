Chatterton (The Death of Chatterton)

Henry Wallis · 1856 · Oil paint on canvas · 62.2 × 93.3 cm · Tate, London

Free to use

Thomas Chatterton was seventeen when he poisoned himself with arsenic in a garret in 1770. Wallis painted the scene in a friend's rooms in Gray's Inn, with St Paul's Cathedral on the skyline through the window, close to the Brooke Street garret where the poet had died 86 years before. The model was the young writer George Meredith, whose first wife later left him for Wallis. It was Wallis's first exhibited work, shown at the Royal Academy in 1856 when he was twenty-six.
