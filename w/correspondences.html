In Nature's temple living pillars rise,
And words are murmured none have understood,
And man must wander through a tangled wood
Of symbols watching him with friendly eyes.

Correspondences

Charles Baudelaire · 1857

Translated by F. P. Sturm

When Les Fleurs du mal came out in 1857, its author and publisher were prosecuted for an insult to public decency. Baudelaire was fined 300 francs and six poems were suppressed; the ban on them was not lifted in France until 1949. The book holds nearly all of his poetry, written from 1840 until his death in August 1867.
