Benin Palace Plaque

Maker unknown · 16th–17th Century · Brass · British Museum

Free to use with credit. Photo: Michel wal, CC BY-SA 3.0

Hundreds of rectangular plaques like this one hung on the pillars of the royal palace in Benin City, fixed by nails punched through them. They were cast by the lost-wax method, in brass that came partly from bracelet-shaped ingots bought from Portuguese traders. British forces took them when they sacked the city in 1897, and the Foreign Office gave a large number to the British Museum. From 1950 the museum sold more than 30 to the Nigerian government as duplicates; the sales stopped in 1972.
