The love of men and women, — is it only when they meet face to face? To feel sorrow at an unaccomplished meeting, to grieve over empty vows, to spend the long night sleepless and alone, to yearn for distant skies, in a neglected home to think fondly of the past — this is what Love is.

Essays in Idleness (137) — Are We Only to Look at Flowers in Full Bloom?

Yoshida Kenkō · 1330–1332

Translated by G. B. Sansom, 1911

Kenkō was probably born around 1283, the son of an administrative official. Documents forged by the Yoshida Shinto authorities gave him the original name Urabe Kaneyoshi, a claim recent research has shown to be false. He also wrote poetry, and his entries in court poetry contests in 1335 and 1344 are on record. A century after him, the poet Shōtetsu compared the book's form to The Pillow Book.
