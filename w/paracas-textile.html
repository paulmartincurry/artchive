Paracas Textile

Maker unknown · ca. 300–200 BCE · Wool and cotton · British Museum

Free to use with credit. Photo: Mike Peel, CC BY-SA 4.0

These embroidered fragments were cut from much larger cloths that wrapped the dead in a necropolis on the Paracas Peninsula in Peru. Julio C. Tello first visited the site in 1925 and uncovered the first of hundreds of wrapped burials on 25 October 1927. Some of the cloths were as long as 34 metres. The natural dyes kept their colour for more than 2,000 years, which is put down to the dry conditions and the absence of sunlight.
