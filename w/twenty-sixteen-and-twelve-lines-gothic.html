Twenty, Sixteen, and Twelve Lines Gothic (Specimen of Leavenworth's Patent Wood Type)

J. M. Debow · c. 1840–49 · Wood type printed on paper · 37 leaves; 19.5 x 32.5 cm (book) · The New York Public Library

Free to use

William Leavenworth made wood type in Allentown, New Jersey, and in 1834 he began cutting letters with a pantograph, so the same shape could be reproduced from a pattern. This book is Leavenworth's only surviving specimen. Wood type took over large poster lettering in the nineteenth century because it was lighter and cheaper than metal type in big sizes.
