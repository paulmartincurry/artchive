The Antilles: Fragments of Epic Memory (Nobel Lecture)

Derek Walcott · 1992

Short quotation from a work still in copyright, credited to the author. Not public domain; kept to one line on purpose.

Walcott was sixty-two when he received the Nobel Prize in 1992, the second Caribbean writer to do so after Saint-John Perse in 1960. He was teaching literature and writing at Boston University, where he worked for more than two decades. He published his first poem at fourteen in The Voice of St Lucia, and by nineteen had self-published two collections, with his mother paying for the printing. As a young man he had also trained as a painter.
