Cihuateotl

Maker unknown · 15th–early 16th century · Volcanic stone (porphyritic andesite), pigment · 66 × 43.8 × 43.2 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

The Cihuateteo were the spirits of women who died in childbirth, which the Aztecs held equal to dying in battle. They were said to descend to earth on five days of the calendar and haunt crossroads, and this figure at the Met is inscribed on top of its head with the name Ce Calli, 1 House, one of those days. Carved in volcanic stone and 66 centimetres tall, it was bought by the museum in 1900.
