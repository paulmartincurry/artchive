’Tis neither body nor soul, for I belong to the soul of the Beloved.
I have put duality away, I have seen that the two worlds are one;
‘One I seek, One I know, One I see, One I call.
He is the. first, He is the last, He is the outward, He is the inward;

Divan-e Shams — What Is to Be Done, O Moslems? (XXXI)

Jalāl al-Dīn Muḥammad Rūmī · 13th century

Translated by R. A. Nicholson, 1898

Rumi's family came from Balkh, and he settled in Konya with them at the age of 19. The arrival in Konya of the wandering dervish Shams Tabrīzī altered the course of his life. When Shams mysteriously disappeared, Rumi went through an intense period of grief, and the collection of his lyric poems carries Shams's name.
