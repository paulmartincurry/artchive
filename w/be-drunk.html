You have to be always drunk. … But on what? Wine, poetry or virtue, as you wish. But be drunk.

Be Drunk (Enivrez-vous)

Charles Baudelaire · 1864

Translated by Louis Simpson · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Baudelaire left Paris for Belgium in 1864, partly in the hope of selling the rights to his works and of giving lectures. His publisher, Poulet-Malassis, had gone bankrupt three years earlier. In Brussels he began to drink to excess. He suffered a massive stroke in 1866 and died in Paris the following year, aged forty-six.
