I am certain of nothing but of the holiness of the Heart's affections, and the truth of Imagination. What the Imagination seizes as Beauty must be truth

Letter to Benjamin Bailey (22 November 1817)

John Keats · 1817

Benjamin Bailey was a friend, and Keats was twenty-two when he wrote to him. His first book, Poems, had come out earlier that year and failed with the critics, and he had moved to new publishers, Taylor and Hessey, who paid him an advance on a second volume. A passage from this letter was later reworked into the closing lines of Ode on a Grecian Urn.
