The Death of Cleopatra

Edmonia Lewis · 1876 · Marble · Smithsonian American Art Museum

Free to use

Edmonia Lewis carved this in Rome in 1875 and shipped it, nearly three tons of it, to Philadelphia for the Centennial Exposition. She could not get it shipped back and sold it in America. A gambler named Blind John Condon bought it from a Chicago saloon to mark the grave of a racehorse called Cleopatra, and it stood in front of the grandstand at his track for nearly a century. Boy Scouts later painted it in a storage yard. It was given to the Smithsonian in 1994 and restored for $30,000.
