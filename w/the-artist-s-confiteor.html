Ah, must one eternally suffer, for ever be a fugitive from Beauty?

Nature, pitiless enchantress, ever-victorious rival, leave me! Tempt my desires and my pride no more. The contemplation of Beauty is a duel where the artist screams with terror before being vanquished.

The Artist's Confiteor

Charles Baudelaire · 1862

Translated by F. P. Sturm

At 21 Baudelaire received a sizable inheritance and squandered much of it within a few years, until his family obtained a decree to place his property in trust. He became known in artistic circles as a dandy and free-spender, and he regularly begged his mother for money throughout his career. The prose poems were published in 1869, after his death.
