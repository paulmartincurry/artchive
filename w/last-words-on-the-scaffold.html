Above all, don’t forget to show my head to the people: it’s worth seeing.

Last Words on the Scaffold

Georges Jacques Danton · April 5, 1794

Translated by Norman Hampson, 1978 · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Danton was beheaded at sunset on 5 April 1794, the last to die of a group of fifteen. He was thirty-four, a lawyer by training. The words are reported, not recorded: it is said he spoke them to his executioner, Sanson. His trial before the Revolutionary Tribunal, a court he had supported setting up a year earlier, lasted three days. He was refused his witnesses, only seven of the twelve jurors required by law were present, and the defendants were taken from the room before the verdict.
