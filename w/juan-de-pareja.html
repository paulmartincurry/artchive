Juan de Pareja

Diego Velázquez · 1650 · Oil on canvas · 32 × 27 1/2 in. (81.3 × 69.9 cm) · The Met

Free to use

Velázquez painted his assistant Juan de Pareja in Rome in 1650, while he was there to buy works of art for the royal palace in Madrid, as practice before his portrait of Pope Innocent X. Pareja was enslaved by Velázquez at the time, and was freed by him four years later. The canvas sold at Christie's in 1801 for 39 guineas. In 1970 it sold there again for £2,310,000, the first painting to sell for more than a million pounds.
