I know that I shall meet my fate
Somewhere among the clouds above;
Those that I fight I do not hate
Those that I guard I do not love;

An Irish Airman Foresees His Death

W. B. Yeats · 1919

Written in 1918, while the First World War was still being fought. Yeats did not want to publish political poems at the height of the war, so he held these sixteen lines back until it was over, and they came out in 1919 in the Macmillan edition of The Wild Swans at Coole. The airman is widely believed to be Major Robert Gregory, his friend and the only child of Lady Gregory, with whom Yeats founded the Abbey Theatre.
