The Thing can not be described--there is no language for such abysms of shrieking and immemorial lunacy, such eldritch contradictions of all matter, force, and cosmic order. A mountain walked or stumbled.

The Call of Cthulhu — The Thing Can Not Be Described (part III)

H. P. Lovecraft · 1926 (Weird Tales, February 1928)

Lovecraft himself thought the story only middling. The scholar S. T. Joshi has compared the vast door the sailors open on R'lyeh to the tilting moon-door in A. Merritt's 1918 novella The Moon Pool, a book Lovecraft often praised. In 1926, shortly before he started work on the story, Lovecraft had read William Scott-Elliot's books on Atlantis and Lemuria.
