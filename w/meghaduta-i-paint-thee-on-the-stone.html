And when I paint that loving jealousy
With chalk upon the rock, and my caress
As at thy feet I lie, I cannot see
Through tears that to mine eyes unbidden press--
So stern a fate denies a painted happiness.

Meghadūta — I Paint Thee on the Stone (stanzas 41–43)

Kālidāsa · ca. 4th–5th century CE

Translated by Arthur W. Ryder, 1912

The yaksha who speaks the poem has been exiled for a year to Central India for neglecting his duties, and his wife waits for him at Alaka on Mount Kailāsa. Horace Hayman Wilson made the first English translation in 1813. Gustav Holst set the poem as a choral work in 1910, and Debaki Bose filmed it as Meghdoot in 1945.
