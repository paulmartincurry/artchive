What moves men of genius, or rather what inspires their work, is not new ideas, but their obsession with the idea that what has already been said is still not enough.

Journal

Eugène Delacroix · 15 May 1824

Translated by Lucy Norton · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Delacroix had turned twenty-six three weeks before he wrote this entry. Orphaned at sixteen, he had trained under Pierre-Narcisse Guérin, and two years earlier the State had bought his first major painting, The Barque of Dante. In 1824 he showed The Massacre at Chios, having repainted its sky and distant landscape after seeing pictures by John Constable. The state bought that one too.
