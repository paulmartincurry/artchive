Ugolino and His Sons

Jean-Baptiste Carpeaux · 1865–67 · Saint-Béat marble · Overall: 77 3/4 × 59 × 43 1/2 in. · The Met

Free to use

Carpeaux, a mason's son from Valenciennes, modelled this group in the last year of his residence at the French Academy in Rome, and sketched dying children as part of his preparation. It was cast in bronze in 1863. This marble was then carved in Paris under his supervision for the owner of the Saint-Béat quarry, who wanted it to show at the 1867 International Exposition. The marble weighs 4,955 pounds, or about two and a quarter tonnes.
