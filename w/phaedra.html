Light cares speak out, the weighty have no words.

Phaedra (line 607)

Seneca the Younger · before 54 CE

Translated by Frank Justus Miller, 1907

Seneca based this tragedy of 1,280 lines on the Hippolytus of Euripides, and most likely wrote it before 54 AD, the year his student Nero became emperor. Historians generally agree that his plays were meant to be recited privately to educated audiences, not acted in the public theatres of Rome. When it was revived in the Renaissance it went by the title Hippolytus, and in 1486 it was staged in Latin in the Campo de' Fiori in Rome. Racine drew on it for his Phèdre of 1677.
