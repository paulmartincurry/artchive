Day of wrath! O day of mourning! / See fulfilled the prophets' warning, / Heaven and earth in ashes burning!

Dies Irae

Attributed to Thomas of Celano · 13th century

Translated by William Josiah Irons, 1849

The sequence is attributed either to the Franciscan Thomas of Celano or to Latino Malabranca Orsini, a Dominican teacher in Rome, and some sources place it centuries earlier. Its text is found in a Franciscan missal in Naples that must date between 1253 and 1255, because it does not name Clare of Assisi, who was canonized in 1255. It was the sequence of the Catholic Requiem Mass for centuries, until the reforms of 1969–70 removed it from funerals.
