To ravage, to slaughter, to usurp under false titles, they call empire; and where they make a desert, they call it peace.

Agricola (Ch. 30)

Calgacus (via Tacitus) · ca. 83 / 98 CE

Translated by Oxford Translation, revised (Edward Brooks Jr. edition)

Tacitus wrote this speech and attributed it to Calgacus, a Caledonian chieftain, setting it before the Battle of Mons Graupius in northern Scotland in AD 83 or 84. The Roman general in that battle, Agricola, was Tacitus's father-in-law. The Agricola is the only historical source that features Calgacus, who is not mentioned during or after the battle, and both the man and the speech may be Tacitus's invention.
