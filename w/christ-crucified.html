Christ Crucified

Diego Velázquez · c. 1632 · Oil on canvas · 248 × 169 cm · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Free to use

The painting was made for the Benedictine nuns of the Convent of San Plácido in Madrid, and it is thought to have been given by the convent's founder, a secretary of Philip IV. Velázquez had made his first visit to Rome between 1629 and 1631. In some passages he scratched the still-damp paint with the tip of the brush, most noticeably in the hair falling over the shoulders.
