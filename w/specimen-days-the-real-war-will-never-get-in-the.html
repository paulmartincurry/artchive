Future years will never know the seething hell and the black infernal background of countless minor scenes and interiors, (not the official surface-courteousness of the Generals, not the few great battles) of the Secession war; and it is best they should not—the real war will never get in the books.

Specimen Days — The Real War Will Never Get in the Books

Walt Whitman · 1882

During the Civil War Whitman worked in hospitals in Washington, caring for the wounded. He first wrote of the experience in a New York newspaper in 1863 and, twelve years later, in a book called Memoranda During the War. After a paralytic stroke in early 1873 he moved from Washington to his brother's home in Camden, New Jersey.
