Dragon and Tiger (Six-Fold Screen)

Sesson Shūkei · ca. 1546–56 · One of a pair of six-panel folding screens; ink on paper · Painting: 157.3 × 339 cm (61 15/16 × 133 7/16 in.) · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

This is one of a pair of six-panel folding screens, painted in ink alone across more than three metres of paper. The layout of the pair follows Chinese versions of the subject, which were usually hanging scrolls, but Sesson's tiger was probably modelled on Korean pictures of the Joseon period then in circulation in Japan. Sesson was in his fifties or early sixties when he made it. The screen was once owned by H. Mitsui, and the Cleveland Museum of Art bought it from a Cleveland dealer in 1959.
