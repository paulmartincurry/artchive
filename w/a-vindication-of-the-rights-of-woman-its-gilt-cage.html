Taught from their infancy, that beauty is woman's sceptre, the mind shapes itself to the body, and, roaming round its gilt cage, only seeks to adorn its prison.

A Vindication of the Rights of Woman — Its gilt cage (Ch. III)

Mary Wollstonecraft · 1792

Wollstonecraft had to write the book hurriedly, and when it was done she told her friend William Roscoe that with more time she could have written a better one. Five years earlier she had left a post as a governess in Ireland after only a year to live by writing, which few women then could. The London publisher Joseph Johnson helped her find a place to live and work; she learned French and German, translated, and reviewed novels for his Analytical Review.
