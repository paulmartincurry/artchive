Ahu Tongariki

Maker unknown · ca. 1250–1500 · Volcanic tuff

Free to use with credit. Photo: Ian Sewell, CC BY 2.5

The heaviest moai ever erected is at Ahu Tongariki, and it weighs 86 tonnes. The statues were cut at the Rano Raraku quarry from tuff, a soft rock with lumps of much harder rock in it, and moved to stone platforms around the edge of the island. Every one that stood on a platform was toppled in the years after Europeans arrived in 1722, and none was left standing by 1868. About 50 have since been set up again.
