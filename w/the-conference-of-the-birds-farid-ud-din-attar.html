There in the Simorgh’s radiant face they saw
Themselves, the Simorgh of the world

The Conference of the Birds

Farid ud-Din Attar · 1177 CE

Translated by Afkham Darbandi and Dick Davis · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Attar is a pen-name meaning apothecary. The poet kept a pharmacy in Nishapur, in what is now northeastern Iran, and attended to a very large number of customers himself. He was about thirty-two when he wrote this poem in rhyming couplets. Its title alludes to a verse of the Qur'an in which Solomon and David are taught the speech of the birds.
