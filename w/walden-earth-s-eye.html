A lake is the landscape’s most beautiful and expressive feature. It is earth’s eye; looking into which the beholder measures the depth of his own nature.

Walden — Earth's eye (The Ponds)

Henry David Thoreau · 1854

This comes from The Ponds, the chapter of Walden set in autumn, in which Thoreau writes down the geography of Walden Pond and its neighbours, Flint's, White and Goose ponds. Flint's Pond is the largest, but his favourites were Walden and White. Elsewhere in the book he records the colour and clarity of the water and recounts his experiments to measure the depth and shape of the bottom of the supposedly bottomless Walden Pond.
