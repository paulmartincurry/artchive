I have not broken your heart—you have broken it; and in breaking it, you have broken mine. So much the worse for me that I am strong. Do I want to live?

Wuthering Heights — You have broken mine (Ch. 15)

Emily Brontë · 1847

Most early reviewers granted the novel power and imagination but were baffled by its story and objected to the savagery and selfishness of its people, and many were curious about who the Bell authors really were. The poet and painter Dante Gabriel Rossetti admired it in a letter of 1854 and in the same letter called it a fiend of a book. Algernon Charles Swinburne published an essay on Emily Brontë in The Athenaeum in 1883.
