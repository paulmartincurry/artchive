The Hundred Guilder Print

Rembrandt van Rijn · c. 1648 · Etching, engraving, and drypoint; second state of two · plate: 28 x 39.3 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

The print is said to take its name from the price it fetched. In 1654 a letter to the Bishop of Bruges claimed it had sold in Holland several times for 100 guilders and more. Rembrandt did not sign it. Around 1775 Captain William Baillie bought the worn copper plate, reworked it for an edition of 100, then cut it into four pieces and printed them as separate images.
