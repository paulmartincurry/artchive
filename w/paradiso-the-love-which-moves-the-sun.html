Here vigour failed the lofty fantasy:
But now was turning my desire and will,
Even as a wheel that equally is moved,

The Love which moves the sun and the other stars.

Paradiso — The Love Which Moves the Sun (Canto XXXIII, closing)

Dante Alighieri · c. 1321

Translated by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow

Giovanni Boccaccio claimed that the Paradiso was at first thought unfinished at Dante's death, until his sons discovered the final thirteen cantos among his papers. The Comedy was completed around 1321, shortly before he died, and this part was likely not published until afterwards. No manuscript in Dante's own hand has survived; the Italian Dante Society lists some 800 copies from the 14th and 15th centuries.
