Keris

Maker unknown · 18th–19th Century · Iron, nickel and gold · Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam

Free to use

The pattern on a kris blade comes from forging alternate layers of iron and nickel-bearing iron, and there are around 250 named patterns and around 60 blade forms. Blade, hilt and sheath are made separately and often replaced, so one kris may have a blade from Java, a hilt from Bali and a sheath from Madura. Javanese tradition requires a blade to be cleaned every year, which leaves old ones worn and thin. In Java the kris stopped being a weapon of war after 1830, when Prince Diponegoro had to hand his over to the Dutch.
