Little Dancer of Fourteen Years

Edgar Degas · 1881 · Ribbon, tulle, wax, bronze · National Gallery of Art, Washington

The model was Marie van Goethem, a Belgian student at the dance school of the Paris Opera Ballet. Degas built the figure at two-thirds life size in pigmented beeswax over a metal armature, with rope and paintbrushes inside for support, and dressed it in a real bodice, tutu and slippers and a wig of human hair. He showed it in a glass case at the Impressionist exhibition of 1881, reworked it after 1903, and never sold it. It was found in a corner of his studio after his death. The bronzes were cast for his heirs from 1920.
