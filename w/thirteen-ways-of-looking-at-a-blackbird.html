Among twenty snowy mountains,
the only moving thing was the eye of the blackbird.

Thirteen Ways of Looking at a Blackbird (I, V, XIII)

Wallace Stevens · 1917 (Harmonium, 1923)

Alfred Kreymborg printed the poem first, in October 1917, in the anthology Others, and again two months later in the magazine of the same name. Stevens was thirty-eight. The year before, his job at a surety company in New York had been made redundant after a merger, and he had moved to Hartford to work for the Hartford Accident and Indemnity Company. The thirteen sections were inspired by haiku, though none of them is one by the traditional definition.
