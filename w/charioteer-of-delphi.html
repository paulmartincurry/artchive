Charioteer of Delphi

Maker unknown · c. 470 BC · Bronze · H. 1.8 m · Delphi Archaeological Museum

Free to use

The driver once stood in a chariot with at least four horses, and fragments of the horses were found with him. An inscription on the limestone base says that Polyzalus, tyrant of Gela in Sicily, dedicated the group to Apollo for a victory in the chariot race at the Pythian Games. The figure survived because a rock-fall buried it, probably in the earthquake of 373 BC, and it was dug up at Delphi in 1896. It is 1.8 metres tall, and the sculptor's name is unknown.
