The path to my fixed purpose is laid with iron rails, whereon my soul is grooved to run. Over unsounded gorges, through the rifled hearts of mountains, under torrents’ beds, unerringly I rush! Naught’s an obstacle, naught’s an angle to the iron way!

Moby-Dick — The path to my fixed purpose (Ch. 37, Sunset)

Herman Melville · 1851

On 1 May 1850 Melville told Richard Henry Dana Jr. in a letter that he was halfway through a book about a whaling voyage. That August he met Nathaniel Hawthorne at a picnic and wrote an unsigned review of Hawthorne's short stories. Some scholars think the friendship, together with his reading of Shakespeare, led him to rewrite a fairly plain whaling adventure into something much larger; others find little concrete evidence for that.
