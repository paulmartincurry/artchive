Thousands of tired, nerve-shaken, over-civilized people are beginning to find out that going to the mountains is going home; that wildness is a necessity; and that mountain parks and reservations are useful not only as fountains of timber and irrigating rivers, but as fountains of life.

Our National Parks (opening)

John Muir · 1901

Muir was elected president of the Sierra Club a week after its first meeting in 1892 and held the post until his death 22 years later. In 1890 Congress had created Yosemite National Park along the lines he proposed in two articles for The Century, though Yosemite Valley itself was left under state control. Writing came hard to him: his friend Henry Fairfield Osborn recorded that he rose at half past four to work, and his secretary said he went over each sentence as many as twenty times.
