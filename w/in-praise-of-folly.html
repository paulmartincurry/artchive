Now, what else is the whole life of man but a sort of play? Actors come on wearing their different masks and all play their parts until the producer orders them off the stage

In Praise of Folly

Desiderius Erasmus · 1509 (printed 1511)

Translated by Betty Radice · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

By Erasmus's own account he wrote this in a week in 1509, while staying at Sir Thomas More's house in Bucklersbury in the City of London. Its Latin title, Moriae Encomium, can also be read as In Praise of More. It was first printed in June 1511, and he went on revising it up to 1532. A copy of the Basel edition of 1515/16 was illustrated in pen and ink by Hans Holbein the Younger. Erasmus later wrote that he almost regretted writing it, such was the trouble it brought him from certain theologians.
