Silly, you men—so very adept
at wrongly faulting womankind,
not seeing you're alone to blame
for faults you plant in woman's mind.

Hombres necios que acusáis

Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz · 1689

Translated by Alan S. Trueblood · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Sor Juana had been a cloistered nun in Mexico City for twenty years by 1689. She had entered the Hieronymite convent in 1669 so that she could study as she wished, and there she built a library of more than 4,000 volumes. The viceroy and vicereine of New Spain were her patrons and had her writings published in Spain. In 1694 she was made to give up her books, and she died the next year after nursing other nuns through a plague.
