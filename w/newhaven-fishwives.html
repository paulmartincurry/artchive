Newhaven Fishwives

David Octavius Hill and Robert Adamson · ca. 1845 · Salted paper print from paper negative · 29.5 × 21.7 cm (11 5/8 × 8 9/16 in.) · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

The fishwives of Newhaven carried fish in creels to Edinburgh to sell. Hill, a painter, and Adamson, an engineer, photographed them with the calotype process during a partnership that produced around 3,000 prints and ended after four years with Adamson's death in 1848. Calotypes faded in sunlight and had to be kept in albums. This print is about thirty centimetres tall.
