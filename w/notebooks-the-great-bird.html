The great bird will take its first flight;-- on the back of his great swan,--filling the universe with wonders; filling all writings with his fame and bringing eternal glory to his birthplace.

Notebooks: The Great Bird

Leonardo da Vinci · c. 1505

Translated by Jean Paul Richter, 1883

Leonardo drew up ideas for flying machines, and relatively few of his designs were built or could have been built in his lifetime. While writing on the flight of birds he set down his earliest memory: a kite came to his cradle and opened his mouth with its tail. The page is now in the Codex Atlanticus. He was about fifty-three when he wrote this passage.
