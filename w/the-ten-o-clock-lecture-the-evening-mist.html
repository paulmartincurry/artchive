And when the evening mist clothes the riverside with poetry, as with a veil, and the poor buildings lose themselves in the dim sky, and the tall chimneys become campanili, and the warehouses are palaces in the night, and the whole city hangs in the heavens, and fairyland is before us

The Ten O'Clock Lecture: The Evening Mist

James McNeill Whistler · 1885

Whistler signed his paintings with a stylized butterfly that had a long stinger for a tail. He gave many of them musical titles: arrangements, harmonies, nocturnes. He had arrived in Paris in 1855 and studied briefly in the studio of Charles Gleyre, who taught him two rules he kept for the rest of his career, that line matters more than colour and that black is the basis of tonal harmony.
