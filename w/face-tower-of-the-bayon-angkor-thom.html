Face Tower of the Bayon, Angkor Thom

Maker unknown · late 12th or early 13th century · Stone

Free to use with credit. Photo: Jakub Hałun, CC BY-SA 4.0

The Bayon was built in the late twelfth or early thirteenth century as the state temple of King Jayavarman VII, at the centre of his capital, Angkor Thom. Its towers carry 216 giant faces. Many scholars have concluded that they are portraits of the king himself; others have theorised that they show Avalokitesvara, the bodhisattva of compassion.
