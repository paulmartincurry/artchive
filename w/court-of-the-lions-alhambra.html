Court of the Lions, Alhambra

Maker unknown · built under Muhammad V, 1362–1391 · Marble, carved stucco and wood · courtyard about 28.7 × 15.6 m

Free to use with credit. Photo: Liberaler Humanist, CC BY-SA 3.0

The sultan Muhammad V had the palace built during his second reign, which began in 1362; the scholar Felix Arnold dates most of it to 1377 to 1390. Its walls carry poems by his vizier Ibn Zamrak, which suggests he was probably involved in the design. The pyramid roof on the eastern pavilion dates from 1934, when Leopoldo Torres Balbás took down a tiled dome added in 1859. A restoration begun in 2002 took the twelve lions off their fountain for a time, and by 2012 the gravel floor had been replaced with Macael marble.
