The Artchive
Men come and go as leaves year by year upon the trees. Those of autumn the wind sheds upon the ground, but when spring returns the forest buds forth with fresh vines. Even so is it with the generations of mankind, the new spring up as the old are passing away.

The Iliad — Men Come and Go as Leaves (Book VI)

Homer · c. 8th century BCE

Translated by Samuel Butler

Glaucus, a Lycian captain fighting for Troy, says this in answer to a challenge from Diomedes. When Diomedes learns that their grandfathers were friends, he plants his spear in the ground and the two exchange armour: his bronze, worth nine oxen, for Glaucus's gold, said to be worth a hundred.
