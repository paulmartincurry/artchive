Our brains ache, in the merciless iced east winds that knife us . . .
Wearied we keep awake because the night is silent . . .
Low drooping flares confuse our memory of the salient . . .
Worried by silence, sentries whisper, curious, nervous,
But nothing happens.

Exposure

Wilfred Owen · 1920

Only five of Owen's poems had been published when he was killed on 4 November 1918. Chatto and Windus brought out Poems in 1920, a slim quarto of 23 poems sold for six shillings, with a sepia photograph of him in uniform. His friend Siegfried Sassoon edited it, with help from Edith Sitwell.
