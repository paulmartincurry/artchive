Then that O-jochū turned around, and dropped her sleeve, and stroked her face with her hand;—and the man saw that she had no eyes or nose or mouth,—and he screamed and ran away.

Mujina

Lafcadio Hearn · 1904

Hearn wrote the introduction to Kwaidan on 20 January 1904, eight months before his death, and said there that most of its stories were translated from old Japanese texts. Mujina is an old Japanese word for the badger, which folklore makes a shape-shifter that deceives people. The faceless ghost in this story is of the kind called a noppera-bō.
