The Death of Actaeon

Titian · c. 1559–1575 · Oil on canvas · National Gallery, London

Free to use

Titian probably began this canvas in 1559, when he told Philip II of Spain in a letter that he had started an Actaeon mauled by hounds. It was never delivered and apparently stayed in his studio until his death in 1576. It later belonged to the Duke of Hamilton and, after his execution in 1649, to Archduke Leopold Wilhelm; David Teniers the Younger painted it hanging in the archduke's gallery in 1651.
