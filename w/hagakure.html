The Way of the Samurai is found in death. When it comes to either/or, there is only the quick choice of death.

Hagakure

Yamamoto Tsunetomo · ca. 1716

Translated by William Scott Wilson · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Yamamoto Tsunetomo never fought in a battle; he was born in 1659, after the end of sanctioned samurai fighting, and worked as a scribe. When his lord died in 1700 he was forbidden to follow him in death, so he became a Buddhist priest and lived in a hermitage in the mountains. There, between 1709 and 1716, he talked with the samurai Tashiro Tsuramoto, who compiled the book. It was largely forgotten for two centuries, and the first modern edition appeared in 1900.
