The Ghost of a Flea

William Blake · ca. 1819–1820 · Tempera and gold on panel · Tate

Free to use

The panel measures 21.4 by 16.2 centimetres. John Varley, a watercolourist and astrologer twenty-one years younger than Blake, held late-night sessions at his house where he tried to summon spirits and Blake drew what appeared; by Varley's account the flea came during one of these in 1819. Blake laid a thin foil of gold-silver alloy under the curtains, the flesh and the stars, and painted the fine detail in powdered gold. Varley bought the picture around 1820. It sold at Sotheby's in 1892 for £10.50.
