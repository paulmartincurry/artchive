The Artchive
As the sweet-apple blushes on the end of the bough, the very end of the bough, which the gatherers overlooked, nay overlooked not but could not reach.

As on the hills the shepherds trample the hyacinth under foot, and the flower darkens on the ground.

As the Sweet-Apple Blushes (fragments 93 and 94)

Sappho of Lesbos · ca. 600 BCE

Translated by Henry Thornton Wharton

The apple lines were quoted by a commentator on Hermogenes to explain Sappho's word for sweet-apple, an apple grafted on a quince. Demetrius quoted the hyacinth lines as an example of a fine closing sentence, and Theodor Bergk, who edited her fragments in Leipzig in 1882, was the first to attribute them to Sappho. Around 360 AD Himerius wrote that it was for Sappho to liken the maiden to an apple.
