For it is not metres, but a metre-making argument that makes a poem,--a thought so passionate and alive that like the spirit of a plant or an animal it has an architecture of its own, and adorns nature with a new thing.

The Poet

Ralph Waldo Emerson · 1844

The Poet opens Essays: Second Series, the book Emerson published in 1844, the year he turned forty-one. He wrote most of his major essays first as lectures and then revised them for print. He had been ordained a Unitarian minister in 1829 and left the ministry in 1832.
