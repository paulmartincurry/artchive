Hippopotamus ("William")

Maker unknown · ca. 1961–1878 BCE · Faience · L. 20 cm; H. 11.2 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

The figurine was dug up in 1910 from a pit in the tomb of the steward Senbi at Meir, and the Metropolitan Museum acquired it in 1917. Three of its legs were probably broken on purpose, to stop the animal harming the dead man in the afterlife; the museum restored them. The name William comes from an article in Punch of 18 March 1931 by Captain H. M. Raleigh, whose family owned a photograph of the hippopotamus.
