I will arise and go now, for always night and day
I hear lake water lapping with low sounds by the shore;
While I stand on the roadway, or on the pavements gray,
I hear it in the deep heart's core.

The Lake Isle of Innisfree

W. B. Yeats · 1890

Yeats wrote the poem in London in 1888, in his early twenties. He was walking down Fleet Street, homesick, when a fountain in a shop window that balanced a little ball on its jet set him remembering lake water. Innisfree is an uninhabited island in Lough Gill, near where he spent his childhood summers. The poem was first printed in the National Observer in 1890. To his annoyance it became one of his most popular.
