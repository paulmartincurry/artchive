Some say the world will end in fire,
some say in ice.

Fire and Ice

Robert Frost · 1920

Harper's Magazine printed this nine-line poem in December 1920, and three years later Frost put it in his collection New Hampshire. The astronomer Harlow Shapley later said that, a year before it appeared, Frost had asked him how the world would end, and he had answered that the sun would either burn the Earth or it would slowly freeze in deep space. One of Frost's biographers traces the poem instead to the traitors frozen in ice in Canto 32 of Dante's Inferno.
