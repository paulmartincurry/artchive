Every form you see has its archetype in the placeless world;
If the form perished, no matter, since its original is everlasting.

Divan-e Shams — Every Form You See

Jalāl al-Dīn Muḥammad Rūmī · 13th century

Translated by R. A. Nicholson, 1898

The collection is named for Shams-e Tabrizi, a wandering dervish whom Rumi met on 15 November 1244, when he was thirty-seven and a jurist and teacher in Konya. On the night of 5 December 1248 Shams was called to the back door and was never seen again. Rumi waited forty days, then put on black. He travelled to Damascus to look for him, and the lyric poems he composed in those years were gathered under his friend's name.
