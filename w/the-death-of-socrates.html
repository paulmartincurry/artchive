The Death of Socrates

Jacques-Louis David · 1787 · Oil on canvas · 51 × 77 1/4 in. (129.5 × 196.2 cm) · The Met

Free to use

The commission came in 1786 not from the crown but from Charles-Michel Trudaine de la Sablière, a rich young man of about twenty. David, then thirty-eight, went back to a composition he had first worked out in 1782 and asked the Hellenist Jean Adry about how Socrates died. He signed the canvas twice: his full name under Crito and his initials under Plato. Trudaine was executed in 1794 during the Reign of Terror, and the Metropolitan Museum bought the painting in 1931.
