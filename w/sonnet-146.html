So shall thou feed on death, that feeds on men,
And death once dead, there’s no more dying then.

Sonnet 146

William Shakespeare · 1609

Shakespeare addresses this sonnet to his own soul. The first printing, in the 1609 Quarto, repeats the last three words of line one at the start of line two, a printing error that breaks the metre. What Shakespeare meant there is still argued over; most modern scholars put in the word feeding, and other guesses run from Thrall to to Pressed by.
