Double-Headed Serpent

Maker unknown · 15th – Early 16th Century · Spanish cedar, pine resin, seashell, hematite, copal, conch, beeswax · British Museum

Free to use with credit. Photo: Geni, CC BY-SA

About 2,000 small flat pieces of turquoise are stuck with pine resin to a single block of cedar, hollowed out at the back. Some of the turquoise came from mines roughly 1,600 kilometres to the northwest. The red is spiny oyster shell and the white teeth are conch; traces of beeswax and resin in the eye holes may once have held inlays. The back was once gilded. Nobody knows how the serpent left Mexico. It measures 20.5 by 43.3 centimetres.
