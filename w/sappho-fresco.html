Sappho Fresco (Portrait of a Young Woman with Stylus)

Maker unknown · c. 50–79 AD · Fresco · National Archaeological Museum, Naples

Free to use

The painting was recovered on 17 May 1760 from a house in the north-west of Pompeii. The name Sappho was attached to it in the nineteenth century without any evidence, and most scholars now treat it as a portrait of an educated, upper-class woman of the town. Her golden hairnet and tight curls were the fashion under Nero. In the house it faced a young man holding a scroll. The Chinese poet Shao Xunmei took up poetry after seeing it in Naples in the early 1920s.
