No man, for any considerable period, can wear one face to himself and another to the multitude, without finally getting bewildered as to which may be the true.

The Scarlet Letter — One face to himself and another to the multitude (Ch. XX)

Nathaniel Hawthorne · 1850

Hawthorne lost his post as surveyor at the Salem Custom House after the election of 1848, and his mother died that July. He wrote the novel in a house at 14 Mall Street, Salem, and gave the last pages to his publisher in February 1850, doubting it would be popular. The first printing of 2,500 copies sold out within ten days. Over fourteen years the book brought him only $1,500.
