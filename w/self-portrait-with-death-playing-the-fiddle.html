Self-Portrait with Death Playing the Fiddle

Arnold Böcklin · 1872 · Oil on canvas · Alte Nationalgalerie, Berlin

Free to use

Böcklin painted this in Munich, after moving back there, and showed it at the Kunstverein München the same year; the showing made his name in the city. He was forty-four or forty-five. The figure of Death behind the sitter follows an anonymous portrait of Sir Brian Tuke from about 1540, now in the Alte Pinakothek in Munich, in which Death points at an hourglass. Alma Mahler later said her husband had this picture in mind while writing the scherzo of his Fourth Symphony.
