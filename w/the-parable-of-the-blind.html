The Parable of the Blind

Pieter Bruegel the Elder · 1568 · Tempera on canvas · Museo di Capodimonte, Naples

Free to use

Bruegel painted this the year before his death, in pigment bound with water-soluble glue on linen, a kind of painting that rarely lasts; it is one of four of his that survive in the medium. Each man has a different eye condition, shown closely enough for later doctors to name them. The village has been identified as Sint-Anna-Pede. The painting turned up in Parma in 1612, when the duke confiscated the property of Count Giovanni Battista Masi for his part in a conspiracy.
