I would prefer not to.

Bartleby, the Scrivener

Herman Melville · 1853

The story ran without Melville's name in two parts, in the November and December 1853 issues of Putnam's Magazine. Its main source was a newspaper advertisement of 18 February that year for a new book, The Lawyer's Story, which printed the whole first chapter, about a lawyer taking on an extra copying clerk. Moby-Dick and Pierre had sold so badly that Melville owed money to his publisher, Harper and Brothers. He was thirty-four.
