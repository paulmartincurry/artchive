In Lo-yang how still it is!
Palaces and houses all burnt to ashes.
Walls and fences all broken and gaping,
Thorns and brambles shooting up to the sky.
I do not see the old old-men:
I only see the new young men.

The Ruins of Lo-yang

Cao Zhi · early 3rd century CE

Translated by Arthur Waley, 1918

Cao Zhi was the third son of the warlord Cao Cao, and according to the Records of the Three Kingdoms he could recite more than ten thousand verses before he turned twenty. In 219 he was too drunk to take up the command of a relief force his father had given him. After his brother Cao Pi became emperor he was kept away from the capital and out of politics. More than 90 of his poems survive.
