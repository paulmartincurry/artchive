Do not be afraid of it, do not be bewildered. This is the natural radiance of your own dharmata, therefore recognise it.

Bardo Thödol (The Tibetan Book of the Dead)

Karma Lingpa / Padmasambhava Tradition · 8th–14th century CE

Translated by Francesca Fremantle and Chögyam Trungpa · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Tibetan tradition holds that Padmasambhava composed this text in the eighth century, that his student Yeshe Tsogyal wrote it down, and that it was buried in the Gampo hills of central Tibet. Karma Lingpa is said to have found it on Mount Gampodar when he was fifteen years old. The English title is not a translation of the Tibetan one. It comes from Walter Evans-Wentz's edition of 1927, which was named after the Egyptian Book of the Dead.
