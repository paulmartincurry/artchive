I will not cease from mental fight,
Nor shall my sword sleep in my hand,
Till we have built Jerusalem
In England's green and pleasant land.

And Did Those Feet in Ancient Time (Preface to Milton)

William Blake · c. 1804–1810

In 1788 Blake worked out a way of printing words and pictures from one copper plate, and he used it for most of his books afterwards. He wrote the text backwards on the copper in an acid-resistant varnish, then let nitric acid eat away the rest so that the letters and the design stood up in relief. He mixed his own pigments with linseed oil. His wife Catherine, who had signed their marriage contract with an X, did engraving and colouring with him.
