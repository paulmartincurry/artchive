There ought to be behind the door of every happy, contented man some one standing with a hammer continually reminding him with a tap that there are unhappy people

Gooseberries — The man with a hammer

Anton Chekhov · 1898

Translated by Constance Garnett

Gooseberries dates from 1898, when Chekhov was thirty-eight. He had renounced the theatre two years earlier after the reception of The Seagull, and in 1898 Stanislavski's Moscow Art Theatre revived the play to acclaim. He had first coughed blood in 1884, the year he qualified as a physician, and when the attacks worsened in 1886 he would not admit his tuberculosis to his family or friends.
