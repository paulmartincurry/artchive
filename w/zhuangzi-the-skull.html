At this, the skull opened its eyes wide and knitted its brows and said, "How should I cast aside happiness greater than that of a king, and mingle once again in the toils and troubles of mortality?"

Zhuangzi — The Skull

Zhuangzi (Chuang Tzu) · 4th–3rd century BC

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, 1889

The Zhuangzi was written in the late Warring States period, between 476 and 221 BC, and stands with the Tao Te Ching as one of the two founding texts of Taoism. It is made up largely of anecdotes, parables and fables, often told with irreverence or humour. The first known commentary on it was written in the Han dynasty.
