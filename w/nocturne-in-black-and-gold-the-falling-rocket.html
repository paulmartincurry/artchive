Nocturne in Black and Gold – The Falling Rocket

James McNeill Whistler · 1875 · Oil on panel · Detroit Institute of Arts

Free to use

The subject is a fireworks display at Cremorne Gardens, a pleasure resort in London. After the panel was shown at the Grosvenor Gallery in 1877, the critic John Ruskin attacked it in print, and Whistler sued him for libel. At the trial, on 25 and 26 November 1878, the painting was shown to the court upside down by accident. Whistler won, and was awarded a farthing. The court costs left him bankrupt, and he pawned, sold and mortgaged what he could.
