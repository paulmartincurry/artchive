Bayeux Tapestry

Maker unknown · ca. 1070 · Linen · Bayeux Tapestry Museum

Free to use

It is an embroidery, not a woven tapestry: wool thread stitched onto coarse linen in four stitches and ten natural dye colours. The cloth is nearly 70 metres long and 50 centimetres tall, and its final scenes are missing. The earliest written record of it is an inventory of Bayeux Cathedral from 1476. In 1792 it was confiscated to cover military wagons, and a local lawyer pulled it off a wagon and kept it in his house.
