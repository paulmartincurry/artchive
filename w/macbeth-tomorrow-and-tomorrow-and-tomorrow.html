A tale told by an idiot,
full of sound and fury,
signifying nothing.

Macbeth — Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow (Act V, Scene V)

William Shakespeare · 1606

Macbeth is the shortest of Shakespeare's tragedies. The story came from Holinshed's Chronicles of 1587, and the play was not printed until the Folio of 1623, possibly from a prompt book. When David Garrick revived it in 1744 he advertised it as written by Shakespeare, but he cut more than a tenth of the text and wrote a lengthy death speech of his own for Macbeth.
