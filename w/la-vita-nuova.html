My lady looks so gentle and so pure
When yielding salutation by the way,
That the tongue trembles and has nought to say,
And the eyes, which fain would see, may not endure.

La Vita Nuova (My lady looks so gentle and so pure)

Dante Alighieri · c. 1294

Translated by Dante Gabriel Rossetti

Dante called this his libello, his little book. It gathers poems he wrote over roughly ten years, from before 1283 to about 1293, and joins them with prose: 25 sonnets, one ballata and four canzoni, one of them left unfinished at the death of Beatrice Portinari. He wrote it in the Tuscan vernacular and not in Latin, and nowhere in it does he give his own name.
