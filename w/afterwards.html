The Artchive
When the Present has latched its postern behind my tremulous stay,
And the May month flaps its glad green leaves like wings,
Delicate-filmed as new-spun silk, will the neighbours say,
“He was a man who used to notice such things”?

Afterwards

Thomas Hardy · 1917

Hardy turned seventy-seven in the year of this poem. He was living at Max Gate in Dorchester, a house he had designed himself and his brother had built, and three years earlier had married his secretary, Florence Dugdale, who was thirty-nine years younger. He died at Max Gate on 11 January 1928. He had wished to be buried at Stinsford church, his executor insisted on Westminster Abbey, and in the end his heart went to Stinsford and his ashes to Poets' Corner.
