The best-laid schemes o’ mice an ’men
Gang aft agley,
An’lea’e us nought but grief an’ pain,
For promis’d joy!

To a Mouse

Robert Burns · 1785

The story is that Burns was ploughing at Mossgiel Farm in November 1785 when he broke open a mouse's nest. His brother Gilbert said the poem was composed while he still held the plough. Burns was twenty-six, a tenant farmer's son who had been the principal labourer on his father's farm by the age of fifteen. The poem went into the Kilmarnock edition of his poems and every edition after it.
