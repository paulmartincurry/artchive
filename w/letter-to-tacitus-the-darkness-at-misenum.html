You might hear the shrieks of women, the screams of children, and the shouts of men; … some lifting their hands to the gods; but the greater part convinced that there were now no gods at all, and that the final endless night of which we have heard had come upon the world.

Letter to Tacitus: The Darkness at Misenum (Letters VI.20)

Pliny the Younger · c. 103–107 CE (on the eruption of 79 CE)

Translated by William Melmoth, revised by F. C. T. Bosanquet

Pliny wrote two letters on the eruption of Vesuvius in 79 to the historian Tacitus, a close friend, who had asked him for a detailed account of his uncle's death to include in his own historical work. This is from the second. Books 4 to 7 of the letters, it is suggested, were composed between 103 and 107.
