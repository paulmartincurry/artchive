Through the fence, between the curling flower spaces, I could see them hitting. They were coming toward where the flag was and I went along the fence.

The Sound and the Fury — Through the fence (April Seventh, 1928, opening)

William Faulkner · 1929

Faulkner knew a mentally disabled man in Oxford, Mississippi, named Edwin Chandler, who was known for running behind a fence. He first planned the book as a short story about children sent away from their grandmother's funeral. The editor, Ben Wasson, at first took the italics out of this opening chapter, meaning to mark its shifts in time with line breaks, and Faulkner wrote demanding that he put them back. He was still trying out its punctuation at the galley proofs.
