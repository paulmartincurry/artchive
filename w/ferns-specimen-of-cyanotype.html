Ferns. Specimen of Cyanotype

Anna Atkins · 1840s · Cyanotype · 26.3 × 20.8 cm · National Gallery of Art, Washington

Free to use

No camera was used. Anna Atkins laid dried specimens directly on paper coated for the cyanotype process, which her friend Sir John Herschel had invented in 1842, and exposed them to the sun. She had learned the method of placing an object on light-sensitive paper from William Henry Fox Talbot himself. The labels Ferns and Specimen of Cyanotype are printed in the lower corners of the sheet.
