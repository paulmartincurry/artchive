The Entombment of Christ

Caravaggio · 1603–1604 · Oil on canvas · Pinacoteca Vaticana

Free to use

The canvas is three metres high and was made for the Vittrice family chapel in the Chiesa Nuova in Rome. Girolamo Vittrice paid for it; his wife was the sister of Caravaggio's friend Prospero Orsi. A document of 1 September 1604 calls the picture new. Nicodemus, the man holding Christ's knees, has the face of Michelangelo, taken from the sculptor's self-portrait in his Florentine Pietà. The painting was carried off to Paris in 1797 and has been in the Vatican since 1816. A copy hangs in the chapel.
