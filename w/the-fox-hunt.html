The Fox Hunt

Winslow Homer · 1893 · Oil on canvas · Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts

Free to use

Homer painted this in his studio at Prouts Neck, Maine, in the winter of 1893, working from a dead fox and dead crows that local hunters supplied. He posed the fox in the snow with string and sticks, and the crows thawed and went limp as the weather warmed. When the local stationmaster told him the birds were wrong, he painted them out, spent three days at the station scattering corn and sketching live crows on telegraph blanks, and repainted them. It is his largest single work. The Pennsylvania Academy bought it from him in 1894.
