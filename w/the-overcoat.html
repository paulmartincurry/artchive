“Let me be. Why do you offend me?”—and in these penetrating words rang other words: “I am your brother.”

The Overcoat (The Mantle)

Nikolai Gogol · 1842

Translated by Richard Pevear and Larissa Volokhonsky · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

The story is written in skaz, a colloquial Russian that comes from oral storytelling and is hard to translate. Gogol grew up on a family estate in Ukraine in a household that spoke Ukrainian and Russian and read Polish, and his father wrote plays for his own theatre. On the night of 24 February 1852 Gogol burned manuscripts holding most of the second part of his novel Dead Souls. He then refused food and died nine days later, aged forty-two.
