But I am not tragically colored. There is no great sorrow dammed up in my soul, nor lurking behind my eyes.

How It Feels to Be Colored Me

Zora Neale Hurston · 1928

The essay appeared in The World Tomorrow in 1928. Hurston grew up in Eatonville, Florida, chartered in 1886 as the first all-Black incorporated town in the United States, where her father was a pastor and later mayor. After her mother died in 1904 she was sent to live with relatives in Jacksonville. She had moved to Harlem in 1925.
