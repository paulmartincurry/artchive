It was a spring without voices.

Silent Spring

Rachel Carson · 1962

Short quotation from a work still in copyright, credited to the author. Not public domain; kept to one line on purpose.

The impetus for the book was a letter written in January 1958 by Carson's friend Olga Owens Huckins to The Boston Herald, describing the death of birds around her property in Duxbury, Massachusetts, after the aerial spraying of DDT. The New Yorker commissioned a long article on the topic from Carson. The book was published on September 27, 1962.
