Pauline Bonaparte as Venus Victrix

Antonio Canova · 1805–08 · White marble · Galleria Borghese, Rome

Free to use with credit. Photo: Architas, CC BY-SA 4.0

Camillo Borghese commissioned this portrait of his wife, Pauline Bonaparte, and Canova carved it in Rome from 1805 to 1808. The wooden base once held a mechanism that turned the sculpture, and the marble surface was waxed. The sculpture went to Camillo's house in Turin and then to Genoa, and reached the Galleria Borghese only around 1838. Asked how she could pose wearing so little, she reputedly replied that a stove in the studio kept her warm.
