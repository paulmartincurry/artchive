Shakespeare's ethereal Ariel symbolizes the noble, soaring aspect of the human spirit. He represents the superiority of reason and feeling over the base impulses of irrationality.

Ariel

José Enrique Rodó · 1900

Translated by Margaret Sayers Peden · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Rodó was twenty-eight or twenty-nine when he published this essay in 1900, in the aftermath of the Spanish-American War. He wrote it as a secular sermon to the young people of Latin America, spoken by a teacher named Prospero, and took the names from Shakespeare's The Tempest. He kept up a correspondence with Rubén Darío and other Spanish-language writers of the day. He died in Palermo, Italy, in 1917, aged forty-five, and one of the main parks in Montevideo, the city of his birth, is named after him.
