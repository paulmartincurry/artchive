Pergamon Altar (Gigantomachy Frieze: Athena and Alkyoneus)

Maker unknown · first half of the 2nd century BC · Marble · Frieze slabs H. 2.3 m · Pergamon Museum, Berlin

Free to use with credit. Photo: Miia Ranta, CC BY-SA 2.0

The altar was built at Pergamon in Asia Minor in the first half of the 2nd century BC, in the reign of Eumenes II. Its large frieze of gods fighting giants is 113 metres long, on slabs 2.3 metres high, cut from grey-veined marble from the island of Marmara. The German engineer Carl Humann began official excavations in 1878 and worked until 1886, and the relief panels were then taken to Berlin.
