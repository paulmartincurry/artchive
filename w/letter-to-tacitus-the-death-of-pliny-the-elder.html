As soon as it was light again, which was not till the third day after this melancholy accident, his body was found entire, and without any marks of violence upon it, in the dress in which he fell, and looking more like a man asleep than dead.

Letter to Tacitus: The Death of Pliny the Elder (Letters VI.16)

Pliny the Younger · c. 103–107 CE (on the eruption of 79 CE)

Translated by William Melmoth, revised by F. C. T. Bosanquet

Pliny the Younger was 17 or 18 when Vesuvius erupted in 79 AD and his uncle, Pliny the Elder, died attempting to rescue victims. He saw the eruption from the town of Misenum, 29 km from the volcano across the Bay of Naples. The terms of his uncle's will passed the estate to him.
