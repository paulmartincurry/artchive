Coatlicue

Maker unknown · ca. 1439–1491 · Andesite · National Museum of Anthropology, Mexico City

Free to use with credit. Photo: Luidger, CC BY-SA 3.0

The statue stands 2.52 metres tall and is carved on every side, including the base, which would always have been hidden from view. It was buried after the Spanish conquest of Tenochtitlan in 1521 and found on 13 August 1790 by workers digging a water canal in the main plaza of Mexico City. The clergy had it buried again because people began to worship it. It was dug up in 1803 so that Alexander von Humboldt could draw it, reburied, and brought out for the last time in 1823.
