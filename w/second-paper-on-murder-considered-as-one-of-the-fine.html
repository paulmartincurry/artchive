For if once a man indulges himself in murder, very soon he comes to think little of robbing; and from robbing he comes next to drinking and Sabbath-breaking, and from that to incivility and procrastination.

Second Paper on Murder Considered as One of the Fine Arts — Incivility and procrastination

Thomas De Quincey · 1839

This comes from the second of De Quincey's papers on murder, which Blackwood's Magazine ran in November 1839, twelve years after the first. In between, William Blackwood had turned down a sequel. As editor of The Westmorland Gazette in 1818, De Quincey had made a point of widening the paper's coverage to take in murder trials. He planned a larger work, Confessions of a Murderer, and never finished it.
