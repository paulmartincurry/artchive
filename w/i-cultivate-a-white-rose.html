I have a white rose to tend
In July as in January;

I Cultivate a White Rose (Versos Sencillos XXXIX)

José Martí · 1891

Translated by Manuel A. Tellechea · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

At sixteen Martí was arrested in Havana over a letter he and a friend had written reproaching a schoolmate who joined the Spanish army. He was condemned to six years in prison, and the chains cut his legs so badly that he was moved and then deported to Spain in 1871. He took a law degree there in 1874 and was kept from returning to Cuba. The book this poem comes from appeared twenty years after his deportation.
