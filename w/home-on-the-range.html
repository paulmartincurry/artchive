Oh, give me a home where the buffalo roam,
Where the deer and the antelope play,
Where seldom is heard a discouraging word
And the skies are not cloudy all day.

Home on the Range

Traditional, words after Brewster M. Higley · 1872–73

Brewster M. Higley, a doctor who had taken up land in Smith County, Kansas, wrote the words as a poem, My Western Home, in 1872 or 1873, and his friend Daniel E. Kelley gave it a melody. Higley's poem did not have the words on the range; ranchers and cowboys changed it as they carried it around. John Lomax printed it in 1910 in Cowboy Songs and Other Frontier Ballads, and Kansas made it the state song on 30 June 1947.
