Come here, thou worthy of a world of praise,
That dost so high the Grecian glory raise,
Ulysses! stay thy ship, and that song hear
That none pass’d ever but it bent his ear,
But left him ravish’d, and instructed more
By us, than any ever heard before.

The Odyssey — Come here, thou worthy of a world of praise (Book XII, Chapman's translation)

Homer · c. 8th century BCE

Translated by George Chapman

Chapman's Homer was, until Alexander Pope's, the most popular in the English language. John Keats wrote of his admiration for it in the poem On First Looking into Chapman's Homer.
