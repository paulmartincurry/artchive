Ah Love! could thou and I with Fate conspire
To grasp this sorry Scheme of Things entire,
Would not we shatter it to bits—and then
Re-mould it nearer to the Heart's Desire!

Rubáiyát (Ah Love! could thou and I)

Omar Khayyam (Trans. Edward FitzGerald) · 11th–12th Century CE (1859 First Edition)

Omar Khayyam worked as a mathematician and astronomer. From 1074 he ran an observatory at Isfahan for the sultan Malik-Shah, and by 1079 he and his colleagues had measured the length of the year to within a fraction of a second, for a reform of the Persian calendar. The quatrains are his by tradition only; his standing as a poet came late. His name means tent-maker in Arabic, which may have been the family trade.
