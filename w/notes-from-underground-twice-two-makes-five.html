I admit that twice two makes four is an excellent thing, but if we are to give everything its due, twice two makes five is sometimes a very charming thing too.

Notes from Underground — Twice Two Makes Five (Part I, Ch. IX)

Fyodor Dostoevsky · 1864

Translated by Constance Garnett

Dostoevsky first published this novella in 1864 in the journal Epoch, where he had announced it under the title A Confession. Its unnamed narrator is a retired civil servant living in St. Petersburg. The book attacks the Russian philosophy of its day, especially Nikolay Chernyshevsky's What Is to Be Done?
