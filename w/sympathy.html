I know why the caged bird sings, ah me,
When his wing is bruised and his bosom sore,--
When he beats his bars and he would be free;

Sympathy

Paul Laurence Dunbar · 1899

Since 30 September 1897 Paul Laurence Dunbar had worked as an attendant at the Library of Congress, a job that strained his health. He was twenty-seven when he wrote this poem there. His wife wrote in 1914 that the iron gratings of the book stacks, heated by the summer sun, had suggested the bars of the cage. The poem was published in 1899 in Lyrics of the Hearthside; he had printed a different poem under the same title in 1893. He died of tuberculosis at thirty-three.
