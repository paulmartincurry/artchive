Yet each man kills the thing he loves,
By each let this be heard,
Some do it with a bitter look,

The Ballad of Reading Gaol

Oscar Wilde (signed C.3.3.) · 1898

C.3.3. was Wilde's cell at Reading: block C, third landing, third cell. On 7 July 1896 a trooper of the Royal Horse Guards was hanged there for killing his wife, the first execution at the prison in eighteen years. Wilde began the poem in France after his release in May 1897 and finished it in Naples, living on three pounds a week from his wife. The first edition of 800 copies, in February 1898, sold out within a week. His name did not appear on the book until the seventh printing, in June 1899.
