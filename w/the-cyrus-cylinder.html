My vast troops were marching peaceably in Babylon, and the whole of [Sumer] and Akkad had nothing to fear.

The Cyrus Cylinder

Achaemenid–Babylonian Chancery Scribes · 539 BCE

Translated by Irving Finkel, British Museum · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

The text is written in Akkadian cuneiform on a barrel of baked clay 22.5 cm long, built up in layers around a clay core. It was buried in the foundations of the main temple of Babylon. Hormuzd Rassam dug it up in March 1879 on an excavation for the British Museum. It came out of the ground in pieces. A smaller fragment went to Yale through an antiquities dealer, was identified as part of the cylinder in 1970, and was rejoined to it in 1972. Forty-five lines survive.
