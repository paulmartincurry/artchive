She turned away with looks fixed fast on the ground, stirred no more in countenance by the speech he essays than if she stood in iron flint or Marpesian stone. At length she started, and fled wrathfully into the shadowy woodland, where Sychaeus, her ancient husband, responds to her distresses and equals her affection.

The Aeneid — Dido Among the Shades (Book VI)

Virgil · 29–19 BCE

Translated by J. W. Mackail

In the account that survives from Virgil's contemporary Trogus, Dido's husband was her uncle Acerbas, a priest second in power to the king of Tyre, and her brother Pygmalion had him murdered for his gold. She is known only from Greek and Roman sources written well after Carthage was founded, so whether she lived is uncertain. Timaeus put the founding in 814 BC.
