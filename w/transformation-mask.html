Transformation Mask

Maker unknown · date not recorded · Carved wood · Peabody Museum of Archaeology and Ethnology, Harvard University

Free to use with credit. Photo: Myrabella, CC BY-SA 4.0

A mask of this kind is worn in ritual dances on the Northwest Coast of North America. The dancer pulls a string and the outer animal face opens to show a human face carved inside. The masks were made by master carvers and worn at potlatches, the ceremonies held for a naming, a new chief, a marriage or a death. The Canadian government banned the potlatch in 1884, and the ban lasted until 1969.
