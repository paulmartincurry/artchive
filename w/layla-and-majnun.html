The Artchive
Sad inmate of the desert wild,
His form and face with dust defiled;

Layla and Majnun

Nizami Ganjavi · 1188 CE

Translated by James Atkinson, 1836

The story of the two lovers came out of Arabia, and had long circulated as short, loosely connected anecdotes. Nizami Ganjavi gathered those sources into a single narrative poem in 1188, the third of his five long poems. In it he mentions that his only son, Mohammad, was then fourteen. One survey counts fifty-nine later imitations of the poem in the Iranian world, more than of any other Persian romance.
