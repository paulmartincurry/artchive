Jupiter's Southern Aurora in Infrared

Juno, NASA/JPL-Caltech/SwRI/ASI/INAF/JIRAM · 27 August 2016 · Infrared mosaic of three images (JIRAM)

Free to use with credit. Photo: NASA/JPL-Caltech/SwRI/ASI/INAF/JIRAM

Jupiter's southern aurora can hardly be seen from Earth, and Juno's polar orbit gave the first close look at it. The camera recorded light at 3.3 to 3.6 microns, the wavelengths given off by excited hydrogen ions over the poles. The view is a mosaic of three images taken minutes apart, about four hours after the spacecraft's closest pass.
