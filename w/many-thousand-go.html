No more peck o’ corn for me, No more, no more;
No more peck o’ corn for me, Many tousand go.

Many Thousand Go

Traditional African American song · printed 1867

Slave Songs of the United States printed it in 1867 with a note from Colonel Higginson: it was among the newest of the songs, made up by nobody knew whom and sung in secret. The peck of corn and pint of salt were slavery's rations. Colonel Trowbridge learned that it was first sung when Beauregard took the slaves of the islands to build the fortifications at Hilton Head and Bay Point.
