So the divine light of contemplation, when it beats on the soul, not yet perfectly enlightened, causes spiritual darkness, because it not only surpasses its strength, but because it blinds it and deprives it of its natural perceptions.

The Dark Night — Why the Divine Light Is Called Night (Book II, ch. 5)

Saint John of the Cross (San Juan de la Cruz) · 1584–1586; English 1916

Translated by David Lewis, rev. Benedict Zimmerman

John of the Cross wrote Dark Night between 1584 and 1586 as a line-by-line commentary on his own poem, eight stanzas of five lines that he never gave a title. He left the commentary unfinished. The poem itself was probably written between 1577 and 1579, perhaps while he was imprisoned in Toledo, though the evidence for that is second-hand.
