Hagia Sophia, Interior

Isidore of Miletus and Anthemius of Tralles · 532–537 · Ashlar and Roman brick · 82 × 73 m; H. 55 m

Free to use with credit. Photo: Diego Delso, CC BY-SA 4.0

The emperor Justinian I had the church built between 532 and 537, after the one before it on the site was destroyed in the Nika riots. Its designers, Isidore of Miletus and Anthemius of Tralles, were geometers. When finished it was the largest interior space in the world. It became a mosque after the fall of Constantinople in 1453, a museum in 1935, and a mosque again in 2020.
