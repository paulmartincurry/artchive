Frankenstein — The creature opens its eyes (Ch. 5)

Mary Shelley · 1818

In the cold, wet summer of 1816 Mary Shelley, aged eighteen, was visiting Lord Byron at his villa by Lake Geneva when he proposed that each of the company write a ghost story. For days she could not think of one. The idea came to her after midnight, when she could not sleep. She began it as a short story and wrote much of the book later that year in a lodging house in the centre of Bath.
