Like men we'll face the murderous, cowardly pack,
pressed to the wall, dying, but fighting back.

If We Must Die

Claude McKay · July 1919

McKay was working for the Pennsylvania Railroad in 1919 and saw the mob violence of that summer himself. The sonnet first ran in the July 1919 issue of The Liberator, whose publisher, Max Eastman, was his friend. Frank Harris had wanted it for Pearson's Magazine and was angry not to get it. Before the year was out it had been reprinted in The Messenger and, in London, in the Workers' Dreadnought.
