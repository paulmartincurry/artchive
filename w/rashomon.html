Under the broad gate there was no one else, just a single cricket clinging to a huge red pillar from which the lacquer was peeling here and there.

Rashōmon

Ryūnosuke Akutagawa · November 1915

Translated by Jay Rubin · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Akutagawa was twenty-three and still a student of English literature at Tokyo Imperial University when this story, his second, came out in the magazine Teikoku Bungaku in 1915. He based it on a twelfth-century tale. His friends criticised it heavily. Akira Kurosawa's film of 1950 took its title and framing story from it, and its plot and characters from another Akutagawa story, In a Grove.
