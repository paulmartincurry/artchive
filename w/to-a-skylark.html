We look before and after,
And pine for what is not:
Our sincerest laughter
With some pain is fraught;
Our sweetest songs are those that tell of saddest thought.

To a Skylark (closing stanzas)

Percy Bysshe Shelley · 1820

Shelley finished the poem in late June 1820. He and his wife Mary had heard the bird on an evening walk through the lanes near Livorno, where they were staying for a week or two. It runs to 105 lines in twenty-one stanzas, each of four short lines and one long one. Thomas Hardy travelled to Livorno and wrote a poem about the same bird in 1887, and Noël Coward took the title of his 1941 play Blithe Spirit from the opening line.
