When lilacs last in the dooryard bloom’d,
And the great star early droop’d in the western sky in the night,
I mourn’d, and yet shall mourn with ever-returning spring.

When Lilacs Last in the Dooryard Bloom'd (sections 1–3)

Walt Whitman · 1865

Whitman wrote this elegy for Abraham Lincoln in the summer of 1865, after the president was shot on 14 April. He was at his mother's home when he heard the news, and when he stepped outside, the lilacs in the yard were blooming. The poem runs to 206 lines and never names Lincoln.
