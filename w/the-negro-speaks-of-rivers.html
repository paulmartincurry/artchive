I’ve known rivers ancient as the world and older than the flow of
human blood in human veins.

My soul has grown deep like the rivers.

The Negro Speaks of Rivers

Langston Hughes · 1921

By Langston Hughes's own account this poem took ten or fifteen minutes and was written on the back of an envelope, in 1920, as he crossed the Mississippi on his way to visit his father in Mexico. Jessie Redmon Fauset, literary editor of The Crisis, took it to W. E. B. Du Bois, and the magazine printed it in June 1921. It was reprinted at least eleven times within ten years. His ashes are interred in Harlem beneath a cosmogram titled Rivers.
