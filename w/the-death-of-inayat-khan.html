The Death of Inayat Khan

Balchand · October 1618 · Opaque watercolor on paper · Bodleian Libraries, Oxford (MS. Ouseley Add. 171b, fol. 4r)

Free to use

In his memoirs Jahangir called Inayat Khan one of his closest servants and described him as addicted to opium and wine. When the dying man was carried before him in a palanquin, the emperor was so struck by his wasted state that he ordered the court artists to record his likeness; Inayat Khan died the next day, 10 October 1618. Balchand wrote his name on the fold of the sheet over the bedpost, and the preliminary drawing survives in the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston.
