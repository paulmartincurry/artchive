It is with life as it is with a play, — it matters not how long the action is spun out, but how good the acting is. It makes no difference at what point you stop. Stop whenever you choose; only see to it that the closing period is well turned.

Moral Letters to Lucilius — It Is with Life as It Is with a Play (Letter 77)

Seneca the Younger · c. 63–65 CE

Translated by Richard M. Gummere

In AD 41 Seneca was exiled to Corsica under the emperor Claudius; he was allowed back in 49 to tutor Nero, and became his adviser when Nero took the throne in 54. His influence declined with time. In one of the early letters he alludes to his retirement from public life.
