But O for the touch of a vanish’d hand,
And the sound of a voice that is still!

Break, Break, Break

Alfred, Lord Tennyson · 1842

These sixteen lines were written early in 1835 and not published until 1842. Tennyson was twenty-five and staying at Mablethorpe on the Lincolnshire coast, where his family had spent summers when he was a boy. The poem is an elegy for Arthur Hallam, who had been one of his closest friends at Cambridge and died of a stroke at twenty-two.
