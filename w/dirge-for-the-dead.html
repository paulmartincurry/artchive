The sea shall rise in waves,
Surround on every side,
But yet that awful bridge,
No thicker than a thread,
Shall stand both firm and strong.

Dirge for the Dead

Badaga tradition (Nilgiri Hills) · printed 1871

Translated by Charles E. Gover

The Badagas of the Nilgiri hills chanted it at a funeral. A leading man laid his hand on the head of a calf without blemish and confessed the dead man's sins, by custom thirteen hundred, the people answering each one; the calf was then let loose and never used for work. This is the closing part. Charles E. Gover printed it in 1871, made from a literal prose version given him by the missionary F. Metz.
