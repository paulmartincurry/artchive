A tent is my house,
Of felt are my walls;
Raw flesh my food
With mare’s milk to drink.
Always thinking of my own country,
My heart sad within.
Would I were a yellow stork
And could fly to my old home!

Lament of Hsi-chün

Liu Xijun (Princess Xijun) · ca. 105 BC

Translated by Arthur Waley, 1918

Liu Xijun was orphaned as an infant. In 105 BC Emperor Wu made her a princess so that she could marry the elderly king of the Wusun, 5,000 miles away in the Ili valley, after the Wusun had sent 1,000 horses to the Han court. She rarely saw her husband and could not speak with him. The poem, believed to be hers, is one of the earliest known Chinese poems attributable to a named woman.
