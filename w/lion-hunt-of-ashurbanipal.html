Lion Hunt of Ashurbanipal

Maker unknown · ca. 645–635 BCE · Gypsum alabaster · British Museum

Free to use with credit. Photo: Carole Raddato, CC BY-SA 2.0

The slabs lined narrow passageways in Ashurbanipal's North Palace at Nineveh, and some had fallen from an upper floor by the time they were dug out. They were probably painted. The hunt they show took place in an arena walled by soldiers' shields, where captured lions were let out of wooden crates for the king to kill. Late in the carving, the tails of nearly all the lions in the tall single-register scenes were shortened. Nineveh was sacked and burnt in 612 BC, perhaps as little as twenty-five years after the reliefs were made.
