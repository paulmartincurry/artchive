The name of wife may seem more sacred or more binding, but sweeter for me will always be the word friend [amica], or, if you will permit me, that of concubine or whore.

First Letter to Abelard

Heloise · c. 1132

Translated by Betty Radice, revised by M. T. Clanchy · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Heloise had been the ward of her uncle Fulbert, a canon of Notre Dame, and was later abbess of the Paraclete near Troyes. She wrote to Abelard after reading the account of their story he had sent to a friend, the Historia Calamitatum. Abelard himself repeats her arguments against marriage there.
