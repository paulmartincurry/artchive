Do not weep, maiden, for war is kind.
Because your lover threw wild hands toward the sky
And the affrighted steed ran on alone,
Do not weep.
War is kind.

War Is Kind (Do not weep, maiden)

Stephen Crane · 1899

War Is Kind, Crane's second collection of poems, was published in the United States in May 1899. Most of his poems have no stanzas or refrains, and this is one of the few that do. By then he had covered two wars as a correspondent, in Greece and in Cuba, where he went ashore with the Marines at Guantánamo Bay. He was writing at Brede Place in Sussex, a manor from the 14th century with neither electricity nor indoor plumbing.
