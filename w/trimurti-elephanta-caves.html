Trimurti, Elephanta Caves

Maker unknown · Mid-6th Century · Basalt rock

Free to use with credit. Photo: Christian Haugen, CC BY 2.0

The three-faced Shiva is cut from the solid basalt of the island, inside a cave temple that runs 39 metres into the hill. Scholars now generally place the temple's completion in the second quarter of the sixth century and attribute it to King Krishnaraja of the Kalachuri dynasty. The Portuguese, who were ceded the island in 1534, named it for a large stone elephant that stood where they docked their boats. Worship in the main cave stopped when they arrived. The Government of India restored the cave in the late 1970s.
