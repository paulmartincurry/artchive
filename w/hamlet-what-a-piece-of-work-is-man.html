What a piece of work is man, how noble in reason, how infinite in faculties, in form and moving, how express and admirable; in action how like an angel, in apprehension, how like a god: the beauty of the world, the paragon of animals. And yet, to me, what is this quintessence of dust?

Hamlet — What a Piece of Work Is Man (Act II, Scene II)

William Shakespeare · 1601

Hamlet says this to Rosencrantz and Guildenstern in prose, not the blank verse Shakespeare usually wrote for his plays. The speech is missing entirely from the First Quarto of 1603. It is in the Second Quarto of 1604, punctuated differently from the 1623 First Folio.
