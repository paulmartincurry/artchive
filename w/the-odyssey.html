Scylla to starboard, dreaded Charybdis off to port,
her horrible whirlpool gulping the sea-surge down, down

The Odyssey (Book XII)

Homer · ca. 700 BCE

Translated by Robert Fagles, 1996 · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Nobody knows who Homer was. Most ancient lives describe him as blind and most place his death on the island of Ios, while cities from Chios and Smyrna to Argos and Athens were named as his birthplace. Scholars now generally regard the Iliad and the Odyssey as the work of separate authors. In the early twentieth century Milman Parry and Albert Lord showed that the poems carry the marks of oral poetry, a method that lets even a poet who cannot read improvise at great length.
