Arise, O prophet, and listen, and guide.
Be thou filled with my will
And going over land and sea
Fire with the word the hearts of men!

The Prophet (Prorok)

Alexander Pushkin · 1826

Translated by Ivan Panin, 1888

The poem dates from 1826, the year Pushkin's exile ended. He had spent two years confined to his mother's estate at Mikhailovskoye, near Pskov, and was summoned to Moscow after one of his poems turned up among the papers of the Decembrist rebels. A meeting with Emperor Nicholas I won his release. From then on the emperor kept strict control of everything he published and he could not travel at will.
