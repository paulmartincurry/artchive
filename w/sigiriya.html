Sigiriya

Maker unknown · 477–495

Free to use with credit. Photo: Wrobell, CC BY-SA 3.0

King Kashyapa seized the throne in 477, moved his capital to the top of this granite rock, about 180 metres high, and had its sides painted. The paintings are thought to have covered a stretch of the western face 140 metres long and 40 high; most are lost. He died in 495, and the site then became a Buddhist monastery. Visitors wrote more than 1,500 poems on a plaster wall there, most of them in the ninth and tenth centuries and most addressed to the painted women. In 1967 paint was splashed on the frescoes, and the repair took until April 1968.
