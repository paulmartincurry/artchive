The Lanzón

Maker unknown · ca. 900–500 BCE · Granite

Free to use with credit. Photo: CyArk, CC BY-SA 3.0

The Lanzón still stands where it was set up around 500 BCE, in the central chamber of a maze of underground passages in the Old Temple of Chavín de Huantar, in the central highlands of Peru. It runs from floor to ceiling and is carved on all three of its sides, so the whole design can only be seen by walking around it. The people of Chavín brought it offerings of food and ceramics. Its name is the Spanish word for a lance, though its shape is closer to a highland plow.
