Stony the road we trod,
Bitter the chast’ning rod,
Felt in the days when hope unborn had died;

Lift Every Voice and Sing

James Weldon Johnson · February 12, 1900

James Weldon Johnson was twenty-eight and principal of Stanton High School in Jacksonville, Florida, when he set out to write a poem for Lincoln's birthday. It was first recited in 1900 by 500 students. His brother, J. Rosamond Johnson, set it to music, and after the Jacksonville fire of 1901 the two left for New York to work on Broadway. Johnson wrote that the schoolchildren kept singing it and, as teachers, taught it to others. In 1919 the NAACP named it the Negro national anthem.
