Gundestrup Cauldron

Maker unknown · c. 150–1 BC · Silver, partly gilded · Diam. 69 cm; H. 42 cm · National Museum of Denmark, Copenhagen

Free to use with credit. Photo: Knud Winckelmann, CC BY-SA 3.0

The cauldron was found in 1891 in a peat bog near Gundestrup in Denmark, taken apart, with the plates stacked inside the base. It is silver, 69 centimetres across, and weighs just under nine kilograms. The plates were hammered out from behind, then gilded, and some figures have eyes of inlaid glass. One of the eight outer plates is missing. It was probably not made in Denmark: the workmanship relates to Thracian silver and many of the figures to the Celts.
