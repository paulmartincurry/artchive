In the morning when thou risest unwillingly, let this thought be present,--I am rising to the work of a human being. Why then am I dissatisfied if I am going to do the things for which I exist and for which I was brought into the world?

Meditations — The work of a human being (Book V, 1)

Marcus Aurelius · c. 170–180

Translated by George Long

Nothing is known of the book's early history. Some time before 907 a bishop, Arethas of Caesarea, wrote that his old copy was falling to pieces and that he had had it recopied. Around 1150 a grammarian in Constantinople, John Tzetzes, quoted passages from Books IV and V. The first printed edition came out at Zürich in 1558 or 1559, and the manuscript it was printed from has since been lost.
