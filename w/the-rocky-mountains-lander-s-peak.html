The Rocky Mountains, Lander's Peak

Albert Bierstadt · 1863 · Oil on canvas · 73 1/2 × 120 3/4 in. (186.7 × 306.7 cm) · The Met

Free to use

The sketches behind this canvas were made in 1859, when Bierstadt travelled with Frederick W. Lander's survey party as far as the Wyoming Range. The scene is not the landscape as it appears at the peak; he altered it for dramatic effect. The mountain was named after Lander on Bierstadt's initiative, after Lander's death in the Civil War. In 1865 the British railway entrepreneur James McHenry bought the ten-foot-wide painting for $25,000, the most paid for an American painting to that point, and Bierstadt later bought it back.
