Tiger

Muqi Fachang · c. 1250–79 · Hanging scroll, ink and slight color on silk · 125.2 × 57.2 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art

Free to use

The faint red that once shaded the tiger's tongue and nose has faded with the silk. Muqi was a Chan monk from Sichuan who moved to Hangzhou. In his own time he was ignored by mainstream Song society, and Yuan critics called his work sketchy and coarse, unfit to hang in monasteries. In Japan his paintings were collected from the fourteenth century on, and this pair passed through the hands of the Ashikaga and Tokugawa shoguns.
