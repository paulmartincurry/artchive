In overthrowing me, you have cut down in San Domingo only the trunk of the tree of liberty. It will spring up again by the roots for they are numerous and deep.

Words on Being Deported

Toussaint Louverture · June 1802

Translated by C. L. R. James · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

In 1802 the French general Jean-Baptiste Brunet invited Toussaint Louverture to a parley and had him arrested when he arrived. He was fifty-nine. Born into slavery on the Bréda plantation and trained as a coachman, he had been a free man since the 1770s and governor-general of Saint-Domingue since 1797. He was shipped to France, jailed at the Fort de Joux, and died there on 7 April 1803. Saint-Domingue declared its independence, as Haiti, on 1 January 1804.
