Behistun Inscription

Maker unknown · ca. 520–519 BCE · Rock relief and inscription on a limestone cliff

Free to use with credit. Photo: Korosh.091, CC BY-SA 4.0

Darius the Great had this carved about 100 metres up a limestone cliff above the old road between Babylon and Ecbatana. It is roughly 15 metres high and 25 metres wide, and it gives the same text in Old Persian, Elamite and Babylonian. The king's beard is a separate block of stone fixed on with iron pins and lead. In 1835 Henry Rawlinson, a British East India Company army officer, climbed the cliff with the help of a local boy to copy the Old Persian text. During the Second World War Allied soldiers used the relief for target practice.
