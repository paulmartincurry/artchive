I know that during that long and racking march of thirty-six hours over the unnamed mountains and glaciers of South Georgia it seemed to me often that we were four, not three.

South — We were four, not three (Ch. X)

Ernest Shackleton · 1919

Shackleton set this down in his 1919 book South. He and five other members of the expedition had made an 800-mile open-boat journey in the James Caird to reach South Georgia. After the book appeared, other survivors of extreme hardship came forward with similar experiences, and Eliot stated in his notes to The Waste Land of 1922 that lines in the poem were inspired by Shackleton's.
