Thinking only of their vow that they would crush the Tartars —
On the desert, clad in sable and silk, five thousand of them fell. . . .
But arisen from their crumbling bones on the banks of the river at the border,
Dreams of them enter, like men alive, into rooms where their loves lie sleeping.

Turkestan

Chen Tao · before 882; English 1929

Translated by Witter Bynner and Kiang Kang-hu

Chen Tao lived from 824 to 882, late in the Tang dynasty. He wrote four poems about war in Longxi, and this one, sometimes called the Lung-hsi Song, is the one that went into the anthology Three Hundred Tang Poems.
