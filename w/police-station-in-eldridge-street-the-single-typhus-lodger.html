Police Station in Eldridge Street: The Single Typhus Lodger

Jacob Riis · c. 1890, printed later · Photographic print · Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, Washington

Free to use

New York police stations gave the homeless temporary shelter, and those lodging rooms were prone to crime and to outbreaks of typhus. Riis worked as a police reporter from a press office facing police headquarters on Mulberry Street. When Theodore Roosevelt became president of the city's Police Board, he and Riis worked together to abolish the police lodging houses. Riis later replaced his dangerous flash pistol with magnesium powder lit in a frying pan.
