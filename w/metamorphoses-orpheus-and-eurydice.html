He, enamoured, fearing lest she should flag, and impatient to behold her, turned his eyes; and immediately she sank back again. She, hapless one! both stretching out her arms, and struggling to be grasped, and to grasp him, caught nothing but the fleeting air.

Metamorphoses — Orpheus and Eurydice (Book X)

Ovid · c. 8 CE

Translated by Henry T. Riley

Virgil had told this story a few decades before Ovid, at the end of the Georgics, his poem about farming. The condition set in the underworld was that Eurydice must walk behind Orpheus and that he must not turn to look at her on the way out.
