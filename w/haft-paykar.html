The eye's black pupil views the world,
and robes of black are never soiled.

Haft Paykar (The Black Dome)

Nizami Ganjavi · 1197 CE

Translated by Julie Scott Meisami · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

This is probably the last of Nizami's five long poems, finished in August 1197 when he was in his mid-fifties, and dedicated to the ruler of Maragheh. Nizami was not a court poet and is believed to have spent his whole life in the South Caucasus. In the poem he tells his son to take on more responsibility, as he was himself growing frail. The king's palace in the story has seven domes, each with its own day of the week, planet and colour, and a tale is told in each. Puccini's opera Turandot comes from the tale told in the red dome.
