As long as the vessel is safe,
whether it be great or small, the mariner, the pilot, every man in turn
should exert himself

The Third Philippic

Demosthenes · 341 BCE

Translated by Charles Rann Kennedy, 1852

Demosthenes was about forty-three when he gave this speech and had been speaking on public affairs since 354 BC. He had earned his living as a logographer, writing speeches for other people's private lawsuits. Orphaned at seven, he took up rhetoric in order to take to court the guardians who had mishandled his inheritance, and at twenty he delivered five orations against them. Plutarch states that he overcame a stammer by speaking with pebbles in his mouth.
