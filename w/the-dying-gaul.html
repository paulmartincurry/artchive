The Dying Gaul

Roman copy of a Greek original · ca. 230–220 BCE (1st–2nd c. CE Marble) · Marble · Capitoline Museums, Rome

Free to use with credit. Photo: antmoose, CC BY 2.0

The statue was found broken into several pieces and first appears in a 1623 inventory of the Ludovisi family's collection. The sculptor Ippolito Buzzi pinned it back together and carved the right arm, which was long credited to Michelangelo. Until the 20th century it was usually called The Dying Gladiator. Napoleon's forces took it to Paris in 1797, and it came back to Rome in 1816. The Greek original it copies is lost and is thought to have been bronze.
