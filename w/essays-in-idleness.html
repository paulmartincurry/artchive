The most precious thing in life is its uncertainty.

Essays in Idleness (7)

Yoshida Kenkō · 1330–1332

Translated by Donald Keene · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Kenkō had been an officer of the guards at the Imperial palace before he retired to become a Buddhist monk and hermit. He wrote these essays between 1330 and 1332, when he was close to fifty, and they were published after his death. The original was not divided or numbered; the division into 243 passages dates to the 17th century. An old account has him writing his thoughts on scraps of paper and pasting them on the walls of his cottage for a friend to compile later, which most modern scholars reject.
