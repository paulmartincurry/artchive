And pluck till time and times are done,
The silver apples of the moon,
The golden apples of the sun.

The Song of Wandering Aengus

W. B. Yeats · 1897

The poem was first printed in 1897 in the British magazine The Sketch, under the title A Mad Song, and took its present name in The Wind Among the Reeds in 1899. Yeats later said a Greek folk song had suggested it, though he was thinking of Ireland and its spirits when he wrote. In a letter of 1899 he called it the kind of poem he liked best himself.
