How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations!

Isaiah 14:12–17

Book of Isaiah (Hebrew Bible / KJV) · 8th–6th century BCE (trans. 1611)

The passage belongs to a taunt against the king of Babylon that runs for 18 verses. The Hebrew name of the fallen one, Helel, comes from a root meaning to shine, and Jerome's Latin Vulgate rendered it Lucifer, the morning star. The song may also have been applied to the Assyrian king Sargon II, who died in 705 BCE and whose body was never recovered from the battlefield.
