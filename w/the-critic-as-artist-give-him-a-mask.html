Man is least himself when he talks in his own person. Give him a mask, and he will tell you the truth.

The Critic as Artist: Give Him a Mask

Oscar Wilde · 1890

This dialogue ran in two parts in The Nineteenth Century in July and September 1890 under a different title, The True Function and Value of Criticism. Wilde revised and renamed it for the collection Intentions, published on 1 May 1891, where it is by far the longest piece. The two speakers are Gilbert, who leads, and Ernest, who puts forward ideas for Gilbert to reject. Wilde had read classics at Trinity College Dublin and at Magdalen College, Oxford, where Walter Pater and John Ruskin were among his tutors.
