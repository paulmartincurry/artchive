Happy the moment when we are seated in the palace, thou and I,
With two forms and with two figures but with one soul, thou and I.

Divan-e Shams — Happy the Moment (XXXVIII)

Jalāl al-Dīn Muḥammad Rūmī · 13th century

Translated by R. A. Nicholson, 1898

The Divan holds more than 40,000 verses and over 3,000 ghazals, written in Persian after the disappearance of Rumi's teacher Shams-i Tabrizi and dedicated to him. A few of its poems are in Arabic, and some are bilingual, in Turkish, Arabic and Greek. By the convention of the ghazal a poet signs off with a pen name at the end of the poem.
