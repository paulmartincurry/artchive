The Heart of the Andes

Frederic Edwin Church · 1859 · Oil on canvas · The Met

Free to use

A businessman, Cyrus West Field, paid for Church's two trips to Ecuador and Colombia in 1853 and 1857, hoping the pictures would draw investors to his South American ventures. The landscape is a composite of places Church saw there, not one view. He showed it alone in a New York studio building in 1859, in a dark window-like frame fourteen feet wide, and more than 12,000 people paid twenty-five cents each to see it, some through the opera glasses handed out. It sold for 10,000 dollars, then the highest price paid for a work by a living American artist.
