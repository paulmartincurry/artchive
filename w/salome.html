Ah! I have kissed thy mouth, Jokanaan, I have kissed thy mouth. There was a bitter taste on thy lips.

Salomé (final speech)

Oscar Wilde (Trans. Lord Alfred Douglas, 1894) · 1891 (1894 English Edition)

Translated by Lord Alfred Douglas, 1894

Sarah Bernhardt took the play for her London season and had it in rehearsal in June 1892 when the Lord Chamberlain withheld approval under a rule against showing biblical characters on stage. It was not performed publicly in Britain until 1931. The first production was a single night in Paris on 11 February 1896, while Wilde was serving his prison sentence, and he never saw the play staged. He had been thinking about the subject since 1877, when Walter Pater introduced him to a Flaubert story about Herodias at Oxford.
