Venus de Milo

Maker unknown · 2nd century BCE · Parian marble · Louvre Museum, Paris

Free to use with credit. Photo: Livioandronico2013, CC BY-SA 4.0

A Greek farmer on Milos dug up the statue on 8 April 1820, in two large pieces and a smaller third. A French sailor, Olivier Voutier, watched and urged him to keep digging. By the time a French envoy came to buy it, another buyer had it loaded on a ship, and the French stepped in. Against the practice of the day it was shown as found, with only the tip of the nose, the lower lip, one big toe and some drapery restored. The back is less finely finished than the front. It has left France once, for Tokyo and Kyoto in 1964.
