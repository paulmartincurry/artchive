Let me be a free man—free to travel, free to stop, free to work, free to trade where I choose, free to choose my own teachers, free to follow the religion of my fathers, free to think and talk and act for myself—and I will obey every law, or submit to the penalty.

An Indian’s Views of Indian Affairs — A Free Man

Chief Joseph (Hin-mah-too-yah-lat-kekt) · 1879

A later life of Joseph describes the article as dictated by him. His band was never allowed back to the Wallowa Valley: he was moved between forts and reservations on the southern Great Plains, and then to the Colville Indian Reservation in Washington State. The Treaty of Walla Walla of 1855 had granted the Nez Perce 7.5 million acres of their ancestral land.
