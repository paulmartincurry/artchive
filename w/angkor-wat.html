Angkor Wat

Maker unknown · Early–Mid 12th Century · Sandstone

Free to use with credit. Photo: Bjørn Christian Tørrissen, CC BY-SA

The Khmer king Suryavarman II ordered it as his state temple, dedicated to Vishnu, and it is generally thought to have been meant as his tomb. Work stopped when he died, and some of the reliefs were left unfinished. Unlike most temples at Angkor it faces west. From the late thirteenth century it was turned by degrees into a Buddhist temple, and though neglected after the sixteenth century it was never completely abandoned. In the 1970s Khmer Rouge soldiers camped there burned what wood was left in the buildings.
