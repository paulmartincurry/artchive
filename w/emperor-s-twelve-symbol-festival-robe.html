Emperor's Twelve-Symbol Festival Robe

Maker unknown · Qianlong period (1736–95) · Silk and gold and silver thread embroidery on silk twill · 143.8 × 161.3 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

The twelve symbols embroidered on this robe were reserved for reigning emperors, empresses and the immediate imperial family. An Eastern Han rule of 59 AD set out that only the emperor could wear the sun, moon and stars, while councillors, dukes and princes were held to nine. The robe was made in the reign of the Qianlong Emperor, in silk embroidered with gold and silver thread, and measures about 1.6 metres from cuff to cuff. Lewis Einstein gave it to the Met in 1954.
