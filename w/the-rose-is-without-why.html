The rose is without why.
It blows because it blows.
It thinks not of itself,
And no display it shows.

The Rose Is Without Why (Cherubinic Wanderer I, 289)

Angelus Silesius · 1657; English 1909

Translated by Paul Carus

Johann Scheffler was court physician to the Duke of Württemberg-Oels when he first tried to publish his poems, and the duke's Lutheran court clergyman refused permission. He converted to Catholicism in 1653, took the name Angelus Silesius, and brought out this collection in 1657. It grew to 1,676 short poems, most of them rhyming couplets.
