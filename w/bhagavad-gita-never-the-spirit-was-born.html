Never the spirit was born; the spirit shall cease to be never;
Never was time it was not; End and Beginning are dreams!
Birthless and deathless and changeless remaineth the spirit for ever;
Death hath not touched it at all, dead though the house of it seems!

Bhagavad Gita — Never the Spirit Was Born (Chapter II)

Vyasa (Sanskrit Epic) · 2nd–1st century BCE; English 1885

Translated by Edwin Arnold

Krishna, who drives Arjuna's chariot and is also his maternal cousin, says these lines to him as the Kurukshetra War is about to begin. Arjuna has seen his own relatives and teachers in the opposing army and cannot bring himself to kill them. The Gita is generally dated to the second or first century BCE, though its date of composition remains unresolved.
