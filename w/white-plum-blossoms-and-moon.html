White Plum Blossoms and Moon

Itō Jakuchū · 1755 · Hanging scroll; ink and color on silk · Image 140.7 × 79.4 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

Jakuchū was about thirty-nine when he painted this scroll in 1755. In his late thirties he built a two-storey studio on the west bank of the Kamo River and named it Shin'en-kan, after a phrase in a poem by Tao Qian. Around the same time he befriended Daiten Kenjō, a Rinzai monk, and through him gained access to the collection of Japanese and Chinese paintings at Shōkoku-ji.
