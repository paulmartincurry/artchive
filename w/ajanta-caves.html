Ajanta Caves

Maker unknown · 2nd Century BCE – ca. 480 · Rock-cut caves in basalt and granite

Free to use with credit. Photo: Soman, CC BY 2.5

Thirty caves, monasteries and worship halls, were cut into a rock wall seventy-five metres high above a gorge of the River Waghur. Monks used them as a retreat during the monsoon. Later scholarship puts most of the work in the twenty years from 460 to 480, though others argue for a longer span. On 28 April 1819 a shepherd boy showed the entrance of Cave 10 to John Smith, a British cavalry officer out hunting tigers. Smith scratched his name and the date across a painting of a bodhisattva.
