The Dog

Francisco de Goya · 1820 · Oil · Museo del Prado

If Goya had a title for this painting he never revealed it, and its names were assigned by others after his death. He painted it in oils directly onto a wall of the Quinta del Sordo, a small two-storey house near Madrid that he bought in 1819, when he was in his seventies. It is one of fourteen works he made on the walls there, none of them commissioned or meant for public display. After the banker Émile d'Erlanger acquired the house in 1874, the murals were transferred to canvas, and he later donated them to the Spanish state.
