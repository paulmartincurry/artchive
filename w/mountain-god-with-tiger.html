Mountain God with Tiger

Maker unknown · late 19th century · Framed painting, ink and color on silk · 83.8 × 99.1 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

Sansin, the mountain gods of Korean shamanism, are thought to grant health and wealth and drive out illness. When the god was taken into Korean Buddhism, most temples built a shrine called a sansingak for him. Sansin was first shown as a woman and, under Confucian influence, gradually became an old man, and Buddhist paintings set him with a tiger against a pine forest. This painting on silk came to the Met in 2024 from the children of Lea R. Sneider.
