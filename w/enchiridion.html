Enchiridion (Ch. 1)

Epictetus (compiled by Arrian) · ca. 125 CE

Translated by Nicholas White, 1983 · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Epictetus left no writings of his own. The handbook was compiled by his pupil Arrian, who studied under him as a young man around AD 108 and said he wrote down what he heard word for word. Epictetus was born into slavery, taught in Rome after he was freed, and opened a school at Nicopolis in Greece when the emperor Domitian banished philosophers from the city. After his death his oil lamp is said to have sold for 3,000 drachmae.
