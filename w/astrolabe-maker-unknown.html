Astrolabe

Maker unknown · late 18th century, with later additions · Brass with pierced and engraved decoration · 25.2 × 18.5 × 3 cm · Art Institute of Chicago

Free to use

An astrolabe is a star chart and a calculating device in one: with it a user could measure the height of a star above the horizon, identify stars and planets, and find the local latitude from the time. This brass one was made in Isfahan in the late 18th century, with later additions, and is 25.2 centimetres high. Emily Crane Chadbourne gave it to the Art Institute in 1929.
