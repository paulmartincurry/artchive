Young Hare (Feldhase)

Albrecht Dürer · 1502 · Watercolor and gouache on paper · Albertina, Vienna

Free to use

The animal's build marks it as a full-grown hare, not a young one; the German title means field hare. Dürer painted it in his Nuremberg workshop over an underpainting of brown washes, and the sheet stayed there until his death in 1528. It was then sold to Willibald Imhoff, who sold it to Emperor Rudolf II in 1588 because of financial difficulties. A window frame is reflected in the eye. At least twelve copies were made by Dürer's contemporaries.
