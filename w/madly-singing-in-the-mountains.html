There is no one among men that has not a special failing:
And my failing consists in writing verses.

Madly Singing in the Mountains

Bai Juyi (Po Chü-i) · c. 816

Translated by Arthur Waley, 1918

Bai Juyi wrote over 2,800 poems and had them copied and distributed to make sure they survived. It is said that he would rewrite any part of a poem that one of his servants could not understand. Around 816 he was in exile from the court. Among the charges against him were two of his own poems, on flowers and on a well, held to show a lack of filial piety because his mother had apparently died by falling into a well while looking at flowers.
