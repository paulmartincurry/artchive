I repeat with more vehement certainty; truth is on the march, and nothing can stop it.

J'Accuse…!

Émile Zola · January 13, 1898

Translated by Benjamin R. Tucker (publisher; translator not named in the book), 1898

The letter filled the front page of Georges Clemenceau's Paris daily L'Aurore on 13 January 1898. Zola was fifty-seven, and he meant to draw the accusation so broadly that men in the government would have to sue him for libel, which would bring the evidence against Dreyfus into the open. He was found guilty on 23 February 1898 and fled to England to avoid prison, returning in June 1899. Four years after the letter he died of carbon monoxide poisoning from a blocked chimney.
