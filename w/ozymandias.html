Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!
Nothing beside remains.

Ozymandias

Percy Bysshe Shelley · 1818

The sonnet came out of a friendly competition with the banker Horace Smith, who spent the Christmas season of 1817–1818 with Percy and Mary Shelley. Both men wrote on the same passage of the Greek historian Diodorus Siculus, which describes a massive Egyptian statue and its inscription. Shelley's poem was published in The Examiner on 11 January 1818 under the pen name Glirastes, and Smith's sonnet of the same name followed several weeks later.
