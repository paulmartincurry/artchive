O grave, O bridal bower, O prison house
Hewn from the rock, my everlasting home,
Whither I go to join the mighty host
Of kinsfolk, Persephassa’s guests long dead,
The last of all, of all more miserable,
I pass, my destined span of years cut short.

Antigone — O Grave, O Bridal Bower (Antigone's farewell)

Sophocles · c. 441 BC

Translated by F. Storr, 1912

Sophocles wrote Antigone in 442 or 440 BC, and it is thought to be his second-oldest surviving play, after Ajax. Its events come last of the three Theban plays, but he wrote it first. The story picks up where Aeschylus' Seven Against Thebes ends.
