the most hideous sight, a green-faced monster, a ghoul with great jagged teeth like a saw, leaning over a human pelt, the skin of an entire human body

The Painted Skin (Huàpí)

Pu Songling · ca. 1700 (Qing Manuscript / 1880 Translation)

Translated by John Minford · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Pu Songling came from a poor merchant family in Shandong, the province where this story is set. He took his first degree in the imperial examinations at eighteen and was seventy-one before he was given a higher degree. For most of his life he worked as a private tutor and collected stories. The collection was published in 1740, twenty-five years after his death. The title of this story has become an everyday phrase in Chinese.
