I remember one day you were handling the well-known commonplace,—that we do not suddenly fall on death, but advance towards it by slight degrees; we die every day. For every day a little of our life is taken from us; even when we are growing, our life is on the wane.

Moral Letters to Lucilius — We Die Every Day (Letter 24)

Seneca the Younger · c. 63–65 CE

Translated by Richard M. Gummere

Seneca wrote the letters in the last two or three years of his life. In 65 he was executed by forced suicide for alleged complicity in the Pisonian conspiracy to assassinate Nero, of which he may have been innocent.
