Young Ladies of the Village

Gustave Courbet · 1851–52 · Oil on canvas · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

The three young women were modelled by Courbet's sisters Zoé, Zélie and Juliette, and the hills are real places near Ornans, his home town. He was thirty-two when he showed the canvas, nearly two by two and a half metres, at the Paris Salon in April 1852. Critics complained that the figures were too small beside the cows, but the duc de Morny bought it at once. His widow sold it at the Hôtel Drouot in 1878 for 5,000 francs.
