Portrait of Paul Revere

John Singleton Copley · 1768 · Oil on canvas · Museum of Fine Arts, Boston

Copley bought his picture frames from Revere and owed him money for them, and he is believed to have painted this portrait to clear the debt. Revere sat without hair powder or formal dress and went on working on the teapot while Copley painted. After Revere's death in 1818 his daughter Harriet, who disliked how informal it was, had a copy made that kept only the face and put him in a red uniform. The original spent some years in a Boston attic.
