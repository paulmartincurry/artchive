The only way to get rid of a temptation is to yield to it. Resist it, and your soul grows sick with longing for the things it has forbidden to itself, with desire for what its monstrous laws have made monstrous and unlawful.

The Picture of Dorian Gray — The only way to get rid of a temptation (Ch. II)

Oscar Wilde · 1890

When the story came out in the July 1890 issue of Lippincott's Monthly Magazine, the bookseller WHSmith, then the largest in Britain, withdrew every copy of the issue from its railway bookstalls. Some reviewers said Wilde deserved prosecution. For the book of 1891 he wrote six new chapters, 3, 5 and 15 to 18. At his trials in 1895 passages from the magazine version were read out during cross-examination.
