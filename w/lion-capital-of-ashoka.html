Lion Capital of Ashoka

Maker unknown · ca. 250 BCE · Sandstone · Sarnath Museum, India

Free to use with credit. Photo: Chrisi1964, CC BY-SA 4.0

The capital was carved from a single block of sandstone, polished, and fixed to the top of Ashoka's column at Sarnath with a metal dowel. It stands seven feet tall. It fell and was buried, and F. O. Oertel, an engineer in the Public Works Department, dug it out in the winter of 1904–1905. Two of the lions' heads had come off and were reattached. In 1947 the capital without its lotus became the model for India's state emblem, and its wheel the model for the wheel on the flag.
