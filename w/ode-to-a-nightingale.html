Darkling I listen; and, for many a time
I have been half in love with easeful Death.

Ode to a Nightingale

John Keats · 1819

In the spring of 1819 a nightingale nested near the house in Hampstead that Keats shared with Charles Armitage Brown. By Brown's account Keats carried a chair from the breakfast table to the grass under a plum tree, sat for two or three hours, and came back in with four or five scraps of paper that held the poem. The editor of the Annals of the Fine Arts paid him a small sum and printed it in the July issue.
