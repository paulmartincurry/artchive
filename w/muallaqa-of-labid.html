The tent marks in Minan are worn away,
where she encamped and where she alighted,

Muʿallaqa of Labīd

Labīd ibn Rabī'a · Early 7th Century CE

Translated by Michael A. Sells, 1989 · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Labīd belonged to the Bani Amir, a division of the Hawazin tribe of higher Nejd, and in his younger years he was an active warrior. He was later sent to Medina by a sick uncle to get a remedy from Muhammad, and he accepted Islam soon afterwards. He vowed to feed people whenever the east wind blew for as long as it kept blowing, and the leader of Kufa once sent him a hundred camels so that he could keep the vow. His dates are given as about 560 to about 661.
