The Daughters of Edward Darley Boit

John Singer Sargent · 1882 · Oil on canvas · Museum of Fine Arts, Boston

Free to use

The four girls were four, eight, twelve and fourteen, and the setting is thought to be the foyer of the family's Paris apartment. Their father, Edward Boit, was a friend of John Singer Sargent and a minor painter himself; nobody is sure whether he commissioned the picture or Sargent proposed it. The canvas is square, 222.5 centimetres a side, which was unusual for a group portrait. None of the sisters married. They gave the painting to the Boston museum in 1919, and it now hangs between the two Japanese vases shown in it.
