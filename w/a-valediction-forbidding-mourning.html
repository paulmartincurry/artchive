Our two soules therefore, which are one,
Though I must goe, endure not yet
A breach, but an expansion,
Like gold to ayery thinnesse beate.

A Valediction: Forbidding Mourning

John Donne · c. 1611 (published 1633)

Around the date given to this poem John Donne was about thirty-nine and ten years into a marriage made in secret. The wedding, just before Christmas in 1601, had cost him his post as a secretary and put him in Fleet Prison. He and Anne More then lived in small houses in Surrey, where he scraped a living as a lawyer. In 1610 he met Sir Robert Drury, who gave the family an apartment in his house in Drury Lane.
