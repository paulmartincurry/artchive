She had been fashioned to adorn and delight; to what other end does nature round the rose-leaf and paint the humming-bird’s breast?

The House of Mirth — Fashioned to adorn and delight (Book II, Ch. 11)

Edith Wharton · 1905

Scribner's Magazine began serializing the novel in January 1905, and the book came out on 14 October. By the end of December it had sold 140,000 copies, and Charles Scribner wrote to Wharton that it was selling faster than any book his firm had published. She was forty-three. One title she considered, A Moment's Ornament, came from a Wordsworth poem; the one she chose comes from Ecclesiastes.
