And a moment that ought to have lasted for ever
Has come and gone before I knew.

The Inlaid Harp

Li Shangyin · before 858; English 1929

Translated by Witter Bynner and Kiang Kang-hu

This is the poem in Chinese called Jin Se, the brocade zither, and the most cryptic thing Li Shangyin wrote. One critic counted five main readings of it, among them frustration with his career and a memorial to his dead wife. Li never held high office; factional disputes, or his link to an opponent of the court eunuchs, kept him down.
