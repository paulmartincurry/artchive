Tipu's Tiger

Maker unknown · ca. 1795 · Wood and metal · Victoria and Albert Museum

Free to use with credit. Photo: Victoria and Albert Museum, CC BY-SA 3.0

Turning a crank works bellows that make the man wail and the tiger grunt, and lifts the man's left arm up and down. A flap in the tiger's flank hides the ivory keyboard of a small pipe organ. It was made around 1795 for Tipu Sultan of Mysore and taken from his palace when East India Company troops stormed his capital in 1799. In London the public could turn the handle freely until it disappeared. A German bomb in the Second World War broke the wooden casing into several hundred pieces, and it was back on display by 1947.
