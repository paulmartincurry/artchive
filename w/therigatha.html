The name I am called by means freed
and I am quite free, well-free from three crooked things,
mortar, pestle, and husband with his own crooked thing.

Therīgāthā (Muttā)

Muttā (Elder Buddhist Nun) · ca. 5th–3rd Century BCE

Translated by Charles Hallisey, 2015 · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

The poems of this collection were composed and handed down by mouth in the Magadhi language, and were written down in Pali only in about 80 BCE. It holds 494 verses in 73 poems, arranged in sixteen chapters roughly by length, and is the earliest known collection of writing by women. The verses are assigned to named nuns, but there is no evidence that those women composed them. A monk named Dhammapala compiled and annotated the text.
