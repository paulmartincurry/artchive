There are more things, Lucilius, likely to frighten us than there are to crush us; we suffer more often in imagination than in reality.

Moral Letters to Lucilius — We Suffer More Often in Imagination (Letter 13)

Seneca the Younger · c. 63–65 CE

Translated by Richard M. Gummere

The letters are addressed to Lucilius Junior, then procurator of Sicily, a man known only through Seneca's writings. They read as open letters, written with a wider readership in mind. A real letter from central Italy to Sicily would have taken four to eight days to arrive.
