At night rises up the smoke of the warriors, a delight to the Lord the Giver of Life; the shield-flower spreads abroad its leaves, marvelous deeds agitate the earth; here is the place of the fatal flowers of death which cover the fields.

A Song Urging to War (stanzas 3–8)

Anonymous Nahua poet (Cantares Mexicanos) · Written down in the 16th century

Translated by Daniel G. Brinton, 1887

The flowery war, xochiyaoyotl in Nahuatl, was a ritual war that the Aztec Triple Alliance fought on and off with Tlaxcala, Huejotzingo and Cholula, probably until the Spaniards arrived in 1519. The two armies met on a date and at a place fixed in advance, and the fighting began with the burning of a pyre of paper and incense. Songs of the Cantares Mexicanos were performed to the upright skin drum and the horizontal log drum.
