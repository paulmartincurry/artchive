Fructus Opuntiae (Hortus Eystettensis)

Basilius Besler · 1613 · Hand-coloured copper engraving · Universitätsbibliothek Eichstätt-Ingolstadt

Free to use

The plants in the book grew in the garden of the Prince-Bishop of Eichstätt, laid out on eight terraces around his palace, the Willibaldsburg. He commissioned Basilius Besler, a Nuremberg apothecary, to record it in 1611 and died before the book appeared. Swedish troops ransacked the garden in 1633–34; it was reconstructed and opened to the public in 1998.
