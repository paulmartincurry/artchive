Lindisfarne Gospels: Opening of the Gospel of Matthew

Eadfrith of Lindisfarne · ca. 715–720 · Parchment · British Library

The vellum for the book took roughly 150 calf skins, and the work took between five and ten years. It is thought to be the work of one hand, that of Eadfrith, Bishop of Lindisfarne, who made ninety colours from six local minerals and vegetable extracts and bound them mostly with egg white. The blue is indigo. Parts were left unfinished, so he was probably still working on it when he died in 721. About 250 years later a priest named Aldred wrote an Old English translation between the lines.
