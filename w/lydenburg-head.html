Lydenburg Head

Maker unknown · ca. 500 · Terracotta · Iziko South African Museum, Cape Town

Free to use with credit. Photo: Nkansahrexford, CC BY 3.0

A boy named Karl-Ludwig von Bezing came across the first pieces while playing on his father's farm at Lydenburg. He went back to dig in 1962 and 1966, and the sherds he collected were put together, largely at the University of Cape Town, into seven hollow heads. The faces were built up with thin strips of wet clay, cut with lines before they dried. Two of the seven are large enough to fit over a child's head. Charcoal found with them was radiocarbon dated to about 500 AD.
