The Slave Ship

J. M. W. Turner · 1840 · Oil on canvas · Museum of Fine Arts Boston

Turner was about sixty-five when he showed this at the Royal Academy in 1840, under a longer title and with lines from an unfinished poem of his own. The subject goes back to 1781, when the captain of the slave ship Zong had 132 people thrown overboard so that insurance could be claimed. That happened in calm water; the typhoon is Turner's addition. John Ruskin's father gave him the painting in 1844, and Ruskin sold it in 1872. Alice Hooper bought it at auction in 1876 and put it on show at the Museum of Fine Arts in Boston.
