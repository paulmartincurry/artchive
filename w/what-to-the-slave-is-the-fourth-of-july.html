For it is not light that is needed, but fire;
it is not the gentle shower, but thunder.

What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?

Frederick Douglass · July 5, 1852

The speech is called a Fourth of July oration but was given on July 5, 1852, at Corinthian Hall in Rochester, New York, to a meeting of the Rochester Ladies' Anti-Slavery Society. Douglass was thirty-four and had lived in the city since 1847, when he moved there to publish his newspaper The North Star. A week later his paper was advertising the address as a forty-page pamphlet at ten cents a copy, or six dollars a hundred.
