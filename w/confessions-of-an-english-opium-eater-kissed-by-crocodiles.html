I was buried for a thousand years in stone coffins, with mummies and sphynxes, in narrow chambers at the heart of eternal pyramids. I was kissed, with cancerous kisses, by crocodiles; and laid, confounded with all unutterable slimy things, amongst reeds and Nilotic mud.

Confessions of an English Opium-Eater — Kissed by crocodiles (The Pains of Opium, May 1818)

Thomas De Quincey · 1821

By his own account De Quincey first took opium to relieve neuralgic pain in his face, and through 1812 he used it no more than weekly. He began taking it daily in 1813, after an illness and the death of Wordsworth's three-year-old daughter Catherine. From 1813 to 1819 his daily dose was very high, and the dreams in the last part of the book come from those years. Late in 1848 he went 61 days with none at all.
