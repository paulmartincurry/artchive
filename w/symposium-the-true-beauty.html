But what if man had eyes to see the true beauty—the divine beauty, I mean, pure and clear and unalloyed, not clogged with the pollutions of mortality and all the colours and vanities of human life—thither looking, and holding converse with the true beauty simple and divine?

Symposium — The True Beauty (Diotima)

Plato · after 385 BCE

Translated by Benjamin Jowett

The dialogue is set at a banquet where the guests make speeches on love in turn. Socrates says that what he tells here he learned from a wise woman called Diotima, who questioned and taught him as he had just questioned Agathon.
