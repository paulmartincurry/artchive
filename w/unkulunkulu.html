Through the word of the lizard, men will die.

Unkulunkulu (the chameleon and the lizard)

Zulu tradition · Recorded c. 1868

Translated by Henry Callaway, 1868

Unkulunkulu means the old-old one, a first ancestor; with the spread of Christianity the name came to be used for a creator god. Henry Callaway, an English missionary, began studying the Zulu after settling at Springvale, near the Umkomazi River, in 1858. He noted that the lizard of this story was killed whenever it was seen, as the one that ran to say men should die, and that to go like a chameleon meant to go slowly.
