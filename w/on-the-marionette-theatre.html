‘Does that mean’, I said in some bewilderment, ‘we must eat again of the tree of knowledge in order to return to the state of innocence?’
‘Of course,’ he said, ‘but that’s the final chapter in the history of the world.’

On the Marionette Theatre

Heinrich von Kleist · 1810

Translated by Idris Parry · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Kleist had been a Prussian army officer, joining in 1792 and retiring as a lieutenant in 1799, and afterwards a clerk in the Ministry of Finance. In 1807 the French arrested him as a spy and held him in the Fort de Joux. By 1810 he had settled in Berlin, where he edited a newspaper, the Berliner Abendblätter. On 21 November 1811 he shot a terminally ill friend, Henriette Vogel, and then himself on the shore of the Kleiner Wannsee. He was thirty-four.
