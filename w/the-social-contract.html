Man is born free; and everywhere he is in chains.

The Social Contract (Book I, Ch. 1)

Jean-Jacques Rousseau · 1762

Translated by G. D. H. Cole, 1913

France prohibited the book's distribution as soon as it appeared in 1762, and Rousseau left the country to avoid prison. Geneva, his birthplace, burned it. Paris banned it too, and the Church placed it on its index of forbidden books. It was the chapter on civil religion that caused most of the trouble. Rousseau was a watchmaker's son, and he generally signed his books as a citizen of Geneva.
