And let her not look to find my love, as before; my love, which by her fault has dropped, like a flower on the meadow's edge, when it has been touched by the plough passing by.

Carmen 11 (Furius and Aurelius)

Catullus · c. 55 BCE

Translated by F. W. Cornish

This is one of only two poems Catullus wrote in the Sapphic metre; the other, poem 51, is his version of a poem by Sappho. The message is carried by Furius and Aurelius, two men he usually addresses with condescension in other poems. In line 10 he compliments Caesar, which is unlike him.
