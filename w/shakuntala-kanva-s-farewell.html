Shakuntala must go to-day;
I miss her now at heart;
I dare not speak a loving word
Or choking tears will start.

Shakuntala — Kanva's Farewell (Act IV)

Kālidāsa · ca. 4th–5th century CE

Translated by Arthur W. Ryder, 1912

Shakuntala was the first Indian play translated into a Western language, by Sir William Jones in 1789, and within a hundred years there were at least 46 translations in twelve European languages. Goethe published an epigram about it in 1791 and took a theatrical convention from its prologue for Faust. Kālidāsa drew the story from the Mahābhārata.
