For famous men have the whole earth as their memorial: it is not only the inscriptions on their graves in their own country that mark them out; no, in foreign lands also, not in any visible form but in people's hearts, their memory abides and grows.

Funeral Oration

Pericles (via Thucydides) · 431 BCE

Translated by Rex Warner · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

In 431 BC, the first year of the Peloponnesian War, Pericles was about sixty-four and held the rank of strategos, or general. He had been the leading man in Athens for some thirty years. Two years later he died in the Plague of Athens, as did several members of his family. Thucydides was his contemporary.
