What then? what are his million bales?
Mere dewdrops on the bamboo grass.

Haiku (A Million Bales)

Kobayashi Issa · ca. 1814

Translated by Basil Hall Chamberlain, 1911

Issa was the first son of a farming family in Kashiwabara, in Shinano Province, and his father sent him to Edo at fifteen to make a living. After his father's death in 1801 he fought his stepmother over the inheritance for years and in the end secured half the property. He went back to the village at forty-nine and married. He wrote more than 20,000 haiku, about a thousand of them on snails, frogs, fleas and other small creatures.
