O, what is this slumber
Now hath o’ercome thee, (for now) art thou dark, nor art able to hear me?”
Natheless he raised not his eyes, and his heart, (when Gilgamish) felt (it),
Made no beat.
Then he veil’d (his) friend like a bride

The Epic of Gilgamesh — The Mourning of Gilgamish (Tablet VIII)

Anonymous (Standard Babylonian) · c. 1200 BCE

Translated by R. Campbell Thompson, 1928

About 15,000 fragments of Assyrian cuneiform tablets were found in the Library of Ashurbanipal in Nineveh, and in 1872 George Smith read translated fragments before the Society of Biblical Archaeology. In the eighth tablet Gilgamesh calls on mountains, forests, fields, rivers, wild animals and all of Uruk to mourn his friend. He tears at his hair and clothes and commissions a funerary statue.
