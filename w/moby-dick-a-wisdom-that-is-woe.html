There is a wisdom that is woe; but there is a woe that is madness. And there is a Catskill eagle in some souls that can alike dive down into the blackest gorges, and soar out of them again and become invisible in the sunny spaces.

Moby-Dick — A wisdom that is woe (Ch. 96, The Try-Works)

Herman Melville · 1851

In September 1850, partway through the book, Melville borrowed three thousand dollars from his father-in-law to buy a 160-acre farm in Pittsfield, Massachusetts. That summer he had been reading Thomas Carlyle in copies borrowed from the library of his friend Evert Duyckinck. The original manuscript of the novel has not survived. His second son, Stanwix, was born on 22 October 1851, between the London and New York editions.
