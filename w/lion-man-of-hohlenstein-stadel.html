Lion-Man of Hohlenstein-Stadel (Löwenmensch)

Maker unknown · c. 35,000–41,000 years ago · Mammoth ivory · 31.1 × 5.6 × 5.9 cm · Museum Ulm

Free to use with credit. Photo: Thilo Parg, CC BY-SA 4.0

The geologist Otto Völzing found the broken ivory on 25 August 1939 in the Hohlenstein-Stadel cave. War began a week later, the trenches were filled back in, and the fragments lay forgotten in the museum at Ulm for about thirty years, until Joachim Hahn began fitting more than 200 of them together. Later digs found more pieces, and after a restoration finished in 2013 the figure had grown from 296 to 311 millimetres. It was carved from mammoth ivory with a flint knife between 35,000 and 41,000 years ago.
