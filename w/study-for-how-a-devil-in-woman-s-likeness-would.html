Study for How a Devil in Woman's Likeness Would Have Tempted Sir Bors

Aubrey Beardsley · 1893 · Pen and black ink, with brush and black wash, over graphite, on tan wove paper · 26.5 × 38.9 cm · The Art Institute of Chicago

Free to use

Beardsley's first commission was Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur for the publisher J. M. Dent, and this is a study for one of its pictures, drawn in 1893 when he was about twenty-one. Malory finished the book around 1470, apparently writing in prison, and William Caxton first printed it in 1485. The study came to the Art Institute of Chicago as a gift of James Deering.
