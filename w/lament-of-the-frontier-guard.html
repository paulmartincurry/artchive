The Artchive
A gracious spring, turned to blood-ravenous autumn,
A turmoil of wars-men, spread over the middle kingdom,
Three hundred and sixty thousand,
And sorrow, sorrow like rain.
Sorrow to go, and sorrow, sorrow returning,

Lament of the Frontier Guard

Li Bai · 8th century; English 1915

Translated by Ezra Pound

Li Bai lived from about 701 to 762 and is credited with around 1,000 surviving poems. His early work was written in years of peace under an emperor who took part in the arts, and his poems changed tone after the An Lushan rebellion left most of northern China wrecked by war and famine. Unlike his younger friend Du Fu, he did not live to see the end of that turmoil.
