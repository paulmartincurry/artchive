But we are fated
To find no foothold, no rest,

Hyperion's Song of Fate

Friedrich Hölderlin · 1799

Translated by Michael Hamburger · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

In 1799 Hölderlin was twenty-nine and living in Homburg on a small allowance from his mother. He had been dismissed the year before from a tutor's post in Frankfurt after falling in love with Susette Gontard, the wife of the banker who employed him, and the two still met in secret once a month. In 1806 he was taken to a clinic in Tübingen and discharged as incurable. A carpenter who had read Hyperion gave him a room in a tower on the old city wall, where he stayed for 36 years.
