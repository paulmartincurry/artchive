Seize the day, place in the hours that come as little faith as you can.

Odes I.11 (Carpe Diem)

Horace · 23 BCE

Translated by A. S. Kline · Free to reproduce for non-commercial use (poetryintranslation.com terms); not public domain.

Horace was the son of a man who had been a slave and who spent a small fortune on his schooling. At Philippi in 42 BC he was a senior officer in the republican army that lost, and by his own account he ran from the field without his shield. He came home under an amnesty to find the family estate confiscated, and took a clerk's post at the Treasury. His patron Maecenas later gave him a country villa, whose remains stand on a hillside at Licenza. He was about forty-two when the ode appeared.
