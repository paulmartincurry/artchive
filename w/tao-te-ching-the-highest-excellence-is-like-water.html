The highest excellence is like (that of) water. The excellence of water appears in its benefiting all things, and in its occupying, without striving (to the contrary), the low place which all men dislike. Hence (its way) is near to (that of) the Tao.

Tao Te Ching — The highest excellence is like water (Ch. 8)

Laozi (Lao Tzu) · c. 4th century BCE

Translated by James Legge

The first account of Laozi's life was set down by the Han historian Sima Qian, who was born about 145 BCE and who tells three different stories. In the first, Laozi is an official in the imperial archives and a contemporary of Confucius. Yinxi, the keeper of the Han-ku Pass, asks him for a book, and he writes one in two parts before leaving for the West. In the third story he is a historian and astrologer who lived more than a century after Confucius.
