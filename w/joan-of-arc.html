Joan of Arc

Jules Bastien-Lepage · 1879 · Oil on canvas · 100 × 110 in. (254 × 279.4 cm) · The Met

Free to use

To prepare, Bastien-Lepage went to Domrémy, Joan's village, which lies near his own birthplace of Damvillers. His cousin Marie-Adèle Robert posed for Joan. The canvas is more than two and a half metres tall and wider still. It was shown at the Salon of 1880, and the response reportedly sent him from Paris to London, and he abandoned a painting of Ophelia in the same manner. The American businessman Erwin Davis bought it and gave it to the museum in 1889. The painter died of cancer five years after finishing it, aged thirty-six.
