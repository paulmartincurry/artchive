The heart has its reasons, which reason does not know. We feel it in a thousand things.

Pensées — The heart has its reasons (277)

Blaise Pascal · 1670

Translated by W. F. Trotter

Pensées is the name given after Pascal's death to the fragments he had been preparing for a defence of Christianity, a title he never used himself. The first English translation was made by John Walker in 1688. In 1776 Condorcet brought out a selectively edited edition, and a Geneva edition two years later added critical notes by Voltaire.
