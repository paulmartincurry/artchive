The Artchive
A noiseless patient spider,
I mark’d where on a little promontory it stood isolated,
Mark’d how to explore the vacant vast surrounding,
It launch’d forth filament, filament, filament out of itself,
Ever unreeling them, ever tirelessly speeding them.

A Noiseless Patient Spider

Walt Whitman · 1868

These ten lines were first printed in London, in the October 1868 issue of a magazine called The Broadway, as the third stanza of a longer poem. Whitman was forty-nine. He gave the stanza its own title in 1871. When it went into Leaves of Grass in 1891 he dropped eleven commas and turned every dash and semicolon into a comma.
