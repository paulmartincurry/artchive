Great Sphinx of Giza

Maker unknown · c. 2600–2500 BCE · Limestone bedrock · 73 m long, 20 m high

Free to use with credit. Photo: Petar Milošević, CC BY-SA 4.0

The Sphinx was carved out of the bedrock of the Giza plateau between about 2600 and 2500 BC, probably for Khufu or his son Khafre, and is 73 metres long. The plateau also served as the quarry for the pyramids. Drifting sand buried it up to the shoulders; around 1400 BC the young Thutmose IV had the front paws dug out and set up a granite slab between them. How the nose was destroyed is unknown, but the face shows marks of rods or chisels, and it was not Napoleon's troops.
