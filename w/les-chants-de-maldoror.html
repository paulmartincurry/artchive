He is fair as the retractility of the claws of birds of prey … and above all, as the chance meeting on a dissecting-table of a sewing-machine and an umbrella!

Les Chants de Maldoror (Canto VI)

Comte de Lautréamont (Isidore Ducasse) · 1869

Translated by Alexis Lykiard · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

The sixth canto closes a book that was printed in Belgium in August 1869, paid for with the money of the author's father, a French consular officer in Montevideo. Isidore Ducasse was twenty-three. With the copies already printed, the publisher Albert Lacroix refused to send them to booksellers for fear of prosecution, and the complete work never went on sale in Ducasse's lifetime. He died in Paris on 24 November 1870, aged twenty-four. In 1874 the stock was bought and sold under a new cover.
