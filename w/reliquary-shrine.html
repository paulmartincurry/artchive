Reliquary Shrine

Jean de Touyl · ca. 1325–50 · Gilded silver, translucent enamel, paint · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

Closed, the shrine is about 25 centimetres tall and 17 wide; with its wings folded out it is about 41 wide. It is first recorded in 17th- and 18th-century inventories of the Poor Clares convent at Buda, in what is now Budapest. Queen Clementia of Hungary had given it in the 14th century to her sister-in-law Elizabeth of Poland, who founded the convent. The attribution to the French goldsmith Jean de Touyl is tentative, and little is known about him. The museum acquired it in 1962 from the collection of Baroness Clarice de Rothschild.
