Statement to the Court

Eugene V. Debs · September 18, 1918

Debs was sixty-two and had been convicted under the Sedition Act of 1918 for a speech against American participation in the First World War. He had left school at fourteen to clean grease from freight engines for fifty cents a day, and later worked as a locomotive fireman. In 1920 he ran for president from his prison cell and took 3.4 percent of the vote. His sentence was commuted in December 1921, but his citizenship was not restored in his lifetime.
