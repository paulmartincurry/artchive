Nocturne: Blue and Gold – Old Battersea Bridge

James McNeill Whistler · ca. 1872–1875 · Oil on canvas · Tate

Free to use

For his Nocturnes Whistler mixed his own medium of copal, turpentine and linseed oil, so runny that he often had to lay the canvas flat to keep it from running off. He worked from memory or from studios overlooking the Thames, and he made the bridge taller than it was. A few of the fireworks are in a yellow found nowhere else on the canvas, which suggests he came back to it later. The painting was discussed in his 1878 libel suit against the critic John Ruskin, which ended with damages of one farthing.
