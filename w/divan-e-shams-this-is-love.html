This is Love: to fly heavenward,
To rend, every instant, a hundred veils.

Divan-e Shams — This Is Love

Jalāl al-Dīn Muḥammad Rūmī · 13th century

Translated by R. A. Nicholson, 1898

Rumi was born in 1207 in Wakhsh, in what is now Tajikistan, and his family went west when the Mongols invaded Central Asia. They settled at Konya in Anatolia, a region that had been a province of the Eastern Roman Empire, and the name Rumi means the Roman. At twenty-five he inherited his father's post at the head of a religious school there. When he died in Konya in 1273, the city's Christians and Jews joined the crowd at his funeral.
