Boy Bitten by a Lizard

Caravaggio · c. 1594–96 · Oil on canvas · 66 × 49.5 cm · The National Gallery, London

Free to use

Caravaggio painted two versions of this picture, both thought to be his: this one in London and another in Florence. He worked from life, so the model had to hold a pose of sudden fright for a long time; one theory names him as Mario Minniti, Caravaggio's companion. The idea of a bitten finger probably came from Sofonisba Anguissola's drawing of a boy bitten by a crab, made around 1554.
