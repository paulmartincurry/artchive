I have discovered that all the unhappiness of men arises from one single fact, that they cannot stay quietly in their own chamber.

Pensées — Diversion: quietly in their own chamber (139)

Blaise Pascal · 1670

Translated by W. F. Trotter

On the night of 23 November 1654, between half past ten and half past twelve, Pascal had an intense religious experience and at once wrote a short note to himself. He seems to have sewn it into his coat and moved it whenever he changed clothes; a servant found it only by chance after his death. A year earlier, at twenty-nine, he had signed his sister's whole inheritance over to the convent of Port-Royal, which left him in genteel poverty.
