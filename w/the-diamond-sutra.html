Thus shall ye think of all this fleeting world:
A star at dawn, a bubble in a stream;
A flash of lightning in a summer cloud,
A flickering lamp, a phantom, and a dream.

The Diamond Sutra (Ch. 32)

Kumārajīva / Sanskrit Mahāyāna Canon · ca. 400 CE (printed 868 CE)

Translated by A. F. Price · Short quotation from a translation still in copyright, credited to the translator. Not public domain; kept to a line or two on purpose.

Kumārajīva was born in 344 in the oasis kingdom of Kucha, the son of an Indian monk and a Kuchean princess. A warlord captured him at about forty and held him for many years, during which he is thought to have learned Chinese. He reached Chang'an in 401 and led a court-sponsored team that turned about thirty-five sutras, this one among them, from Sanskrit into Chinese. He read each text aloud with a running commentary in Chinese, the monks discussed it, and he checked the draft that came out.
