Could you believe me without? I had no portrait, now, but am small, like the wren; and my hair is bold like the chestnut bur; and my eyes, like the sherry in the glass, that the guest leaves. Would this do just as well?

Letter to T. W. Higginson, July 1862 — Small, like the Wren

Emily Dickinson · 1862

Higginson's Letter to a Young Contributor was the lead piece in The Atlantic Monthly in April 1862, and Dickinson wrote to him. From 1862 to 1864 he served as colonel of the 1st South Carolina Volunteers, the first federally authorized black regiment. After her death he edited the first selection of her poems with Mabel Loomis Todd.
