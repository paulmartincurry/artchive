But the iniquity of oblivion blindly scattereth her poppy, and deals with the memory of men without distinction to merit of perpetuity. Who can but pity the founder of the pyramids? Herostratus lives that burnt the temple of Diana, he is almost lost that built it.

Hydriotaphia, Urn Burial — The iniquity of oblivion (Ch. V)

Sir Thomas Browne · 1658

Browne wrote this after some 40 to 50 Anglo-Saxon burial urns were dug up in Norfolk. It was published in 1658 as the first half of a two-part work that ends with The Garden of Cyrus, and the pair were the last works he published.
