The Last Communion of Saint Jerome

Sandro Botticelli · early 1490s · Tempera and gold on wood · 13 1/2 × 10 in. (34.3 × 25.4 cm) · The Met

Free to use

The panel was commissioned by Francesco del Pugliese, a known follower of the preacher Girolamo Savonarola, who was preaching in Florence at the time. The scene comes from a medieval letter falsely credited to Eusebius, on the death of Jerome; it had been reprinted several times in Italy between 1471 and 1487. The picture is only thirty-four centimetres tall. It came to the Metropolitan Museum in 1913 with the bequest of Benjamin Altman.
