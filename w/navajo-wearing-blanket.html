Navajo Wearing Blanket

Maker unknown · 1860–70 · Wool · 171.5 × 124.46 cm · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Free to use

Written records show the Navajo as fine weavers for at least 300 years, and by 1812 Pedro Piño called them the best weavers in the province. The Navajo used cotton before the Spanish arrived and wool afterwards. This wool wearing blanket, woven in the 1860s, is 171.5 centimetres long and was given to the Met in 1910 by Mrs. Russell Sage.
