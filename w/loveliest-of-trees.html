Now, of my threescore years and ten,
Twenty will not come again,
And take from seventy springs a score,
It only leaves me fifty more.

Loveliest of Trees

A. E. Housman · 1896

The first draft, from April or May 1895, had only two stanzas, and its manuscript survives. The middle stanza was composed later and went through much rewriting. The poem had no title when it was printed as the second in A Shropshire Lad. The garden of Housman's childhood home had a cherry tree that was known locally, and for several years in the 1890s he recorded the flowering of cherry trees in his diary.
