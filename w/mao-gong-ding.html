Mao Gong Ding

Maker unknown · ca. 827–782 BCE · Bronze · National Palace Museum, Taipei

Free to use with credit. Photo: National Palace Museum, Taipei, CC BY 4.0

The cauldron was dug up in Qishan County, Shaanxi, in 1843. Inside it runs an inscription of 500 characters in 32 lines, and the collector Chen Jieqi, who acquired it in 1852, had rubbings made of them. It later sat in a Tianjin bank as security for a loan, was sold to a Shanghai millionaire during the war with Japan, and was given to the government in April 1946. It stands 53.8 centimetres high and weighs 34.7 kilograms. The Lord of Mao presented it to King Xuan of Zhou after the king appointed him to help run the state.
