The sandy road by Oxus’ banks, that rugged way,
Silk-soft beneath my feet to me appears to-day:

Bū-yi Jū-yi Mūliyān

Rudaki · ca. 930 CE

Translated by A. V. Williams Jackson, 1920

In this poem Rudaki calls on the Samanid emir to come back to Bukhara from Herat. He was a court poet, singer and musician, and early writers imply that he was blind. After his patron, the vizier Bal'ami, fell in 937, Rudaki was dismissed from court; he died poor in his home village in 940 or 941. He is reported to have composed more than 180,000 verses, most of them lost. In the twentieth century the poem was set to music and sung as a duet on Iranian radio.
