Raimondi Stele

Maker unknown · ca. 500–300 BCE · Stone · National Museum of Archaeology, Anthropology, and History of Peru

Free to use with credit. Photo: Ariadnayteseo, CC BY-SA 3.0

This slab of polished granite is 1.98 metres high, 74 centimetres wide and 15 centimetres thick, and its design is lightly incised. It was not found where it was first set up. When the Italian-Peruvian naturalist Antonio Raimondi visited Chavín de Huantar in 1873, it was in the house of a local farmer, Timoteo Espinoza. The carving reads as a different image when the stone is turned upside down, and the figure and its staffs contain 50 snakes.
