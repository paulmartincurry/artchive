Behold! human beings living in a underground den, which has a mouth open towards the light and reaching all along the den; here they have been from their childhood, and have their legs and necks chained so that they cannot move, and can only see before them, being prevented by the chains from turning round their heads.

The Republic — The Allegory of the Cave (Book VII)

Plato · c. 375 BCE

Translated by Benjamin Jowett

The cave passage is told by Socrates to Glaucon, who was Plato's own brother; another brother, Adeimantus, also speaks in the Republic. Plato never appears in his dialogues in his own voice. An ancient tradition holds that he wrote poems and tragedies as a young man and burnt them when he met Socrates. He went on to found the Academy, a school of philosophy in Athens, and his complete works are believed to have survived.
