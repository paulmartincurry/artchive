# Deep cuts by famous names: the plan

Written 25 Nov 2026, after Paul asked whether the site should pride itself on deep cuts by well-known artists, especially ones that lean into the site's feel. Nothing here is built yet. It is a map and an order of work.

## The idea

A famous name gets a stranger in the door. The work they did not expect is why they remember the site. So for every household name, the site should hold the hits and then the dark, strange or intense work that never made the posters.

This is already in the brief ("famous first, deep cuts inside") and it matches Paul's taste order. It is a direction, not a new feature.

## What the site already does

More than expected. A check of 60 well-known deep cuts found most of them here already:

- Goya's Caprichos (12), Disasters of War (13) and a Disparate, beside the Majas.
- Van Gogh's Head of a Skeleton with a Burning Cigarette and Prisoners Exercising.
- Monet's Camille on Her Deathbed. Cézanne's Pyramid of Skulls. Manet's Dead Toreador.
- Hokusai's ghost prints. Blake's Ghost of a Flea. Sargent's Gassed. Homer's Gulf Stream.
- Michelangelo's sonnets and Leonardo's notebooks on the writing side. Keats's letters (11). Wilde's De Profundis.

So the job is not to start. It is to go deeper where the free supply is large, fill the few clear holes, and make the deep cuts easier to stumble on.

## What counts as a deep cut

1. **The maker is a name a stranger would search.** Otherwise it is just a find, which is a different shelf.
2. **The work is not what they are known for.** A different subject, mood or medium from the poster version of that artist.
3. **It hits on its own.** A weak sketch is not interesting because Monet signed it. Same bar as everything else.
4. **It leans the site's way.** Drama and intensity first, then quiet beauty. This is Paul's taste order, applied inside one artist.
5. **It is free to use**, by the same routes as the rest of the site.

## Three shelves

**A. The dark side of the famous painters.** The table above. Mostly prints and drawings from Chicago and Cleveland. Largest supply, easiest to source, closest to the site's feel.

**B. Famous people in the wrong medium.** The novelist who drew, the painter who wrote.
- Victor Hugo's ink drawings (the Paris museums hold them and release them free; that source needs a key the site does not have yet).
- Strindberg's storm paintings. Blake as printer of his own books.
- On the writing side: Michelangelo's poems, Van Gogh's and Gauguin's letters, Leonardo's notebooks, Delacroix's journal. Several are started; none is deep.

**C. Famous writers' back pages.** Letters, notebooks, diaries and marginal notes by writers already here for their big lines. The site has 68 letters, 27 diary pieces and 17 notebook pieces. Clear holes found in the check: Poe's Marginalia, Whitman's notebooks, Melville's letters to Hawthorne, more Dickinson letters (4 here).

## Two things that pull against this

- **The paper problem.** Paul's own watchout: walls of beige sheets read as a dull archive. Most deep cuts by old masters are prints on cream paper. So: pick dense, dark plates that fill the frame; crop to the plate, not the sheet; cap each maker per round; keep them from bunching on the wall.
- **Europe again.** The trim pass said to lean away from European old masters, and this table is mostly that. So shelf A should run Hokusai, Kuniyoshi and Hiroshige alongside Goya and Rembrandt, and the round after it should not be European.

## How visitors would find them

No new interface to start. Three small things, in order of cost:

1. **Search for a famous name leads with the hits, then the deep cuts.** Today it is ranked by fame alone, so the deep cuts sink. A light reorder after the first few results would surface them.
2. **Put some deep cuts in the front-of-house pool.** The opening wall is hand-picked. A Rembrandt beggar next to a famous Monet does the job without a word.
3. **Later, if wanted:** a quiet "lesser known" line on the label. Not before Paul has seen 1 and 2.

## Order of work

Each round is a fixed list with a finish line, picked by eye from contact sheets, same rules and checks as every round before it.

1. **Round A1, the printmakers (about 120 works).** Goya, Rembrandt, Dürer, Redon, Daumier, Piranesi, Géricault from Chicago and Cleveland, with Hokusai and Kuniyoshi in the same round. About 12 to 15 per maker, dark and dense plates first.
2. **Round C, the back pages (about 60 pieces).** Poe, Whitman, Melville, Dickinson, plus more letters from painters. Text from fetched editions only, as always.
3. **Round B, the wrong medium (about 40 works).** Needs the Paris museums key for Hugo, and a hunt for free Strindberg, Bosch, Bruegel, Klimt and Schiele.
4. **The search reorder and the front-of-house picks**, once A1 is in and there is something to surface.
5. **Round A2**, the second wave from the table (Whistler, Delacroix, Turner, Millet, Homer, Lautrec), only if A1 lands well.

## What is not known yet

- None of the museum counts has been looked at. Many of those prints will fail the bar or the paper test. Expect one in ten to make it.
- The Met's numbers. Its records answer from here but its pictures need Paul's browser.
- Whether a search reorder feels right. It should be tried and looked at before it ships.
- Small clean-up found on the way: Turner is on the site under three spellings ("J. M. W. Turner", "J.M.W. Turner", "Joseph Mallord William Turner") and should be one.
